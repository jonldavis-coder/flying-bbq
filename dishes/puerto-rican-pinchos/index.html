Puerto Rican Pinchos

Next stop: Dominican Wasakaka Chicken · continuing the voyage

Pronounced PEEN-chos

Grilled pork cubes with garlic, oregano, and a light sweet-sharp glaze.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Pinchos are pork skewers sold from small grills along Puerto Rican roads and beaches. They use the island's adobo tastes, garlic and oregano, and often a light glaze. They are a street portion, not the whole pig of a lechonera. The cubes are whole muscle, so they are cooked as pork pieces rather than as ground meat.

Cultural background

This is a skewer for a hot grill. The glaze is brown sugar, garlic, and vinegar, mixed at home. It is not a bottled barbecue sauce, and it is not the long roast of lechón.

Why it tastes this way

Small cubes brown on all sides, so you get more crust than a roast shoulder. Garlic and oregano match the rest of the island's pork, and a little sugar in the glaze darkens on the coal without turning the meat into candy. Sea air and a quick grill are the street setting, and the seasoning still works in a backyard.

How it is made

Skewer garlic-seasoned pork cubes and grill them, brushing on a small glaze near the end.

Authentic touch

Cut the cubes the same size so they finish together. A thick end next to a thin one will mislead a single thermometer reading.

How it is used

Eat the skewers on a piece of bread if you want the street sandwich, or without bread. Bread is a pairing and adds wheat.

Field notes

  • Pinchos are street skewers, sold from grills along roads and beaches.
  • They are pork cubes, not the whole animal of a lechonera.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds pork shoulder, in 1 1/2 inch cubes
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • Metal or soaked bamboo skewers

Steps

  1. Toss the pork with garlic, oregano, salt, and oil. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. Thread it onto skewers. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Stir brown sugar, vinegar, and tomato paste into a small glaze. Keep it cold until the grill is ready. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill the skewers, turning, until the center of the thickest cube reads 145°F (63°C). Brush on the glaze in the last few minutes. Rest the skewers 3 minutes.
  4. The cubes should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Eat them hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of guava paste in the glaze is a common street version. This recipe uses brown sugar instead.
  • Chicken pinchos would need 165°F (74°C). These are pork.

Pairings

  • pan de agua
  • a pickle
  • grilled pineapple
