Tandoori Fish

Next stop: Mughal-Style Raan · continuing the voyage

Pronounced tan-DOOR-ee fish

Firm fish with a tangy yogurt crust, chili color, and a short, hot cook.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Tandoori fish is firm fish in a yogurt and chili marinade, cooked in a tandoor or a very hot oven. It is part of the same North Indian tandoor menu as the chicken, using fish instead of poultry. It is not fried Amritsari fish, which is a batter fry from Amritsar and a different technique. Yogurt keeps dairy in the dish.

Cultural background

The cook is a tandoor or a labeled home oven, not a frying pan. There is fish and dairy. It is not the chickpea-batter fish of Amritsar, and it is not a vegan plate.

Why it tastes this way

Fish cooks quickly, so the yogurt marinade browns before the flesh dries. Chili and ajwain, a sharp seed used with fish in North Indian cooking, sit on that crust. The tandoor taste is dry heat and char, which a fryer full of oil does not give.

How it is made

Marinate thick fish steaks in yogurt, chili, and ajwain, then cook them in a tandoor or a very hot home oven until the center flakes.

Authentic touch

Choose a steak that is at least an inch thick. Thin fillets fall apart and overcook before the marinade colors.

How it is used

Eat it with onion, lemon, and a plain salad. Do not batter or fry it.

Field notes

  • This is tandoor-cooked fish. It is not fried Amritsari fish.
  • Yogurt in the marinade means the plate contains dairy.

Ingredients

  • 4 firm fish steaks, about 6 ounces each
  • 3/4 cup plain yogurt
  • 1 tablespoon Kashmiri chili powder
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ajwain seeds
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Mix the yogurt, chili, ginger, garlic, ajwain, salt, lemon, and oil. Coat the fish and refrigerate it for 20 minutes. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The fish is still raw. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  2. Heat a tandoor, or heat a home oven to 450°F (230°C) and name it as an oven. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  3. Cook the steaks until the center reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque. Do not fry them. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve hot with lemon. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Kingfish, salmon, or another firm steak can be used if the local fish is the same thickness.
  • A home oven at high heat should be called an oven, not a tandoor.

Pairings

  • sliced onion
  • lemon
  • cucumber salad
