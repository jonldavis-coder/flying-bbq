Senegalese Dibi

Next stop: Nigerian Suya · on Africa's fires by plane, by Plane

Pronounced DEE-bee

Grilled lamb with onion and a sharp mustard relish.

20 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Dibi is grilled lamb at Wolof street grills in Dakar and in other Senegalese cities. The meat is often lamb chops or a larger piece carved after the fire, served with raw onion and mustard. It is street food you eat at the grill, not a restaurant tasting menu. This recipe uses chops and a simple onion-mustard relish. The lamb is a whole-muscle cut.

Cultural background

Wolof dibiterie cooks tend these fires. The dish is lamb and charcoal. It is not yassa, the onion stew, though onion is on the plate.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb fat over charcoal is the base, and raw onion and mustard cut it the way a heavy sauce would not. The street grill is close to the coals, so the outside browns before the inside overcooks. Mustard is sharp and familiar at these stands. The combination is simple because the fire is doing the work.

How it is made

Season lamb chops, grill them, rest them, and serve with an onion and mustard relish.

Authentic touch

Serve the onion raw and the mustard cold against the hot meat. Do not cook the relish into a jam.

How it is used

Chopped or on the bone, with the relish, at the grill or at home.

Field notes

  • Dibi is grilled lamb associated with Wolof street grills in Senegal.
  • Onion and mustard are a common accompaniment at the stand.

Ingredients

  • 8 lamb chops
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons prepared mustard
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Salt and oil the chops. Stir the onion with mustard and pepper for the relish.
  2. Grill the chops over hot coals, turning once.
  3. Cook whole lamb to at least 145°F (63°C) and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with the onion-mustard relish. Mustard is an allergen in the relish. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A larger leg, roasted and then sliced, still checked with a thermometer.
  • Less mustard in the relish if you want the onion to lead.

Pairings

  • Raw onion
  • Mustard relish
  • Fries
