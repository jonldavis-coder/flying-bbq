Ethiopian Grilled Tibs

Next stop: Mozambican Peri-Peri Chicken · continuing the voyage

Pronounced tibs

Beef with berbere, onion, and a hot grilled edge.

20 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Tibs is Ethiopian beef cooked with berbere and onion. It is often a hot pan and sometimes a grill. This recipe is the grilled version. It is not kitfo, and it is not served raw. Berbere blends differ by house and shop; the rub here is a simple chili, paprika, ginger, and garlic mix, not a claim that one tin is the only berbere. The beef is a whole-muscle cut cooked to temperature.

Cultural background

Ethiopian restaurants and home cooks in Addis Ababa make tibs as a cooked meat dish. Kitfo is a different food and is raw or lightly warmed. This page does not serve raw beef.

Why it tastes this way

Berbere's chili and warm spices bloom on beef fat as the surface browns. A grill adds char that a covered pan softens. Onion, grilled beside the meat or served raw, sweetens the heat. The difference from kitfo is total: tibs is cooked. A shop berbere may be more complex than this simple mix, and that is fine to use if you know what is in the tin.

How it is made

Rub beef with a simple berbere-style spice and grill it until it reaches a safe temperature, then rest it.

Authentic touch

Cut the beef in strips so the spice coats it, and do not serve it raw. If your berbere tin lists other allergens, follow the tin.

How it is used

With cooked vegetables. Injera may be eaten beside it and is not in this recipe. The beef is fully cooked.

Field notes

  • Tibs is often cooked in a hot pan or on a grill, and this recipe is the grilled version.
  • It is not kitfo, and it is not served raw.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb beef sirloin, in strips
  • 1 tablespoon paprika
  • 2 teaspoons chili flakes
  • 1 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 onion, sliced

Steps

  1. Mix paprika, chili, ginger, garlic, salt, and oil. Toss with the beef. This is a simple berbere-style rub, not a single official blend.
  2. Grill the beef and the onion over a hot fire. This is the grilled version of tibs, not a raw dish.
  3. Cook whole beef to at least 145°F (63°C) and give it a 3-minute rest. Do not serve it raw.
  4. Serve hot with the onion. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of a shop berbere you have read the label on, in place of the simple spice.
  • A pan tibs at home, still cooked through, if you have no grill.

Pairings

  • Cooked greens
  • Onion
  • Tomato
