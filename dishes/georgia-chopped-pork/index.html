Georgia Chopped Pork

Next stop: South Carolina Barbecue Hash · continuing the voyage

Chopped smoked pork with a thin, peppery tomato-vinegar sauce.

30 min prep · 10 hr cook · Serves 10 · about 10 servings

History

Georgia pits serve chopped pork, and the state does not have one official sauce. Some tables lean vinegar, some add tomato, and neither is a state law. This version is one thin tomato-and-vinegar dressing for smoked shoulder, offered as a Georgia plate rather than as the Georgia plate. It is not a claim of a single founding pit.

Cultural background

Chopped pork with a thin sauce. Georgia's sauces vary, so this is a family version, not an official standard.

Why it tastes this way

A thin sauce soaks into chopped pork instead of sitting on it like a glaze. Tomato softens vinegar, and black pepper does the work that a sweet sauce would do somewhere else. Hickory is a common Georgia pit wood, mild enough that the sauce still shows.

How it is made

Smoke a pork shoulder, chop it, and dress it with a thin, lightly tomatoed sauce.

Authentic touch

Keep the sauce thin enough to pour. If it mounds on a spoon, it has left this style.

How it is used

Chopped, on a plate or a bun, with sauce on the side so people can add more.

Field notes

  • Georgia barbecue is real, and the state does not have one official sauce.
  • This chopped pork uses a thin sauce, not a thick molasses glaze.

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in pork shoulder, about 8 pounds
  • 3 tablespoons kosher salt
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 3 tablespoons tomato sauce
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper, for the sauce
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt, for the sauce

Steps

  1. Salt and pepper the shoulder. Smoke it indirectly over hickory at about 250°F (121°C).
  2. Stir vinegar, water, tomato sauce, pepper, and salt. The sauce should pour like seasoned vinegar, not like ketchup.
  3. Cook the shoulder to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) and pull or chop it. Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest; chopped shoulder is taken higher for tenderness.
  4. Moisten the pork with sauce and serve the remainder on the side.

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A vinegar-only dressing is another Georgia habit. This written sauce includes a little tomato.

Pairings

  • brunswick stew
  • white bread
  • pickles
