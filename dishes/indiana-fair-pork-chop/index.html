Indiana Fair Pork Chop

Next stop: Iowa Mustard-Glazed Pork Chop · continuing the voyage

A thick bone-in pork chop with a salty, peppery crust and a juicy center.

5 min prep · 16 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Thick bone-in pork chops, salted and peppered, are a standard plate at Indiana fairs and backyard grills. This one has no glaze. It is not the Iowa chop with mustard and apple, and it does not borrow a festival story beyond the fairground habit of cooking a plain chop over heat.

Cultural background

Salt and pepper on a thick chop. An Indiana fair and backyard plate, deliberately plain.

Why it tastes this way

A thick chop can take a hard sear and still stay moist at the bone, which is why fair cooks cut them heavy. Salt and pepper are the whole seasoning so the pork itself is the taste. Direct heat, not smoke and sauce, defines it against a pit shoulder.

How it is made

Grill thick bone-in pork chops with only salt and pepper.

Authentic touch

Buy chops at least an inch thick. A thin chop will be past temperature before the crust exists.

How it is used

Whole, on a plate. No glaze. Corn or a salad can sit beside them.

Field notes

  • The Indiana plate here is a thick bone-in chop with salt and pepper, without a sweet glaze.
  • It is written to stay distinct from the Iowa chop, which carries mustard and apple.

Ingredients

  • 4 bone-in pork chops, about 1 to 1 1/4 inches thick
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt
  • 2 teaspoons black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Oil the chops lightly and season both sides with salt and pepper. Do not add a glaze.
  2. Heat a grill for direct cooking, with a cooler zone.
  3. Grill, turning once, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes before serving.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A cooler side of the grill finishes a very thick chop if the outside browns too fast.

Pairings

  • corn
  • sliced tomato
  • potato
