Baltimore Pit Beef

Next stop: Cornell Chicken · continuing the voyage

Charred top round, sliced thin, with a sharp horseradish bite.

15 min prep · 50 min cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Baltimore pit beef is top round grilled over very hot charcoal and sliced thin, often with horseradish. It is a city sandwich meat, closer to a steak cook than to a smoked brisket. This version does not name a single shop as the inventor. Many slices are served pink in the center; the temperature used here is 145°F with a rest.

Cultural background

Hot-charcoal top round for thin slices. It is not Texas brisket and not a roasted deli round cooked until gray.

Why it tastes this way

A hard charcoal crust and a short cook leave the interior juicy, and slicing thin across the grain makes a lean roast feel tender. Horseradish is sharp and cold against the warm beef. The fire is direct, which is why this is not called a smoke.

How it is made

Sear a salted top round over hot charcoal and slice it thin.

Authentic touch

Slice thin, against the grain, as soon as the rest is done. Thick slices of top round eat dry.

How it is used

On a roll or a plate, with plain prepared horseradish.

Field notes

  • Pit beef is top round over hot charcoal, sliced thin, which is a different cook from smoked brisket.
  • Slicing across the grain is how this lean roast stays pleasant to chew.

Ingredients

  • 1 beef top round roast, about 3 pounds
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt
  • 2 teaspoons coarse black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • Prepared horseradish made from root, vinegar, and salt, for serving
  • Charcoal

Steps

  1. Salt, pepper, and garlic-powder the roast. Heat charcoal until it is very hot, and keep a cooler edge.
  2. Sear the roast on all sides over the hot coals, then finish over the cooler side.
  3. Cook until the center reaches 145°F (63°C). Baltimore plates are often pink; this is the safety line used here, not a well-done roast.
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes and slice thin against the grain. Serve with prepared horseradish.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A cooler zone on the grill helps if the crust is browning before the center is ready.

Pairings

  • horseradish
  • sliced onion
  • rolls
