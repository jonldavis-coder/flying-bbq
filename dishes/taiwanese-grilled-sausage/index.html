Taiwanese Grilled Sausage

Next stop: Mongolian Khorkhog · continuing the voyage

Pronounced Taiwanese sausage

A slightly sweet pork sausage, grilled, with garlic and five spice.

30 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Taiwanese grilled sausage is a night-market pork sausage, a little sweet, cooked over charcoal and often served on a stick. Taipei night markets made it a standard snack. This recipe mixes fresh pork and cooks it the same day. It uses no curing salt and it is not hung to dry; soy sauce in the mix contains soy and wheat; it is a Taiwanese sausage; it does not need a China label to be itself.

Cultural background

Night-market grills in Taiwan cook these links to order. The written mix is fresh ground pork, sugar, soy sauce, and five spice. There is no dairy. Do not cure it. Cook it through as ground meat.

Why it tastes this way

Sugar and a little soy caramelize on the casing, and five spice perfumes the fat. Grilling beats boiling because the skin browns and the sweetness concentrates. Sticky rice is sometimes tucked into a split sausage. The sausage itself is the sweet pork and the fire.

How it is made

Mix fresh pork with sugar, soy sauce, and five spice, shape links or logs, and grill them the same day until the center is done.

Authentic touch

Form small, even logs if you are not using casings. A huge log will brown before the center is safe.

How it is used

Eat them hot, with garlic and a little raw cabbage if you want a sharp bite beside the sweetness.

Field notes

  • The sweet grilled pork sausage is a Taiwanese night-market food.
  • This mix is cooked the day it is made. It is not a cured sausage.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds ground pork
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 tablespoon rice wine
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon five-spice powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Mix the pork, soy sauce, sugar, rice wine, garlic, five spice, salt, and oil. Do not add curing salt. Refrigerate the mix and cook it the same day. The pork is still raw. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  2. Shape eight small logs. Heat a grill to medium. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  3. Grill, turning, until the center of each log reaches 160°F (71°C). Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  4. Rest 3 minutes. Serve hot and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A purchased fresh Taiwanese sausage can be grilled if you still check the center temperature.
  • Gluten-free soy sauce would remove the wheat. This recipe uses ordinary soy sauce.

Pairings

  • raw garlic
  • cabbage
  • rice
