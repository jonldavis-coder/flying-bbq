South Carolina Barbecue Hash

A peppery pork-and-onion stew, usually spooned over rice.

30 min prep · 3 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

South Carolina barbecue hash is a pork and onion stew served with rice beside the barbecue, not another name for the chopped pork itself. It shows up in the Midlands and toward the Savannah River. Mustard or vinegar may season the pot depending on the cook. This version uses mustard and cooks the pork through.

Cultural background

A side stew for a barbecue plate. It is not the mustard-sauced shoulder itself, though it often sits next to one.

Why it tastes this way

Long cooking turns pork and onion into a thick spoonful, and mustard ties it to the Midlands sauce habit. Rice underneath catches the gravy. It tastes like the pit table rather than like a smoke ring, because it is a pot.

How it is made

Simmer pork with onions and mustard until it can be chopped into a thick stew, then serve it over rice.

Authentic touch

Cook the onions until they collapse. Hash is a stew, not a stir-fry of crisp onion.

How it is used

Over cooked rice, next to mustard pork or on its own as a plate.

Field notes

  • Barbecue hash is a stew served beside the meat, often over rice, not a name for the chopped pork.
  • It is associated with South Carolina pits, especially through the Midlands and the Savannah River country.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds pork shoulder, cut into chunks
  • 4 onions, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons yellow mustard
  • 1/4 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 4 cups water
  • Cooked rice, for serving

Steps

  1. Put pork, onions, mustard, vinegar, salt, pepper, and water in a heavy pot.
  2. Simmer, covered, until the pork reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and then continue until it is soft enough to chop, about 195°F (90°C) in the chunks. Whole pork is safe at 145°F with a 3-minute rest; hash pork is cooked longer.
  3. Chop the pork in the pot and simmer uncovered until the stew is thick. The onions should have collapsed.
  4. Serve over rice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some pots add potato. This one stays with pork and onion.

Pairings

  • rice
  • mustard pork
  • sliced onion
