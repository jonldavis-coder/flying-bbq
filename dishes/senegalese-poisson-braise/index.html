Senegalese Poisson Braise

Next stop: Ivorian Poulet Braise · continuing the voyage

Pronounced pwah-SON breh-ZAY

Grilled fish with chili, lime, garlic, and a charred skin.

20 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Poisson braisé is grilled fish in Senegal, cooked at street fires and at home along the coast, including in Saint-Louis. The catch changes, and the method does not: a whole fish, scored, rubbed with garlic, chili, and lime, and set over charcoal. It is a different plate from dibi, which is lamb. The fish is done when it flakes.

Cultural background

Coastal Senegalese grill cooks make this from the day's fish. It is not a fried fritter and not a fish yassa stewed in onion, though those are also Senegalese.

Why it tastes this way

A hot grate blisters the skin and keeps the flesh moist if the fish is not left over dying coals. Lime and chili cut the oil of a sea fish. Garlic cooks on the surface. Saint-Louis and the Atlantic coast supply the habit of cooking the whole animal rather than a boneless fillet.

How it is made

Rub a whole fish with garlic, chili, and lime, and grill it until it flakes.

Authentic touch

Leave the fish whole and turn it once with a wide spatula. Stuffing the cavity with too much wet marinade will steam it.

How it is used

With lime, onion, and rice or boiled millet. Eat it hot.

Field notes

  • Poisson braisé is grilled fish in Senegal, at street fires and at home.
  • The species follows the catch, and the method is a whole fish over charcoal.

Ingredients

  • 4 whole firm fish, about 1 lb each, gutted and scaled
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 fresh hot chile, chopped
  • 1 lime, juiced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Score the fish. Mix garlic, chile, lime juice, salt, and oil and rub the fish inside and out.
  2. Heat a charcoal grill and oil the grate.
  3. Grill, turning once, until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh flakes.
  4. Serve with lime. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sea bream or another firm fish, named as the fish you bought.
  • A milder chile if the one you have is very hot.

Pairings

  • Lime
  • Rice
  • Sliced onion
