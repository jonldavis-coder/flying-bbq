Patagonian Cordero al Asador

Lamb roasted beside a wood fire, salted, with a browned outside.

History

Cordero al asador is lamb opened on an iron cross beside a wood fire in Argentine Patagonia. A whole lamb is the estancia version. A leg is the home version on this page and is not a whole lamb. Mapuche and Tehuelche histories are part of Patagonia, and this cross is an estancia fire rather than a claim to be their only cook. The seasoning is salt.

Cultural background

The fire sits beside the cross, and the cook turns the lamb. Uruguay's asado al espeto in this file is a beef cross. This one is Patagonian lamb. Both can be true without becoming the same page.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb fat bastes the meat as it faces the wood, and the pace is slow because the fire is to the side. Wind and dry wood in Patagonia make a hot, clean coal. Salt is enough. The taste is lamb and light smoke, not a marinade.

How it is made

Salt a leg of lamb, the home stand-in, and roast it on a spit beside wood to a whole-lamb temperature.

Authentic touch

Keep the flames off the leg. The cross works because the heat is steady and near, not because the meat is on fire.

How it is used

Slice it and eat it with a simple salad and bread. Bread is a pairing and adds wheat.

Field notes

  • Cordero al asador is lamb on a cross beside a Patagonian wood fire.
  • A single leg is the home version and is not a whole lamb.

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in leg of lamb, about 5 pounds, a home cut and not a whole lamb
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • Wood and a spit, a cross, or a rotisserie you will name honestly

Steps

  1. Oil the leg and salt it. It is raw and is not ready to eat. This is a leg, not a whole lamb on a cross. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Set it on a spit beside a wood fire, or on a rotisserie, and say which tool you used. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Cook until the thickest part, away from the bone, reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Carve the rested lamb. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A home rotisserie counts if you say it is not an iron cross on an estancia.
  • Some cooks rub a little garlic. Salt alone is the version written here, plus oil so the salt sticks.
