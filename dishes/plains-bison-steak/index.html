Plains Grilled Bison Steak

Next stop: Dearborn Lamb Skewers · continuing the voyage

Lean bison with a salted crust and a pink center.

5 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Plains nations, including the Lakota, dried and fire-cooked bison long before ranches and rail shipments. This steak is a modern whole-muscle grill from ranch or market bison. It is not a ceremonial recipe, and it is not a dried-meat method. The temperature here is 145°F with a rest, because bison is lean and dries out if it is cooked like a well-done roast.

Cultural background

A ranch or market bison steak on a hot grill. It does not claim to be a Lakota ceremonial dish or a historical drying recipe.

Why it tastes this way

Bison is leaner than most beef, so the flavor is beefy without a thick fat cap, and a short hot grill is what keeps it tender. Salt is enough. The Plains relationship to this animal is older than the steakhouse cut, which is why the history and the recipe are not the same thing.

How it is made

Grill bison steaks quickly over a hot fire and rest them.

Authentic touch

Pull the steak at 145°F and rest it. Cooking bison to brisket temperatures will turn a steak dry and chalky.

How it is used

Sliced, with the juices. It does not need a sweet sauce.

Field notes

  • Lakota and other Plains nations dried and fire-cooked bison long before ranch steaks existed.
  • This is a modern whole-muscle grill, not a ceremonial recipe.

Ingredients

  • 4 bison steaks, about 1 inch thick
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Oil the steaks and season them with salt and pepper. Let the grill get hot.
  2. Grill, turning once. Bison is lean, so do not walk away.
  3. Cook until the center reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes. This is a market steak, not a ceremonial cook and not a drying recipe.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A ribeye or strip from bison uses the same temperature. Do not treat it as brisket.

Pairings

  • potatoes
  • grilled onion
  • green salad
