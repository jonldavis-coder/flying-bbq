South African Lamb Tjops

Next stop: Cape Malay Sosaties · continuing the voyage

Pronounced chorps

Lamb chops with salt, a browned fat edge, and braai smoke.

10 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Tjops are lamb chops on a South African braai. The word is the Afrikaans pronunciation of chops. Karoo and other ranching districts built a reputation on lamb, and the chop is the quick piece: salted and grilled, not marinated for days. Beaufort West stands in for that dry interior. The meat is a whole cut and is rested after it reaches temperature.

Cultural background

South African braai cooks put tjops on the grid next to sausage and salad. It is lamb, plainly seasoned. It is not sosaties and not a mutton stew.

Why it tastes this way

A hot grid crisps the fat cap and leaves the eye of the chop juicy if you do not cook it to shreds. Salt and wood or charcoal smoke are the seasoning. Karoo and similar grazing have a reputation for a clean lamb flavor, which a thick sauce would cover. The braai is social, and the chops are one of the faster items on it.

How it is made

Salt lamb chops and grill them over a hot braai, then rest them.

Authentic touch

Salt the chops and leave them. Turn once. A flare-up from the fat should be moved, not pressed out.

How it is used

With a green salad or pap. Lemon is optional.

Field notes

  • Tjops is the braai name for lamb chops in South Africa.
  • The word comes from the Afrikaans pronunciation of chops.

Ingredients

  • 8 lamb chops
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Oil the chops and season them with salt and pepper.
  2. Grill over a hot braai, turning once, until the fat browns.
  3. Cook whole lamb to at least 145°F (63°C) and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve at once. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A light sprinkle of coriander, which some braai cooks borrow from the sausage tin.
  • Shoulder chops, which need a little more time than a thin loin chop.

Pairings

  • Green salad
  • Pap
  • Lemon
