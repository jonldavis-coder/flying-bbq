Georgian Mtsvadi

Next stop: Armenian Khorovats · continuing the voyage

Pronounced mts-VAH-dee

Pork with onion, a little vinegar, and grapevine or charcoal smoke.

20 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 skewers

History

Mtsvadi are Georgian skewers, often pork or veal, grilled over charcoal or grapevine wood at supras and ordinary gatherings. This recipe uses pork and notes that veal is also common. The meat is cut large, salted, and sometimes rested with onion. Pomegranate juice is a frequent table accompaniment and is not required in the marinade. It is a Caucasus grill, not a Russian dacha copy.

Cultural background

Georgian hosts cook mtsvadi when people are coming to eat for a long time. It is pork in this version. It is not khinkali and not a stew.

Why it tastes this way

Pork shoulder cubes take a hard grill and stay rich. Vine wood, when it is the fuel, adds a slight fruit-wood smoke that charcoal only partly matches. Onion and salt are the pantry. The feast setting matters: the meat is cooked in batches while people are already at the table, so it is eaten hot off the skewer.

How it is made

Season large pork cubes, skewer them, and grill over hot coals until they reach a safe temperature and rest.

Authentic touch

Cut the pieces larger than street-souvlaki cubes. If you have dry grapevine cuttings, use them as part of the fuel, not as a garnish.

How it is used

With raw onion, herbs, and pickles. A spoon of pomegranate seeds is optional and not in the cook.

Field notes

  • Mtsvadi are Georgian skewers, commonly pork or veal.
  • This page uses pork and treats veal as another usual choice, not as a different dish.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb pork shoulder, cut in 1 1/2 inch cubes
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon wine vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 4 metal skewers

Steps

  1. Toss the pork with onion, vinegar, salt, and pepper. Rest 30 minutes in the refrigerator.
  2. Thread onto skewers. Heat the coals until they are hot.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest cube reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Give the meat a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with onion and herbs. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Veal shoulder in the same cut and to the same whole-muscle temperature.
  • A handful of dry vine cuttings on the coals for smoke.

Pairings

  • Raw onion
  • Fresh herbs
  • Pickles
