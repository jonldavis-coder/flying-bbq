Seekh Kebab

Next stop: Boti Kebab · continuing the voyage

Pronounced seek keh-BAB

Minced lamb with onion, green chili, cilantro, and warm spice, browned on a skewer.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Seekh means skewer. The kebab is minced lamb pressed along that skewer and cooked over charcoal or in a tandoor. It is a North Indian Muslim grill dish, kin to other minced kebabs and different from boti, which is cut in chunks. Awadhi cooks in Lucknow made very fine versions; this one is a home seekh, spiced and grilled, not a claim about one inventor.

Cultural background

The meat is ground lamb, so it has to be cooked through. There is no yogurt and no wheat in the mix. It is not a chunk kebab and not a cream-based kakori paste.

Why it tastes this way

Fat in the lamb bastes the mince as it turns, and onion and green chili keep it from tasting like a plain sausage. Charcoal browns the outside while the center comes up to a ground-meat temperature. Cilantro and cumin are the green, bright note against that browned lamb.

How it is made

Knead ground lamb with onion, chili, and spice, press it onto skewers, and grill it until the center is fully cooked.

Authentic touch

Wet your hands and press the mince on firmly. Loose meat falls into the fire before it sets.

How it is used

Slide the kebabs onto a plate and eat them with onion, mint, and lemon.

Field notes

  • Seekh refers to the skewer the minced lamb is cooked on.
  • Ground lamb has to reach a higher temperature than a whole lamb chop.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds ground lamb
  • 1 small onion, very finely chopped and squeezed dry
  • 1/4 cup chopped cilantro
  • 2 green chilies, minced
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1.5 teaspoons salt

Steps

  1. Knead the lamb with the onion, cilantro, chili, ginger, garlic, cumin, coriander, and salt until the mince is sticky. Keep it cold. It is still raw. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  2. Press the mince into long shapes around flat skewers. Refrigerate them if they wait. Do not leave garlic sitting in a warm kitchen. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  3. Grill over a hot fire, turning gently, until the center of each kebab reaches 160°F (71°C). Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  4. Rest them 3 minutes off the fire. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A flat metal skewer holds the mince better than a thin round stick.
  • A hotter green chili is a common change. This amount is medium.

Pairings

  • sliced onion
  • mint leaves
  • lemon
