Ugandan Muchomo

Next stop: Ethiopian Grilled Tibs · continuing the voyage

Pronounced moo-CHO-mo

Roasted goat with salt, a little chili, and smoke from an open fire.

15 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Muchomo is the usual word in Uganda for meat roasted over an open fire, sold at roadsides into Kampala and eaten at gatherings. Goat is a common choice, and beef is also roasted. The seasoning is often salt and a chili salt rather than a long marinade. It is a fire you stand next to. This recipe uses goat and keeps the method plain.

Cultural background

Ugandan roadside roasters and hosts at weekend fires cook muchomo. It is roasted meat, not a groundnut stew. The goat is a whole-muscle cut.

Why it tastes this way

An open fire dries the surface of goat and smokes the fat. Salt and chili are added so each piece is seasoned after or before the roast, not buried in a sauce. The roadside setting means the meat is eaten hot, often with banana or cassava that has been cooked separately. The fire is the flavor.

How it is made

Salt goat and roast it over an open fire until it is safe, then rest and slice it.

Authentic touch

Turn the pieces often over the coals. A still roast burns one side and leaves the other raw.

How it is used

Sliced, with chili salt, tomato, and onion. Matooke or another starch can sit beside it if you cook that starch on its own.

Field notes

  • Muchomo is the usual word in Uganda for meat roasted over an open fire.
  • Goat is a common choice at roadside fires.

Ingredients

  • 3 lb bone-in goat pieces
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 teaspoon chili flakes
  • 1 onion, sliced

Steps

  1. Rub the goat with oil, salt, and half the chili. Build an open coal fire.
  2. Roast over the coals, turning often, until the outside is browned.
  3. Cook whole goat to at least 145°F (63°C), away from bone, and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Slice and serve with onion and the rest of the chili. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Beef on the same fire, still a whole cut.
  • A hotter chili salt mixed at the table.

Pairings

  • Chili salt
  • Tomato
  • Onion
