Santa Maria Pinquito Beans

Next stop: Mexican Elote · continuing the voyage

Pronounced peen-KEE-toh

Small pink beans, soft and savory, with onion, garlic, and chile.

20 min prep · 2 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Pinquito beans are the small pink beans grown around Santa Maria, California, and they are the pot that sits beside the valley's grilled beef. The Santa Maria grill is a central-coast ranch table. Some pots add cured pork. This written recipe has no meat, so it stays vegetarian. The beans are boiled until they are completely tender.

Cultural background

Use dried pinquito beans, or small pink beans if the local name is not on the bag. Soak them, then boil them. Do not hold raw dried beans warm. There is no bacon, no lard, and no dairy in the pot.

Why it tastes this way

Small pink beans take on onion, garlic, and a dried chile and stay a little brothy. Next to beef grilled over red oak, the traditional local wood, they are the mild, filling side. This pot leaves the meat on a different plate, so the bean flavor stays vegetal and clean.

How it is made

Soak dried pinquito beans, boil them until they are completely tender, and season them with onion, garlic, and chile.

Authentic touch

Boil the beans hard for at least ten minutes before you drop to a simmer. Tender means no chalky center, not just a soft skin.

How it is used

Eat them beside grilled beef, salsa, and bread. The beef and the bread are pairings and are not in this pot.

Field notes

  • Pinquito beans are the small pink bean associated with the Santa Maria Valley in California.
  • This pot has no meat, so the written recipe stays vegetarian.

Ingredients

  • 1 pound dried pinquito beans or small pink beans, picked over
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 dried mild chile such as guajillo, stemmed
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 8 cups water

Steps

  1. Rinse the beans and soak them overnight in plenty of water in the refrigerator. Drain them. There is no meat in this pot.
  2. Cover the soaked beans with the 8 cups of water. Bring them to a hard boil for at least 10 minutes. Do not cook raw dried beans only at a warm temperature.
  3. Add the onion, garlic, chile, and oil. Simmer until the beans are completely tender, about 1 1/2 to 2 hours. Add water if they go dry.
  4. Stir in the salt near the end. The beans should be fully soft, with no chalky center. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Boil dried beans until they are completely tender. Kidney beans must be boiled hard, not held warm from raw. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Small pink beans are the stand-in if a bag does not say pinquito. Say which bean you cooked.
  • A cured-pork version exists on some ranch tables. It is not this recipe.

Pairings

  • grilled beef
  • salsa
  • green salad
