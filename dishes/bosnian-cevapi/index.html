Bosnian Cevapi

Next stop: Romanian Mititei · continuing the voyage

Pronounced CHEH-vah-pee

Grilled minced beef and lamb, salty and lightly spiced, with a browned crust.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Ćevapi are skinless grilled sausages of minced beef and lamb, a Sarajevo standard that is also cooked across Bosnia and Herzegovina. Grilled minced meat of this kind spread through the Balkans in the Ottoman era, and no trustworthy record names a single inventor. The sausages are shaped by hand and cooked over charcoal. Onion is the usual accompaniment. This mix has no starch filler.

Cultural background

Bosnian grill shops and home cooks make ćevapi as ordinary food. They are not a burger and not a lamb chop. The Ottoman minced-meat grill is the historical frame, without a fake founder.

Why it tastes this way

Beef and lamb together give the sausage both lean chew and fat. A hot grill sets the outside before the inside dries. The seasoning is restrained, so the meat and the charcoal do the talking. Onion, served raw, cuts the richness. That pattern of a simple mince over coals is what traveled in the Ottoman period, in many local shapes.

How it is made

Mix beef and lamb with salt and onion, form small sausages, and grill them until the center is cooked through.

Authentic touch

Knead the mince until it is tacky so the sausages hold without a casing. Keep them small and turn them often.

How it is used

With raw onion and a pepper relish.

Field notes

  • Ćevapi are skinless sausages of minced beef and lamb, closely associated with Sarajevo grills.
  • Ottoman-era minced-meat grilling is the broad history, and no single inventor is established.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ground beef
  • 1/2 lb ground lamb
  • 1 small onion, grated
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda

Steps

  1. Mix the beef, lamb, onion, salt, pepper, and baking soda until the meat is tacky. Chill 15 minutes.
  2. Form small sausages about 3 inches long. Heat a grill to a medium-hot coal bed.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C). Ground meat is not served rare.
  4. Rest a few minutes and serve with onion. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • All beef if lamb is unavailable, still cooked as ground meat.
  • A little baking soda in the mix, which some shops use for a springier bite.

Pairings

  • Raw onion
  • Pepper relish
  • Tomato
