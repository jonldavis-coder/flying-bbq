Armenian Khorovats

Next stop: Russian Dacha Shashlik · continuing the voyage

Pronounced kho-ro-VATS

Lamb with onion, salt, and a clean charcoal char.

25 min prep · 16 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 skewers

History

Khorovats is the Armenian grill of meat on skewers, cooked for weekends and gatherings in Yerevan and in the regions. Pork and lamb are both common. This page picks lamb and says plainly that pork is also common. The marinade is usually onion and salt rather than a long list of spices. Vegetables are often grilled beside the meat. It is a Caucasus fire, related to neighboring skewers and still its own table.

Cultural background

Armenian hosts tend the khorovats while guests are already together. This version is lamb. It is not a dolma and not the Russian home shashlik described separately.

Why it tastes this way

Onion juice tenderizes the surface and smells sweet as it cooks off. Lamb fat over charcoal is the main flavor, which is why the spice list stays short. Grilled pepper and tomato on the same fire pick up the same smoke and make the plate feel complete. The choice of lamb or pork changes the fat, not the method.

How it is made

Skewer lamb cubes, grill them over charcoal, and rest them. Pork can be cooked the same way as another common choice.

Authentic touch

Salt the lamb and leave most of the onion on the side to eat raw. Burnt onion on the skewer turns bitter.

How it is used

With grilled peppers, tomato, and raw onion.

Field notes

  • Khorovats is Armenia's skewer grill, and both pork and lamb are common.
  • This recipe uses lamb and treats pork as the other usual meat, not as a mistake.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb boneless lamb, cut in 1 1/2 inch cubes
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 4 metal skewers

Steps

  1. Toss the lamb with onion, salt, pepper, and oil. Rest 20 minutes, then thread the meat on skewers.
  2. Grill over hot charcoal, turning so the faces brown evenly.
  3. Cook whole lamb to at least 145°F (63°C) and give it a 3-minute rest. Pork, if you use it instead, follows the same temperature and rest.
  4. Serve with onion and grilled vegetables. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pork shoulder cubes, still a whole cut, if that is the meat on hand.
  • Eggplant and pepper grilled beside the skewers.

Pairings

  • Grilled peppers
  • Tomato
  • Raw onion
