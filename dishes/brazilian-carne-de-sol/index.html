Northeastern Carne de Sol

Next stop: Chilean Asado · continuing the voyage

Pronounced KAR-neh jee sohl

Salty grilled beef from the Northeast, served with cassava and a fresh relish.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Carne de sol is beef salted in the Northeast of Brazil, traditionally dried in the sun and then cooked. The name means meat of the sun. It is a sertão food, not the fat-capped picanha of southern churrasco, and it does not belong to the southern asado set. This home recipe does not ask you to cure or sun-dry meat. Buy prepared carne de sol, or salt a steak briefly in the refrigerator and grill it.

Cultural background

The written cook is a hot grill of whole-muscle beef. A short refrigerator salting is seasoning, not a cure. Sun-drying in the yard is not a step. Cassava and a tomato relish are the usual company.

Why it tastes this way

Salt concentrates the beef, and a hot grill browns it before the slice dries out. The Northeast is hotter and drier than the southern grasslands, which is why salting, rather than a long wood roast of a fat cap, became the keeping method. The plate tastes salty and direct, with lime or a fresh relish to balance it.

How it is made

Grill prepared or briefly salted beef to a whole-muscle temperature. Do not cure it at home.

Authentic touch

If the market meat is very salty, soak it in cold water in the refrigerator and change the water once before you grill.

How it is used

Serve it with boiled cassava, rice, and a tomato relish. Butter on the cassava would add dairy and is not in this recipe.

Field notes

  • The name means meat of the sun and belongs to the Northeast, where salt was a way to keep beef.
  • It is not picanha, and this recipe does not include home curing.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds beef steak, or purchased carne de sol
  • 2 teaspoons salt, only if you are not using already salted meat
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • Cold water, if the purchased meat needs a rinse

Steps

  1. If the beef is already salted, rinse it and refrigerate it in cold water for 30 minutes, then pat it dry. If it is fresh steak, salt it and refrigerate it for 1 hour. Do not sun-dry it and do not cure it. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Oil the meat lightly and heat a grill. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested beef. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Purchased carne de sol may only need a rinse and a grill. Follow the salt you taste, and still take a temperature.
  • A thin steak salted for one hour in the refrigerator is the stand-in, and it should be called a stand-in.

Pairings

  • boiled cassava
  • rice
  • tomato relish
