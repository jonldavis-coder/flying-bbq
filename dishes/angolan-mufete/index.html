Angolan Mufete

Next stop: Congolese Liboke · continuing the voyage

Pronounced moo-FET-eh

Grilled fish with palm oil, onion, beans, and sweet plantain.

30 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Mufete is an Angolan plate of grilled fish with beans and plantain, strongly associated with Luanda weekends. The fish is whole, the beans are cooked through, and ripe plantain is boiled or grilled until soft. Palm oil is a common finish. This page does not use raw bitter cassava. The fish is the center, and the beans and plantain are part of the plate rather than optional garnish.

Cultural background

Luanda households cook mufete when there is fish and time. It is a grilled fish plate, not a stew of dried fish. The beans must be fully cooked if they start out dried.

Why it tastes this way

A charred fish, a soft plantain, and a bean relish give three textures. Palm oil adds fruit and color. Onion and a little chili keep the beans savory. The Atlantic catch and the weekend table are why the plate is large. Leaving the cassava out avoids a food this recipe is not equipped to process safely.

How it is made

Grill a whole fish, and serve it with fully cooked beans and ripe plantain.

Authentic touch

Grill the fish whole and simmer the beans until they are completely soft. If you use dried kidney beans, boil them hard. Do not hold them warm from raw.

How it is used

The fish beside the beans and plantain, with onion. It is a shared plate.

Field notes

  • Mufete is an Angolan plate of grilled fish with beans and plantain.
  • Luanda weekends are closely associated with the dish.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole firm fish, about 3 lb, gutted and scaled
  • 2 cups dried beans, or 4 cups beans already boiled until tender
  • 3 ripe plantains, peeled and halved
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons palm oil or another oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 fresh chili, chopped

Steps

  1. If the beans are dried, boil them in plenty of water until completely tender. Kidney beans must be boiled hard, not held warm from raw. Drain them.
  2. Simmer the plantains in salted water until soft, or grill them beside the fish. Rub the fish with salt and oil.
  3. Grill the fish until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh flakes.
  4. Toss the beans with onion, chili, and palm oil. Serve with the fish and plantain. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Whatever firm local fish is fresh, named as that fish.
  • Sweet potato in place of plantain if plantain is not ripe.

Pairings

  • Beans
  • Plantain
  • Onion
