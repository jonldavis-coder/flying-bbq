Country Ham Steak

Next stop: Appalachian Vinegar Chicken · continuing the voyage

Salty, dry-cured ham with a browned edge.

15 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Country ham is a salt-cured, aged ham sold by producers in Virginia, Kentucky, Tennessee, North Carolina, and nearby states. The cure is the product you buy. This page does not give a curing recipe, a nitrate schedule, or aging times. It only cooks a steak cut from that ham on a griddle.

Cultural background

A purchased country-ham steak, fried. The cure happened before you bought it. This is not a fresh pork chop.

Why it tastes this way

Salt and time concentrate the pork, so a thin steak tastes intense after a short fry. A little surface browning is the cook's part. The mountain and upper-South climate of traditional hams was cool storage, which is the producer's work, not a step in this skillet.

How it is made

Fry a purchased country-ham steak on a griddle or in a skillet until it is heated through.

Authentic touch

Rinse or soak the steak briefly in cold water if it is too salty for you. That is a cook's rinse, not a cure. Pat it dry before it hits the pan.

How it is used

Sliced or whole, often with biscuits or eggs at breakfast. Biscuits and some egg dishes would add wheat and egg, which this steak recipe does not include.

Field notes

  • Country ham is salt-cured before you buy it. This page does not give a cure.
  • A steak from that ham is a fry, not a long smoke.

Ingredients

  • 4 purchased country-ham steaks, about 1/2 inch thick
  • Cold water, for a brief rinse
  • 1 teaspoon neutral oil, if the steaks are very lean
  • Black pepper to taste

Steps

  1. Buy the ham already cured from a producer. Do not try to cure one from these instructions.
  2. Rinse the steaks or soak them 10 minutes in cold water, then pat them dry. This only pulls a little surface salt.
  3. Heat a plancha or skillet and cook the steaks until the center reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes. Pepper them if you want. They are already salty.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Red-eye gravy, made by loosening the pan with black coffee, is a common companion and is still not a cure.

Pairings

  • biscuits
  • grits
  • eggs
