Hyderabad Pathar ka Gosht

Next stop: Kakori Kebab · continuing the voyage

Pronounced PUH-tuhr kah gosht

Thin mutton steaks, spiced and seared on a very hot flat stone or a cast-iron griddle.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Pathar ka gosht is a Hyderabad dish of mutton cooked on a pathar, a thick flat stone used as a griddle. The meat is usually a thin steak, marinated with yogurt and spice, and it cooks fast on that hot surface. This home recipe uses a cast-iron griddle. It does not ask you to heat a found rock; a restaurant pathar is a purpose-made cooking stone, not a river stone from outside.

Cultural background

Hyderabadi cooks sear whole-muscle mutton this way. It is not a minced kebab and not a tandoor chicken. Yogurt in the marinade means dairy. The home pan is a griddle, named as a griddle.

Why it tastes this way

A screaming-hot flat surface browns thin mutton before the inside dries, which is why the steaks are cut thin. Yogurt, chili, and coriander make a savory crust. The stone in a Hyderabad shop holds heat the way a thick cast-iron griddle does at home.

How it is made

Marinate thin mutton steaks, then sear them on a cast-iron griddle standing in for the Hyderabad stone.

Authentic touch

Pound or slice the mutton thin. A thick chop will steam on the griddle and miss the point of the dish.

How it is used

Eat it at once with onion, lemon, and a piece of bread only if you want wheat on the side. The meat itself has none.

Field notes

  • Pathar means stone. In Hyderabad the meat is cooked on a flat cooking stone.
  • This recipe uses a cast-iron griddle and does not heat a found rock.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds boneless mutton or lamb leg, sliced 1/3 inch thick
  • 1/2 cup plain yogurt
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon Kashmiri chili powder
  • 1.5 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Mix the yogurt, ginger, garlic, coriander, chili, salt, and oil. Coat the meat and refrigerate it for 30 minutes. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The meat is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Heat a cast-iron griddle until it is very hot. This griddle stands in for a Hyderabad pathar. Do not heat a found rock. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Sear the steaks until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Work in batches so the pan stays hot. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the meat 3 minutes. Serve it hot and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lamb leg steaks are the right cut if mutton is not sold.
  • A hotter chili is used in some Hyderabadi kitchens. This batch is medium.
