Santa Maria Tri-Tip

Next stop: Huli Huli Chicken · continuing the voyage

Beefy tri-tip with salt, pepper, garlic, and red-oak smoke.

15 min prep · 35 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Santa Maria tri-tip belongs to the California central coast and its ranch and vaquero beef culture. The local fire is red oak, and the cut is the triangular bottom sirloin. Pinquito beans, a small pink local bean, are often served beside the meat as a pairing, not as a step in this roast. The cook is an open grill, not a Southern offset pit.

Cultural background

Tri-tip over red oak on the central California coast. Western US fire. Beans and salsa can sit beside it and are separate recipes.

Why it tastes this way

Red oak gives a lighter smoke than mesquite, which suits a roast that is sliced at steak temperature rather than cooked to shreds. Salt, pepper, and garlic are the rub. The dry coast and an open grate brown the fat cap without a long stall.

How it is made

Grill a salted tri-tip over red oak coals and rest it before slicing.

Authentic touch

Slice across the grain. Tri-tip grain changes direction, so turn the knife when the slices start to look stringy.

How it is used

Sliced, with salsa. Pinquito or another small pink bean can be on the table and is not cooked inside this roast.

Field notes

  • Red oak is the wood Santa Maria names for tri-tip.
  • Pinquito beans are a small pink bean associated with the valley, served beside the meat rather than inside this recipe.

Ingredients

  • 1 beef tri-tip, about 2 1/2 pounds
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 2 teaspoons coarse black pepper
  • 2 teaspoons garlic powder
  • Red oak for the fire, or another oak

Steps

  1. Mix salt, pepper, and garlic powder and rub the tri-tip. Rest it in the refrigerator if you season ahead.
  2. Burn red oak, or another oak, down to coals in an open grill.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C). Local plates are often pink; this is the safety line used here.
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes and slice across the grain.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Another oak is the workable substitute if coastal red oak is unavailable. Say you used it.

Pairings

  • salsa
  • pinquito beans
  • green salad
