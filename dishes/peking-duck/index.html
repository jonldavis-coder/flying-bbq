Home-Roast Peking Duck

Next stop: Cantonese Char Siu · on Asian grills by plane, by Plane

Pronounced PEE-king duck

Roast duck with lacquered skin, eaten with scallion, cucumber, and wheat pancakes.

40 min prep · 2 hr cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Peking duck is a Beijing restaurant duck, lacquered and roasted so the skin crisps, then carved and eaten with scallion, cucumber, and a sweet sauce in thin wheat pancakes. Restaurant ovens and a long drying step are part of that service. This is a simplified home roast. It is not a restaurant ticket, and it does not reproduce a hung duck oven; the duck still has to reach a poultry temperature; the pancakes and hoisin contain wheat, and hoisin contains soy.

Cultural background

Beijing restaurants built the dish guests travel for. A home oven can roast a duck and serve the same accompaniments if you say it is the home roast. There is duck, wheat, soy, and sesame in the sauce. There is no dairy.

Why it tastes this way

Duck skin renders and crisps when the bird is dried and roasted in open heat, and the fat underneath keeps the meat rich. Scallion and cucumber are cool against that fat. A sweet wheat-and-soy sauce ties the pancake to the skin. A restaurant oven does this more evenly. The home bird can still taste of rendered duck, which is the center of the plate.

How it is made

Dry a duck, roast it in a home oven until the thigh is done, and serve the carved meat with scallion, cucumber, hoisin, and wheat pancakes.

Authentic touch

Dry the skin. A wet duck steams, and the pancake then wraps soft skin, which is a different meal.

How it is used

Slice skin and meat and eat them in small wheat pancakes with scallion, cucumber, and hoisin.

Field notes

  • Peking duck is a Beijing restaurant roast. This page is a simplified home roast, not a restaurant ticket.
  • Duck is poultry and is cooked to 165°F (74°C).

Ingredients

  • 1 whole duck, about 5 pounds
  • 2 tablespoons maltose or brown sugar, dissolved in 1/2 cup hot water
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon five-spice powder
  • 8 wheat pancakes
  • 4 scallions, cut in slivers
  • 1 cucumber, cut in sticks
  • 1/4 cup hoisin sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil

Steps

  1. Pat the duck dry. Rub the cavity with salt and five spice. Paint the skin with the maltose or sugar water. Refrigerate the duck uncovered so the skin dries. The duck is still raw. Do not leave it on the counter to dry. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Heat a home oven to 375°F (190°C). Set the duck on a rack. This is a home roast, not a restaurant ticket and not a hung oven. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Roast until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone. Do not serve the duck rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest it 10 minutes, carve it, and serve with pancakes, scallion, cucumber, and hoisin stirred with the sesame oil. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A day of uncovered drying in the refrigerator improves the skin.
  • Store-bought wheat pancakes are the wrapper. Rice wrappers would be a different dish.

Pairings

  • wheat pancakes
  • scallion
  • cucumber
  • hoisin
