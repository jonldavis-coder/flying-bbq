Dearborn Lamb Skewers

Charcoal lamb with garlic, salt, and a little allspice.

25 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Dearborn is home to a large Lebanese American community, and charcoal lamb skewers are part of that home and restaurant table. No one restaurant invented the practice. The seasoning here is a simple garlic and allspice marinade a home cook can mix, not a secret house blend. The meat is lamb, cooked to a whole-muscle temperature and rested.

Cultural background

Lebanese American charcoal lamb in Dearborn. A skewer cook, not a claim that one shop owns the recipe.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb fat browns over charcoal, and garlic and allspice are seasonings Lebanese American cooks use without needing a sweet barbecue sauce. Cutting the meat into cubes shortens the cook so the inside stays juicy. Migration from Lebanon to industrial Detroit is why this fire sits in Dearborn rather than only on a Mediterranean coast.

How it is made

Marinate lamb cubes briefly and grill them on skewers over charcoal.

Authentic touch

Keep the marinade refrigerated and grill the same day. Do not leave garlic in oil on the counter.

How it is used

Off the skewers, with rice or a salad. A pita would add wheat and is not in this ingredient list.

Field notes

  • Dearborn's Lebanese American community has made charcoal lamb a local table, not the invention of one restaurant.
  • The marinade is used the same day and kept cold, because garlic in oil should not sit at room temperature.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds lamb shoulder, cut into 1 1/2-inch cubes
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground allspice
  • Metal skewers

Steps

  1. Toss the lamb with oil, garlic, salt, pepper, and allspice. Refrigerate it and grill it the same day. Do not store the garlic oil at room temperature.
  2. Thread the cubes on metal skewers. Heat charcoal until it is hot.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center of a cube reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes. Serve with rice or salad. Pita is not part of this recipe.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Beef cubes can be grilled the same way. This written recipe is lamb.
