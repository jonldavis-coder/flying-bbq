Malaysian Ikan Bakar

Next stop: Kelantan Ayam Percik · continuing the voyage

Pronounced EE-kahn BAH-kar

Charcoal-grilled fish with a hot sambal of chili, lime, and shrimp paste.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Ikan bakar means grilled fish, and in Malaysia it is a coastal meal: a whole fish or a large piece, cooked over charcoal and eaten with sambal. The east coast, including Terengganu, grills a great deal of fish this way. The sambal here uses chili, lime, and shrimp paste. It is not a fried fish and not a coconut curry; the flesh is cooked until it flakes.

Cultural background

Coastal Malay cooks and grill stalls sell ikan bakar wrapped in banana leaf or set straight on the grate. Shrimp paste in the sambal is shellfish, and the fish is fish. There is no wheat and no peanut in this sambal.

Why it tastes this way

Oily or firm coastal fish can take a hot sambal and a smoky grate. Shrimp paste adds a savory depth that chili alone does not have, and lime keeps the sauce sharp. The banana leaf, when you have one, steams the spice onto the skin. Without it, the grill still chars the fish, which is the bakar part.

How it is made

Grill a whole fish and serve it with a sambal of chili, lime, and shrimp paste.

Authentic touch

Oil the grate and do not flip a delicate fish more than once. The sambal can go on after the first side has set.

How it is used

Eat the fish with rice and the sambal spooned on at the table.

Field notes

  • Ikan bakar is grilled fish, a coastal Malaysian meal rather than a curry.
  • Shrimp paste in the sambal is a shellfish ingredient.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole fish, about 2 pounds, cleaned and scored
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 6 hot red chilies
  • 1 teaspoon shrimp paste
  • 2 garlic cloves, peeled
  • 2 tablespoons lime juice
  • 1 teaspoon sugar

Steps

  1. Rub the fish with salt and oil. Blend the chilies, shrimp paste, garlic, lime juice, and sugar into a sambal. The fish is still raw. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  2. Heat a grill. Oil the grate. Cook the fish with the cavity closed. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  3. Grill, turning once, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve with the sambal. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Banana leaf around the fish is common when the leaf is sold. Parchment is only a wrapper, not a leaf.
  • A milder chili makes a gentler sambal. This one is hot.

Pairings
