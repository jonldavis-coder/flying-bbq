Papua New Guinea Mumu

Pronounced MOO-moo

Chicken and sweet potato steamed with ginger and greens in a covered pot.

30 min prep · 1 hr 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Mumu, in Papua New Guinea, is an earth-oven cook, and the practice differs by community. One village's leaves, meats, and stones are not a national recipe. This page does not pretend they are. It is a home covered cook of chicken, sweet potato, ginger, and greens, for a kitchen in Port Moresby or anywhere else; it is not a ceremony; there is no raw cassava; the chicken is cooked to a poultry temperature.

Cultural background

People across Papua New Guinea cook with earth ovens in ways that are local. A single written pot cannot represent them. This pot is chicken and sweet potato, with ginger. There is no wheat, dairy, or peanut. Call it a home covered cook.

Why it tastes this way

Ginger and closed steam scent the chicken, and sweet potato takes the juices. Greens wilt into the pot and keep it from tasting only of meat. A community mumu can include foods and timings this page will not universalize. The home pot tastes of the ingredients you actually put in it, which is the honest limit.

How it is made

Cook chicken, sweet potato, ginger, and greens in a covered pot until the chicken is done. Treat it as a home cook, not as every mumu.

Authentic touch

Say that practices differ. Do not add a leaf you cannot name, and do not add raw cassava.

How it is used

Serve the chicken, sweet potato, and greens together.

Field notes

  • Mumu practices in Papua New Guinea differ by community. There is no single national recipe.
  • This is a home covered cook of chicken and sweet potato, not a ceremony.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds chicken pieces
  • 2 pounds sweet potato, peeled and chunked
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 4 cups chopped greens, such as spinach
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 mild chili, sliced
  • 1/2 cup water

Steps

  1. Put the chicken, sweet potato, ginger, greens, onion, salt, chili, and water in a heavy pot. Do not add cassava. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Cover the pot. Practices differ, and this is a home covered cook, not a ceremony and not every community's mumu. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Cook until the thickest chicken piece reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone, and the sweet potato is tender. Do not serve the chicken rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest a few minutes and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pork is used in some places and would need a pork temperature. This pot is chicken.
  • Banana leaves can line the pot when they are sold. They are a liner, not a claim of authenticity by themselves.
