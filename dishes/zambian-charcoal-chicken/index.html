Zambian Charcoal Chicken

Next stop: Mali Lamb Brochettes · continuing the voyage

Pronounced charcoal chicken

Chicken with salt, a little garlic, and charcoal smoke.

15 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Charcoal chicken in Zambia is a plain cookout bird, sold and cooked in Lusaka and at home. Nshima, a maize porridge, is the starch people often eat with it, and that porridge is its own food. No ritual is invented here to make the chicken sound older than a weeknight fire. The seasoning is salt, garlic, and maybe a mild chili. The meat is poultry and is cooked through.

Cultural background

Zambian households and roadside grills cook this as ordinary food. It is not a ceremony and not a copy of a restaurant chain. The fire is charcoal.

Why it tastes this way

Salt and charcoal are the flavor. Garlic helps the skin taste finished. A mild chili is optional heat, not a brand sauce. Nshima on the side is mild and filling, which is why a simply grilled bird makes sense next to it. The honesty of a plain recipe is the point.

How it is made

Season chicken and grill it over charcoal until the thigh reaches a safe temperature.

Authentic touch

Do not add a story the cookout does not have. Salt the skin and keep the coals medium so the outside does not carbonize before the thigh is done.

How it is used

With nshima or a salad. The porridge is a side.

Field notes

  • Charcoal chicken in Zambia is a plain cookout bird.
  • No ceremony is invented here to explain it.

Ingredients

  • 4 chicken leg quarters
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 teaspoon mild chili flakes

Steps

  1. Rub the chicken with salt, garlic, oil, and chili.
  2. Grill over medium charcoal, turning now and then.
  3. Cook until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), not touching bone.
  4. Rest 5 minutes. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch of chili in the salt.
  • Pieces rather than a whole bird.
