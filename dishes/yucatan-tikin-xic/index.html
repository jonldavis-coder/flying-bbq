Yucatán Tikin Xic

Next stop: Mixiote in Maguey or Parchment · continuing the voyage

Pronounced tee-KEEN sheek

Fish stained with achiote and sour orange, gently charred, sometimes steamed a little by a leaf.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Tikin xic is a Yucatecan fish cook: achiote and sour orange on a fillet or a split fish, sometimes wrapped in banana leaf and grilled. Achiote is a native American seed. Sour orange arrived after Spanish contact. The leaf is a wrapper cooks use when they have it. This page does not turn the grill into a ceremony.

Cultural background

The dish is fish, in the same seasoning family as Yucatecan pork, with a different animal and a shorter cook. A home kitchen without banana leaves can use parchment and should say so.

Why it tastes this way

Achiote colors the fish and adds a peppery earth taste, and sour orange keeps it bright. If a banana leaf is on the grill, the fish steams a little as well as browns. Without the leaf, the same paste tastes more directly of the fire. Either way the fish, not the wrapper, is the center.

How it is made

Coat fish with achiote and sour orange and grill it, in a leaf if you have one, until it flakes.

Authentic touch

Oil the leaf or the parchment so the paste does not stick and tear the cooked fish.

How it is used

Eat it with corn tortillas, pickled onion, and a tomato salsa.

Field notes

  • Achiote and sour orange are the Yucatecan seasoning pair used here on fish.
  • A banana leaf wraps the fish when cooks have one; it is not itself the seasoning.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds firm white fish fillets, such as snapper or grouper
  • 2 tablespoons ground achiote (annatto)
  • 1/4 cup sour orange juice, or 2 tablespoons orange juice plus 2 tablespoons lime juice
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried Mexican oregano
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 banana leaf, or parchment paper

Steps

  1. Mix achiote, juice, garlic, oregano, salt, and oil. Coat the fish. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. The fish is not ready to eat until it reaches 145°F (63°C) and turns opaque.
  2. Wrap the fish in a banana leaf, or in parchment if you have no leaf. Parchment is not a banana leaf. Refrigerate it if it waits more than 2 hours. The fish is not ready to eat until it reaches 145°F (63°C) and turns opaque.
  3. Grill the packet over a medium-hot fire, turning once, until the center of the thickest fillet reads 145°F (63°C) and the fish is opaque and flakes.
  4. Open the packet carefully. The fish should already be at 145°F (63°C). Eat it the same day, and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Grouper, snapper, or another firm white fish all work. Cook each to the fish temperature.
  • No banana leaf: use parchment, and say the packet is not the leaf.

Pairings

  • corn tortillas
  • pickled red onion
  • tomato salsa
