Korean Beef Galbi

Next stop: Japanese Yakitori · on Asian grills by plane, by Plane

Pronounced GAHL-bee

Beef short ribs in a sweet soy marinade with pear, garlic, and sesame.

30 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Galbi is Korean grilled beef short rib. The meat is often cut thin across the bone, marinated with soy sauce, sugar, garlic, sesame, and grated pear or apple, then grilled. It is a restaurant and home barbecue dish. Ordinary soy sauce contains wheat, and sesame oil contains sesame; the rib meat is a whole cut, cooked and rested; it is not pork belly, and it is not the thinly sliced bulgogi from a different cut.

Cultural background

Korean barbecue tables grill galbi over charcoal or on a gas grill set into the table. The marinade has soy, wheat, and sesame. There is no dairy. Pear tenderizes and sweetens the surface.

Why it tastes this way

Soy, sugar, and pear brown quickly on a thin rib, and sesame oil perfumes the fat. Garlic stays sharp. The bone and the thin cut mean a short grill, so the marinade becomes a crust instead of a boiled sauce. A thick uncut roast would not taste like galbi even with the same bowl.

How it is made

Marinate thin beef short ribs in soy, pear, garlic, and sesame, then grill them and rest them.

Authentic touch

If the ribs are thick, ask the butcher to cut them flanken-style, across the bones. Thick English-cut ribs are a different grill.

How it is used

Cut the meat off the bone if you like and eat it with rice, lettuce, and ssamjang.

Field notes

  • Galbi is beef short rib, often cut thin across the bone for the grill.
  • Ordinary soy sauce in the marinade contains wheat.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds beef short ribs, cut thin across the bone
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce
  • 1 ripe pear, grated
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Mix the soy sauce, pear, sugar, garlic, sesame oil, scallions, and pepper. Coat the ribs and refrigerate them. The beef is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Heat a grill until it is hot. Drain extra marinade off the meat so it does not steam. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the ribs 3 minutes. Serve hot and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pork galbi uses the same marinade family on pork ribs and still needs a whole-pork temperature and rest.
  • A gluten-free soy sauce would change the wheat allergen. This uses ordinary soy sauce.

Pairings

  • rice
  • lettuce
  • ssamjang
