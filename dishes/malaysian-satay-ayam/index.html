Malaysian Chicken Satay

Next stop: Cebu Lechon · on Asian grills by plane, by Plane

Pronounced sah-TAY AH-yum

Turmeric chicken skewers with a warm peanut sauce.

35 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Malaysian satay ayam is chicken on small skewers, marinated with turmeric, lemongrass, and coriander, and served with a peanut sauce. Kajang, in Selangor, is one of the towns known for satay shops. Peanuts reached Southeast Asian sauces through later trade. The sauce contains peanut, so the dish is not nut-free; a Singapore hawker satay is a related city plate and not this Kajang shop.

Cultural background

Malay cooks grill the skewers over charcoal and pound or simmer the peanut sauce beside them. There is chicken and peanut. There is no dairy. This recipe does not use soy sauce, so there is no wheat in the written dish.

Why it tastes this way

Turmeric and lemongrass scent the chicken, and charcoal dries the edges of small pieces. The peanut sauce is rich, slightly sweet, and hot from chili, which is why the meat itself can stay mild. Peanut, not a cream gravy, is the sauce that makes Malaysian chicken satay what it is.

How it is made

Marinate chicken with turmeric and lemongrass, grill it on skewers to a poultry temperature, and serve it with a peanut sauce.

Authentic touch

Cut the chicken small. Large cubes do not cook like satay and they shed the sauce.

How it is used

Eat the skewers with the peanut sauce, cucumber, and rice.

Field notes

  • Kajang is a Selangor town known for Malay satay shops.
  • The sauce is peanut, so this satay is not nut-free.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds boneless chicken thighs, cut in small pieces
  • 1 stalk lemongrass, tender part minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1/2 cup roasted peanuts, ground
  • 1 tablespoon palm sugar
  • 1 hot chili, minced
  • 1/2 cup coconut milk

Steps

  1. Toss the chicken with lemongrass, turmeric, coriander, salt, and oil. Refrigerate it. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Simmer the peanuts, palm sugar, chili, and coconut milk with a pinch of salt for 8 minutes, until the sauce is thick. Stir so it does not scorch. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Thread the chicken on skewers and grill until the center of the thickest piece reaches 165°F (74°C). Do not serve it rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Serve with the peanut sauce. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Beef or goat satay uses the same sauce family and a different meat temperature.
  • A Singapore hawker satay is a different stall, even when the sauce is also peanut.
