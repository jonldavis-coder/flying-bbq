Home-smoked Haddock in the Arbroath Manner

Next stop: Irish Grilled Mackerel · continuing the voyage

Pronounced AR-broath SMO-kee

Hot-smoked haddock, salty and lightly sweet from the wood, still moist inside.

20 min prep · 1 hr cook · Serves 4 · 4 fillets

History

Traditional Arbroath smokies are a protected smokehouse product, tied to a specific way of hot-smoking haddock in Arbroath. This recipe is a home hot-smoke, not a legal copy of the name-protected product. It uses fillets, a kettle, and a short smoke cooked through for the table. Do not sell it or label it as the protected smokie.

Cultural background

The locale is Arbroath, and the fish is haddock. The page is a home method in that direction. It is not a certificate and not a cold-smoked finnan haddie left silky and raw in the center.

Why it tastes this way

Haddock takes smoke without the heavy oil of mackerel. A short hot smoke over hardwood cooks the fish and leaves a pale gold surface. Salt seasons it. The protected town product has its own pits and tied pairs of fish; a backyard kettle can only suggest that flavor.

How it is made

Salt haddock fillets and hot-smoke them in a covered grill until they flake and reach a safe temperature. Call it a home smoke, not the protected name.

Authentic touch

Keep the smoke thin and the heat gentle. If the wood flares, the fish will taste bitter instead of sweet.

How it is used

Hot, with potatoes and a lemon wedge, or flaked into a chowder later. Eat it as cooked fish.

Field notes

  • Arbroath smokies are a protected smokehouse product and are not a name for any home-smoked haddock.
  • A home hot-smoke cooks the fish for the table and is not a legal copy of that product.

Ingredients

  • 4 haddock fillets, about 6 oz each
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • A small handful of hardwood chips, soaked 15 minutes

Steps

  1. Mix the salt and sugar and sprinkle them over the fillets. Rest 20 minutes, then rinse lightly and pat dry. Oil the fish.
  2. Set a covered grill for indirect heat. Add a small handful of drained wood chips to the coals so the smoke stays thin.
  3. Hot-smoke the fillets until they reach 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque and flakes. This is a home hot-smoke, not the protected smokehouse product.
  4. Rest a few minutes and serve hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Whole headed and gutted haddock if you can tend them without breaking the flesh.
  • Oak or beech if those are the woods you have, kept to a light smoke.

Pairings

  • Potatoes
  • Lemon
  • Peas
