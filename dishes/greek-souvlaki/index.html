Greek Pork Souvlaki

Next stop: Cypriot Souvla · continuing the voyage

Pronounced soov-LAH-kee

Pork with oregano, lemon, garlic, and a browned edge.

25 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 skewers

History

Souvlaki is pork on a small skewer in Greece. Stands in Athens and other cities grill it over charcoal and serve it on a plate with onion and tomato. The word refers to the skewer, not to a single marinade recipe. This page uses lemon and oregano and keeps dairy out of the mix.

Cultural background

Street cooks and home grillers both make souvlaki. It is smaller than Cypriot souvla and it is pork here, not chicken. It is not a minced kebab.

Why it tastes this way

Pork shoulder cubes have enough fat to stay juicy on a hot grate. Lemon and oregano are the Greek pantry pair that seasons the surface. Charcoal adds a light smoke. Because the pieces are small, the browning happens fast, which is why a souvlaki stand can feed a line.

How it is made

Marinate pork cubes briefly, thread them, and grill over a hot fire, then rest.

Authentic touch

Cut the pork in even cubes and do not crowd the skewer. Oil the grate so the lean edges do not stick.

How it is used

On the skewer with tomato, onion, and fries.

Field notes

  • Souvlaki means food on a small skewer, and pork is the common meat in Greece.
  • The pieces are smaller than the chunks on a Cypriot souvla.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb pork shoulder, cut in 1 inch cubes
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 lemon, juiced
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Toss the pork with oil, lemon, garlic, oregano, and salt. Rest 20 minutes in the refrigerator.
  2. Thread onto skewers without packing the cubes tight. Heat the grill until it is hot.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest cube reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Give the skewers a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with tomato and onion. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chicken souvlaki, which would be poultry and must reach 165°F (74°C).
  • A plate of the same pork with a salad.

Pairings

  • Tomato
  • Onion
  • Fries
