Indonesian Sate Kambing

Next stop: Balinese Sate Lilit · continuing the voyage

Pronounced SAH-tay KAHM-bing

Goat skewers glazed with sweet soy, with a little chili heat at the edge.

30 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Sate kambing is goat satay, strongly associated with Madura and East Java, where it is grilled on small skewers and brushed with sweet soy. Surabaya is a city where that Madurese grill is easy to find. Kecap manis, the sweet soy, usually contains wheat as well as soy. The goat is cut in small whole pieces, not minced; it is a different satay from a peanut-heavy chicken satay.

Cultural background

Madurese cooks and East Javanese stalls grill goat this way. Sweet soy is the glaze. There is no peanut in this recipe, and there is no dairy. The wheat is in the kecap manis.

Why it tastes this way

Goat is sharper than chicken, and sweet soy caramelizes on the edges so the sharpness has a glossy counterweight. Chili in the glaze warms it without hiding the meat. A peanut sauce would pull it toward a different satay. Here the soy glaze is the point.

How it is made

Season goat chunks, grill them on skewers, and brush them with kecap manis once the meat is nearly done.

Authentic touch

Brush the glaze near the end. If it goes on at the start, the sugar burns before the goat is cooked.

How it is used

Eat the skewers with rice, cucumber, and a little extra chili if you want it hotter.

Field notes

  • Sate kambing from Madura and East Java is goat, often finished with sweet soy.
  • Kecap manis usually contains wheat as well as soy.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds boneless goat, cut in small chunks
  • 3 tablespoons kecap manis (sweet soy sauce; it contains soy and usually wheat)
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon chili flakes
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Toss the goat with garlic, coriander, salt, oil, and 1 tablespoon of the kecap manis. Refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The meat is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Thread the chunks on skewers. Heat a grill. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center of the thickest chunk reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Brush with the remaining kecap manis in the last minutes. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the goat 3 minutes. Serve hot and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A peanut sauce is served with some Javanese satay and would add peanut. This glaze does not.
  • Lamb can stand in where goat is not sold. Treat it as a whole cut and rest it.

Pairings

  • rice
  • cucumber
  • sliced shallot
