Cantonese Roast Goose

Next stop: Korean Samgyeopsal · continuing the voyage

Pronounced roast goose

Roast goose with a seasoned skin, five spice, and a savory gravy from the pan.

30 min prep · 2 hr 30 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Cantonese roast goose is a roast-shop bird, especially associated with Hong Kong shops that also sell char siu and siu yuk. The goose is seasoned, lacquered, and roasted until the skin is glossy and the meat is cooked through. A shop oven is not a home oven. This page roasts a goose, or a large goose leg if a whole bird will not fit, in a home oven and says so; goose is poultry and is cooked to 165°F (74°C); soy sauce in the seasoning brings soy and wheat.

Cultural background

Hong Kong roast shops are the reference for this goose. The home oven is a substitute. There is goose, soy, and wheat. There is no dairy. It is not a festival story this page cannot source. It is a roast-shop technique brought into a kitchen.

Why it tastes this way

Goose is fattier than a chicken, so a hot oven renders the skin and leaves the meat rich. Five spice and soy season that fat. In a shop the bird hangs and the skin dries evenly. At home a rack over a pan collects the fat and still browns the skin, which is the taste you can reach without the shop.

How it is made

Season a goose with five spice and soy, roast it on a rack in a home oven, and cook it until the thigh reaches a poultry temperature.

Authentic touch

Prick the skin, not the meat, so fat can escape and the skin can crisp. Deep holes dry the flesh.

How it is used

Chop it into pieces and eat it with rice and a spoon of the defatted pan juices.

Field notes

  • Cantonese roast goose is a roast-shop bird strongly associated with Hong Kong.
  • Goose is cooked to 165°F (74°C), like other poultry.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole goose, about 8 pounds, or 2 large goose legs
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon five-spice powder
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed

Steps

  1. Prick the skin all over. Rub the goose with soy sauce, five spice, salt, and oil, and put the garlic in the cavity. Refrigerate it uncovered if you have time to dry the skin. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The goose is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Heat a home oven to 375°F (190°C). Set the goose on a rack. This is a home oven, not a Hong Kong roast shop. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Roast until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone. Do not serve it rare. Pour off fat as it collects so the pan does not scorch. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest 10 minutes, then chop. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A whole goose leg is the right size when a whole bird will not fit the oven. Check the temperature at the bone line without touching bone.
  • Maltose in the glaze is traditional in some shops. This recipe uses a soy and five-spice rub.

Pairings
