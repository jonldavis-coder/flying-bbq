Omani Shuwa

Next stop: Yemeni Chicken Mandi · continuing the voyage

Pronounced SHOO-wah

Lamb with cumin, coriander, and chili, slow-cooked until the fat softens.

30 min prep · 4 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Shuwa is lamb cooked for Eid in an earth oven in Oman. The meat is rubbed with spice, wrapped, and buried with fire until it is very tender. A home oven is not a pit. This page uses a covered pot in an oven and says so. The Omani mix is often cumin, coriander, chili, and sometimes a shop blend. There is no single secret formula. The rub below is a simple published-style mix of those spices.

Cultural background

Omani households prepare shuwa for Eid al-Adha and other gatherings. The earth oven is the traditional pot. The home cook here is not digging a pit. It is not a quick grill.

Why it tastes this way

A long, enclosed cook turns lamb fat and spice into one smell: cumin and coriander stay earthy, and chili warms rather than chars. The pit does this by trapping steam and smoke. A covered oven pot keeps the moisture and loses the buried-fire smoke, which is an honest trade. Shop blends vary, so the taste at two houses will not match, and that is normal.

How it is made

Rub a lamb shoulder with a simple cumin, coriander, and chili mix, wrap it, and cook it covered in a home oven until it is safe and tender.

Authentic touch

Wrap the lamb so the spice stays on the meat, and do not uncover the pot for the first half of the cook. Call it a home oven, not a pit.

How it is used

With rice. It is Eid food for the families who make it, pulled or sliced.

Field notes

  • Omani shuwa is Eid lamb cooked in an earth oven, and a home oven is not that pit.
  • Cumin, coriander, and chili are a common mix, and shop blends vary. There is no single secret formula.

Ingredients

  • 1 lamb shoulder, about 5 lb
  • 2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 2 teaspoons ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon chili flakes
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 4 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Mix cumin, coriander, chili, salt, garlic, and oil. Rub the shoulder. This is a simple spice mix, not a secret formula. Wrap the meat in parchment and then foil.
  2. Set it in a covered pot. Heat a home oven to 325°F (165°C). This is a home oven, not an earth pit.
  3. Cook until the thickest part reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and give it a 3-minute rest. For a shoulder you want to pull, continue toward 195°F (90°C).
  4. Rest, then slice or pull. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A shop spice blend from an Omani grocer, used in place of the simple mix and named on the jar.
  • Goat shoulder with the same method, still a whole-muscle roast.
