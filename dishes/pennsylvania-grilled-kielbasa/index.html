Pennsylvania Coal-Region Kielbasa

Next stop: Block Island Grilled Swordfish · continuing the voyage

Pronounced keel-BAH-sah

Garlicky kielbasa, simmered and then browned on a grill.

5 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Polish American communities in Pennsylvania's coal region and at church picnics simmer kielbasa and then brown it on a grill. This plate uses water for the simmer, not beer, so it is a different cook from a Sheboygan brat. Sausage labels vary. If the kielbasa is fresh, cook it to 160°F; if it is fully cooked, heat it through to 165°F.

Cultural background

Picnic kielbasa, simmered then grilled. Polish American food in eastern Pennsylvania, not a Cleveland mustard-and-kraut plate and not a Wisconsin beer bath.

Why it tastes this way

A simmer softens the casing and heats the link before the grill browns it, which is how a picnic line keeps sausage from scorching. Garlic and marjoram in the sausage are the Polish American flavor. Without beer, the taste stays with the pork and the smoke of the grill.

How it is made

Simmer kielbasa in water, then grill it until browned and hot in the center.

Authentic touch

Discard the simmering water. It touched raw sausage if the links were fresh, and it is not a soup.

How it is used

Sliced or whole, with rye bread or potatoes. Rye bread contains wheat; the simmer itself does not use beer.

Field notes

  • Coal-region and picnic kielbasa in Pennsylvania is often simmered before it is grilled.
  • This version uses water, not the beer bath of a Sheboygan brat.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds plain kielbasa, no cheese
  • 2 quarts water
  • 1 onion, halved
  • A grill

Steps

  1. Read the label. Brands differ, and some contain wheat or milk. This recipe does not add beer, but it does not promise the sausage is gluten-free.
  2. Simmer the kielbasa and onion in water for about 10 minutes. Discard the water and the onion.
  3. Grill over medium heat until the casing browns. If the sausage is fresh, cook the center to 160°F (71°C). If the package says fully cooked, heat it through to 165°F (74°C).
  4. Slice and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Onions in the simmer water are a common picnic addition. Discard them with the water if the sausage was raw.

Pairings

  • rye bread
  • potatoes
  • sauerkraut
