Nepali Sekuwa

Pronounced seh-KOO-wah

Goat chunks with ginger, turmeric, and chili, grilled over a hot fire.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Sekuwa is a Nepali grilled skewer, sold in shops and cooked at home, with meat cut in pieces rather than minced. Goat is a usual meat. Ginger, turmeric, and chili are worked into the marinade, and the skewers go over charcoal. It is Nepali cooking; a yogurt-heavy North Indian boti is a different marinade even when both use a skewer.

Cultural background

Nepali cooks grill these skewers in cities such as Pokhara and Kathmandu and in smaller shops along the roads. This recipe uses goat, in whole chunks. There is no wheat and no dairy in the marinade.

Why it tastes this way

Goat has a deeper flavor than young chicken, and turmeric and chili stain the surface before the fire browns it. A hot charcoal grate gives a dry edge. Lemon at the table brightens the fat. The taste sits with Nepali grill shops, where the skewer is the everyday plate.

How it is made

Marinate goat chunks with ginger, turmeric, and chili, then grill them on skewers and rest them.

Authentic touch

Do not crowd the skewers. Goat chunks that touch each other steam instead of grilling.

How it is used

Eat the meat with onion, lemon, and a dusting of chili if you want it hotter.

Field notes

  • Sekuwa is Nepali grilled meat on skewers, cut in pieces rather than minced.
  • Goat is a usual meat for the skewer.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds boneless goat, cut in 1.5-inch chunks
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1.5 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice

Steps

  1. Mix the ginger, garlic, turmeric, chili, cumin, salt, oil, and lemon. Coat the goat and refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The meat is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Thread the chunks on skewers with a little space between them. Heat a charcoal or gas grill. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center of the thickest chunk reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the meat 3 minutes. Serve hot and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chicken sekuwa is common and would be cooked to a poultry temperature. This page is goat.
  • A hotter chili oil on the side is how many shops let you raise the heat.

Pairings

  • sliced onion
  • lemon
  • chili
