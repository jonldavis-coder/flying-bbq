Polish Grilled Kielbasa

Next stop: Grilled Oscypek · continuing the voyage

Pronounced kyel-BAH-sah

Pork sausage with garlic and marjoram, browned over coals.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 sausages

History

Kielbasa is the Polish word for sausage, not one single recipe. Grilling a fresh garlic sausage is a common home fire, at a dacha plot or in a backyard. This version is a fresh pork mix with marjoram and garlic, formed and cooked through. It is not a claim on any one protected regional sausage. Smoked kielbasa from a shop is a different product and is already cooked before it meets the grill.

Cultural background

Polish households grill fresh or smoked sausage as ordinary food. This page is the fresh one, so the temperature matters. It is not a stew of hunter's sausage.

Why it tastes this way

Garlic and marjoram are the Polish sausage notes that survive a simple grill. Coarse pork renders at the surface and picks up smoke. Because the sausage is fresh, the inside has to set, which keeps it juicy if you do not leave it over a flame. The word covers many sausages; the taste here is this herb-and-garlic grill.

How it is made

Mix a fresh pork sausage, chill it, and grill it until the center is cooked.

Authentic touch

Use a coarse grind and a moderate fire. A blasting flame splits fresh sausage and dumps the fat into the coals.

How it is used

With sauerkraut and mustard on the side. The sausage itself has no grain filler.

Field notes

  • Kielbasa is the Polish word for sausage and covers more than one recipe.
  • A fresh grilled sausage is ground meat and is cooked through, unlike a slice of already-smoked sausage warmed on the grate.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb coarsely ground pork
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon dried marjoram
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons cold water

Steps

  1. Mix the pork, garlic, salt, marjoram, pepper, and water. Form six plump sausages. Chill while the grill heats.
  2. Use a moderate coal bed, not a tall flame.
  3. Grill, turning often, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C).
  4. Rest a few minutes. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Already-smoked kielbasa heated on the grill, which is a different product and should still be hot in the center.
  • Patties without casings, cooked to the same temperature.

Pairings

  • Sauerkraut
  • Mustard on the side
  • Pickles
