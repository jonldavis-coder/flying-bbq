Cebu Lechon

Next stop: Xinjiang Lamb Skewers · on Asian grills by plane, by Plane

Pronounced LEH-chon

Slow-roasted pork with salt, garlic, lemongrass, and a crackled skin if the heat allows it.

30 min prep · 4 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Cebu lechon is a whole pig cooked on a spit, seasoned with salt and stuffed with aromatics such as lemongrass, garlic, and spring onion. It is feast food in Cebu, at family events and in specialist shops. A home kitchen that cannot turn a whole pig can roast a pork shoulder with the same seasoning. That shoulder is a home substitute, and it should be labeled as one; it is not the whole Cebu spit.

Cultural background

Cebu cooks are known for a simply seasoned lechon, with the herbs inside the pig rather than a heavy glaze. This page uses a shoulder. There is no dairy and no wheat in the seasoning. The liver sauce is a separate dish.

Why it tastes this way

Salt and a long cook make the pork taste like pork, and lemongrass and garlic inside the meat perfume the drippings. On a spit the skin blisters over coals. A home oven can still brown a shoulder and keep the Cebu seasoning, which is the part you can do without a whole animal and a pit crew.

How it is made

Season a pork shoulder with salt, garlic, and lemongrass and roast it until it is both safe and tender. Call a home oven an oven, not a Cebu spit.

Authentic touch

Salt the meat, not only the skin. A salty crust over bland pork is not the Cebu seasoning.

How it is used

Chop it and eat it with rice. A liver sauce can be served beside it.

Field notes

  • Cebu lechon is a whole spit-roasted pig. This page uses a pork shoulder at home.
  • The usual Cebu seasoning is salt and aromatics inside the meat, not a sweet glaze.

Ingredients

  • 1 boneless pork shoulder, about 4 pounds
  • 2 tablespoons salt
  • 8 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 3 stalks lemongrass, smashed
  • 4 scallions, whole
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Rub the pork with oil, salt, and pepper. Tuck garlic, lemongrass, and scallions into cuts in the meat. Refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The pork is still raw. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  2. Heat a home oven to 325°F (165°C). This is a home shoulder, not a whole Cebu spit pig. If you truly have a spit, you may use it and still check the temperature. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  3. Roast until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C). For meat you want to chop tender, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C). Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  4. Rest it, then chop. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A real spit and a whole pig are the feast. They are not required to cook this shoulder.
  • Spring onion inside the meat is common beside the lemongrass.

Pairings

  • rice
  • liver sauce
  • vinegar dip
