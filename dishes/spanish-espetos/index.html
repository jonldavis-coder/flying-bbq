Malaga Sardine Skewers

Next stop: Catalan Calcots · on Mediterranean by balloon, by Hot air balloon

Pronounced ess-PAY-tohs

Charred sardines, salt, olive oil, and smoke from a beach fire.

20 min prep · 8 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Espetos are sardines skewered and cooked over a beach fire in Malaga, especially along the old fishing beaches east of the city. The fish are threaded on a spit and set beside the flames, often stuck in the sand so they stand in the heat. Salt and the fire are the seasoning. It is a shore method that moved into beach bars without losing the skewer.

Cultural background

The cooks are Malaga's beach grillers and the households that copy them. The dish is sardines, not a mixed seafood paella. Coverage here is Spain.

Why it tastes this way

Sardines are small and oily, so a few minutes beside a wood fire crisps the skin and leaves the inside moist. Sea air and salt are already in the story of the catch. Olive oil after the grill shines the fish. A thick marinade would hide the point.

How it is made

Skewer fresh sardines, salt them, and grill them briefly over a wood fire until they flake.

Authentic touch

Thread the sardines through the belly so they stay flat, and keep them beside the fire rather than in the flame.

How it is used

Off the skewer, with bread, lemon, and a simple salad. Eat them hot.

Field notes

  • Malaga espetos are sardines on skewers cooked over a beach fire.
  • The traditional setup often plants the skewer in the sand beside the flames.

Ingredients

  • 12 fresh sardines, gutted
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 4 metal or bamboo skewers
  • 1 lemon

Steps

  1. Thread three sardines on each skewer through the belly so they lie flat. Salt them.
  2. Build a wood fire and let it settle to a hot bed of coals, as on a beach grill.
  3. Cook beside or just over the coals until the fish reach 145°F (63°C) and the flesh flakes. They need only a few minutes a side.
  4. Oil them lightly, add lemon, and eat at once. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A kettle grill at home with the skewers raised off the grate.
  • A squeeze of lemon after cooking, which is enough acid.

Pairings

  • Bread
  • Lemon
  • Tomato salad
