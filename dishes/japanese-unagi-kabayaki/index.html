Japanese Unagi Kabayaki

Next stop: Japanese Sanma Shioyaki · continuing the voyage

Pronounced oo-NAH-gee kah-bah-YAH-kee

Grilled eel with a sweet soy glaze, rich and lacquered.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Unagi no kabayaki is eel split open, grilled, and brushed with a sweet sauce of soy, mirin, and sugar. Eel shops in cities such as Hamamatsu specialize in it. The eel is fully cooked, not raw. Ordinary soy sauce contains wheat; this is a home grill of fillets, not a claim that a kitchen can copy a specialist shop's long-reduced sauce pot; the fish reaches 145°F (63°C).

Cultural background

Japanese eel restaurants grill kabayaki to order and serve it over rice. There is fish, soy, and wheat. There is no dairy. Buy eel from a fishmonger who has already filleted it if you do not have the skill to do that safely.

Why it tastes this way

Eel is fatty, so a sweet soy glaze clings and caramelizes instead of soaking in and disappearing. Mirin adds aroma and shine. The grill browns the sauce in a second pass, which is the kabayaki smell. Plain salted eel is a different dish. The sweetness is the point of this one.

How it is made

Grill eel fillets, brush them with a soy, mirin, and sugar sauce, and cook them until the flesh is opaque.

Authentic touch

Glaze in thin coats and grill between coats. One thick layer slides off and burns in the coals.

How it is used

Serve the eel over rice, with the extra sauce spooned on.

Field notes

  • Kabayaki is eel opened flat and grilled with a sweet soy sauce.
  • Hamamatsu is one of the Japanese cities known for eel shops.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds eel fillets
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce
  • 2 tablespoons mirin
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 tablespoon sake
  • 1 teaspoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Simmer the soy sauce, mirin, sugar, and sake for 3 minutes. The eel is still raw. Keep the sauce separate. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  2. Heat a grill and oil it lightly. Grill the eel, skin side first if the skin is on. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  3. Brush with sauce and continue until the center reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque. Brush once more at the end. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve over rice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A shop sauce reduced for years is their pot. This sauce is made the day you cook.
  • Sansho pepper on the side is a usual fragrance and is not in the glaze.

Pairings

  • rice
  • pickled ginger
  • sansho
