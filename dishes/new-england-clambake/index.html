New England Shore Clambake

Next stop: Maine Grilled Lobster · continuing the voyage

Steamed clams and lobster with sweet corn, salty and rich with butter.

30 min prep · 45 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Wampanoag and other Native communities along this coast have long harvested clams and other shellfish. The later New England shore bake, layered with seaweed, corn, and lobster, is a regional feast on that same coast. It is not a reconstruction of a 1621 menu. The recipe below is a covered pot for a home kitchen, labeled as such, not a beach pit.

Cultural background

A home covered-pot version of a New England clambake. It is not a beach pit and not a claim about a 1621 table.

Why it tastes this way

Clams and lobster steam in their own liquid, and corn and potatoes take on that brine. Butter is the later shore-bake habit at the table. Seaweed in a real pit adds a marine smell a covered pot only partly suggests, which is why this page names the pot honestly.

How it is made

Steam clams, lobster, corn, and potatoes in a covered pot, as a home version of a shore bake.

Authentic touch

Call this a covered pot. If you ever do a shore pit, that is a different project with permits, tides, and a crew.

How it is used

On a table with bowls for shells, melted butter, and empty plates for corn cobs. Butter contains dairy.

Field notes

  • Wampanoag and neighboring Native communities used clams long before the later New England shore bake took its summer-feast shape.
  • A covered pot at home is not the same vessel as a shore pit lined with seaweed.

Ingredients

  • 4 pounds live clams, scrubbed
  • 2 live lobsters, about 1 1/4 pounds each
  • 6 ears corn, husked
  • 1 1/2 pounds small potatoes
  • 2 cups water
  • 4 tablespoons butter

Steps

  1. Discard any clam that is cracked or that stays open when tapped. Keep shellfish cold. This is a covered pot, not a seaweed pit on a beach.
  2. Put potatoes and water in a large pot, then corn, then lobsters and clams. Cover tightly.
  3. Steam until the potatoes are tender, the clams open, and the lobster tail meat is opaque and reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Discard clams that stayed shut. Melt the butter and serve it beside the shellfish, not as a claim that the pot was an earth oven.

Cook lobster until the flesh is opaque and 145°F (63°C). Clams should open; discard any that stay shut. This covered pot is not a beach-pit clambake.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Mussels can join the clams if they are fresh. Discard any shellfish that stay shut.

Pairings

  • corn
  • potatoes
