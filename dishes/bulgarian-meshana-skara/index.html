Bulgarian Meshana Skara

Next stop: Uzbek Lamb Shashlik · continuing the voyage

Pronounced meh-SHAH-nah SKAH-rah

Pork chops and skinless minced sausages with cumin, black pepper, and grill smoke.

30 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Meshana skara means mixed grill in Bulgarian. A plate may hold pork chops, kebapche sausages, and sometimes other cuts, cooked over charcoal and served with salad and fries. This home version keeps two items: a pork chop and a skinless minced sausage, each taken to its own temperature. It is restaurant and home food in Sofia and in smaller towns. No single shop invented the idea of putting several grilled meats on one plate.

Cultural background

Bulgarian mehana grills and family cookouts serve this as a shared plate. It is not one recipe with one meat. The minced sausage and the chop are cooked differently.

Why it tastes this way

Cumin and black pepper season the mince, while the chop tastes of browned pork fat and salt. Charcoal is the common fuel and gives both items a similar smoke so the plate feels like one meal. The contrast is the point of a mixed grill: you taste more than one texture from the same fire.

How it is made

Grill pork chops and skinless minced sausages together, and check each with a thermometer.

Authentic touch

Shape the kebapche smaller than the chop is thick, and do not salt the mince so hard that it dries before it browns.

How it is used

With a salad of tomato, cucumber, and onion. The salad in this recipe has no dairy.

Field notes

  • Meshana skara means mixed grill in Bulgarian.
  • A chop and a minced sausage on the same plate are cooked to different temperatures.

Ingredients

  • 4 boneless pork chops, about 3/4 inch thick
  • 1 lb ground pork
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Mix the ground pork with cumin, garlic, and 1 teaspoon salt. Form eight small sausages. Oil and salt the chops.
  2. Grill the sausages first over medium-hot coals, then add the chops.
  3. Cook the ground sausages to 160°F (71°C). Cook the pork chops to at least 145°F (63°C) and give them a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve together. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chicken pieces added only if they reach 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part.
  • All pork, as here, when you want a simpler fire.

Pairings

  • Tomato and cucumber salad
  • Fries
  • Raw onion
