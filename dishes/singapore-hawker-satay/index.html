Singapore Hawker Satay

Next stop: Cambodian Sach Ko Ang · continuing the voyage

Pronounced sah-tay

Chicken skewers with turmeric and a peanut sauce, from a hawker stall rather than a roadside shed.

35 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Singapore hawker satay is grilled meat on skewers sold in hawker centers, often by Malay cooks, with a peanut sauce, cucumber, and rice cakes on the side. It shares a family with Malaysian satay and it is not the same stall: a hawker-center satay in Singapore is a city cooked-food-center plate, not a Kajang shop. Peanut in the sauce means the dish is not nut-free. The chicken is cooked through.

Cultural background

Hawker cooks work in public cooked-food centers with many stalls side by side. This recipe is the chicken satay and its peanut sauce. There is no dairy. The peanut is the allergen that keeps it off a nut-free table.

Why it tastes this way

Turmeric, cumin, and lemongrass season small pieces of chicken, and a hot grate browns them quickly. The peanut sauce is sweet, spicy, and rich, which is the spoonful that defines the plate. Eating it at a hawker table, with the sauce in a small bowl, is the Singapore setting. The spice is close to Malay satay because the cooks who built those stalls cooked in that tradition.

How it is made

Marinate chicken, grill it on skewers to a poultry temperature, and serve a separate peanut sauce.

Authentic touch

Serve the sauce beside the skewers, not cooked onto them from the start. Early peanut sauce burns.

How it is used

Eat the skewers with the sauce, cucumber, and onion.

Field notes

  • Singapore hawker satay is a cooked-food-center plate. It is not a Kajang satay shop.
  • Peanut sauce means the dish is not nut-free.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds boneless chicken thighs, cut in small pieces
  • 1 stalk lemongrass, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1/2 cup roasted peanuts, ground
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 hot chili, minced
  • 1/2 cup coconut milk

Steps

  1. Toss the chicken with lemongrass, turmeric, cumin, salt, and oil. Refrigerate it. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Simmer the peanuts, sugar, chili, and coconut milk with a pinch of salt until thick. This is a hawker-style sauce, served on the side. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Skewer the chicken and grill until the center reaches 165°F (74°C). Do not serve it rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Serve with the peanut sauce. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Beef and mutton satay are sold at the same stalls and need their own temperatures.
  • Ketupat, a rice cake, is a common side when you can get it.

Pairings

  • onion
  • rice
