Egyptian Kofta

Next stop: Nigerian Suya · continuing the voyage

Pronounced KOF-tah

Minced beef and lamb with parsley, onion, and a grilled crust.

25 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Egyptian kofta is grilled minced beef or lamb, mixed with parsley, onion, and spices, and cooked over charcoal in Cairo and elsewhere. It is a different plate from Moroccan kefta brochettes, which have their own spice and skewer habit. The Egyptian mix here leans on parsley, cumin, and coriander. The meat is ground and cooked through. It is street and home food, not a festival invention.

Cultural background

Egyptian households and grill shops cook kofta as ordinary meat. North Africa is the region for this page. It is not raw kibbeh and not a meatball in tomato sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Parsley and onion cut the fat of a beef-and-lamb mince, and cumin and coriander toast on the grill. Charcoal gives a bitter-brown edge that pan frying only approximates. The blend is familiar across Egyptian grills and still not the same as a Moroccan brochette heavy on paprika. Ground meat needs a cooked center, which sets the texture.

How it is made

Mix minced beef and lamb with herbs and spices, form sausages or patties, and grill them until the center is cooked.

Authentic touch

Grate and squeeze the onion. If the mix is wet, the kofta will slump through the grate.

How it is used

With salad, tahini sauce if you eat sesame, and rice. Tahini is not in this recipe, so sesame is not an allergen here.

Field notes

  • Egyptian kofta is grilled minced beef or lamb with parsley and onion.
  • It is a different plate from Moroccan kefta brochettes.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ground beef
  • 1/2 lb ground lamb
  • 1 small onion, grated and squeezed dry
  • 1 cup parsley, finely chopped
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Mix the beef, lamb, onion, parsley, cumin, coriander, and salt until the meat holds together.
  2. Form eight short sausages. Heat a grill to medium-hot.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C).
  4. Serve with salad. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • All beef or all lamb, still ground.
  • On skewers, which is a shape change, not a different spice mix.

Pairings

  • Salad
  • Rice
  • Tomato
