Nigerian Asun

Next stop: Ghanaian Kyinkyinga · continuing the voyage

Pronounced ah-SOON

Chopped grilled goat with ginger, garlic, and a heavy pepper heat.

25 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Asun is spicy grilled goat associated with Yoruba parties in southwestern Nigeria. The meat is grilled, chopped, and tossed with pepper, onion, and ginger so the spice coats every piece. Ibadan and Lagos parties both claim it as a tray that disappears fast. It is party food, not a quiet weekday soup. The goat is a whole-muscle cut cooked to a safe temperature before it is chopped.

Cultural background

Yoruba hosts and the cooks they hire for celebrations make asun. It is goat, not beef suya, though both can show up at a large party. The pepper is the point.

Why it tastes this way

Goat fat carries ginger and chili better than a lean chop does. Grilling first gives a browned edge, and chopping lets the raw-heat of fresh pepper sit on the hot meat without a long stew. That two-step is why asun tastes both smoky and freshly hot. It belongs to a crowded table.

How it is made

Season goat, grill it, rest it, chop it, and toss it with onion, ginger, and chili.

Authentic touch

Grill the goat far enough that the center is safe, then chop it and toss it with pepper off the fire so the fresh chili stays bright.

How it is used

In a pile, with more onion, at a party. Plantain is a common side and is not fried in this recipe.

Field notes

  • Asun is spicy grilled goat associated with Yoruba parties in southwestern Nigeria.
  • The meat is grilled and then chopped and peppered, which is why it is party food rather than a single chop on a plate.

Ingredients

  • 3 lb bone-in goat shoulder or leg, in large pieces
  • 1 tablespoon ground ginger
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 3 fresh hot chiles, chopped
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Rub the goat with ginger, garlic, salt, and oil. Heat a grill for medium-high cooking.
  2. Grill, turning, until the meat is browned.
  3. Cook whole goat to at least 145°F (63°C) in the thickest part, away from bone, and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Chop the meat, toss it with onion and fresh chile, and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A milder pepper level, still goat.
  • Bone-in pieces served whole if you would rather not chop them.

Pairings

  • Sliced onion
  • Tomato
  • Plantain
