Cape Malay Sosaties

Next stop: Shisa Nyama · continuing the voyage

Pronounced soh-SAH-tees

Lamb skewers with apricot, cumin, coriander, and a little chili.

40 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · 6 skewers

History

Sosaties are Cape Town lamb skewers marinated with apricot and warm spice. Cape Malay cooking was built by enslaved and exiled people from the Indian Ocean world who were brought to the Cape under the Dutch East India Company, including people from Southeast Asia and South Asia, and by their descendants. They were not one people with one recipe, and this skewer should not be told as a single stereotype. The name is widely connected to sate. The meat here is lamb, a whole-muscle cut.

Cultural background

Cape Town families who keep Cape Malay foodways grill sosaties for gatherings and for the table at home. The history is forced movement and skill under the Dutch East India Company, not a costume. It is not a generic curry and not Nigerian suya.

Why it tastes this way

Apricot jam sweetens the edge of the lamb, and cumin, coriander, and chili toast on the grill. That sweet-spice balance is a Cape kitchen pattern built through Indian Ocean trade and through the cooking of people who were enslaved and exiled at the Cape. Charcoal browns the cubes. The fruit and the spice are doing different jobs, which is why the skewer is not just hot and not just sweet.

How it is made

Marinate lamb in apricot and spice, skewer it with onion, and grill until the cubes are safe, then rest.

Authentic touch

Thread onion between the lamb cubes. Wipe off thick marinade so the apricot does not burn black before the meat is done.

How it is used

With rice and a salad. Yellow rice is a common Cape side and is not required for the meat to be finished.

Field notes

  • Sosaties are a Cape Town skewer, and the name is widely connected to sate.
  • Cape Malay cooking comes from enslaved and exiled people of the Indian Ocean world at the Cape, including people from Southeast Asia and South Asia, not from one stereotype.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb boneless lamb, cut in 1 1/2 inch cubes
  • 2 onions, cut in chunks
  • 3 tablespoons apricot jam
  • 2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 2 teaspoons ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon chili flakes
  • 2 tablespoons vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 6 metal skewers

Steps

  1. Mix the jam, cumin, coriander, chili, vinegar, and salt. Toss with the lamb and one chopped onion. Refrigerate for a few hours if you can.
  2. Thread lamb and onion chunks on skewers. Heat a braai to medium-hot.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest cube reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Give the meat a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with rice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A firmer apricot jam or a few soaked dried apricots chopped into the marinade.
  • Beef cubes with the same marinade, still a whole-muscle cut.

Pairings

  • Rice
  • Cucumber salad
  • Grilled onion
