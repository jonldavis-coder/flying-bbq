Huli Huli Chicken

Next stop: Kalua Pig · continuing the voyage

Pronounced HOO-lee HOO-lee

Sweet-salty grilled chicken with soy, pineapple, and ginger.

20 min prep · 1 hr 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Ernest Morgado is the person Hawaii credits with huli huli chicken in the 1950s, chickens turned over a fire and brushed with a teriyaki-like sauce. Huli means turn. The sauce uses soy sauce, so this plate contains soy and wheat and is not vegan. It is a twentieth-century Hawaiian grill, not a kalua pig and not an imu.

Cultural background

Rotisserie or grill chicken with a soy and pineapple baste. A Hawaiian plate tied to Morgado's 1950s chicken, separate from kalua pork.

Why it tastes this way

Soy, pineapple, and ginger make a salty-sweet glaze that caramelizes when the bird turns over charcoal. Turning, which the name names, keeps one side from burning while the sugar browns. Kiawe charcoal is a common Hawaiian grill fuel if you can get it; ordinary charcoal still cooks the chicken.

How it is made

Baste chicken with a soy, pineapple, and ginger sauce and cook it on a rotisserie or a covered grill, turning it.

Authentic touch

Turn the chicken often and move it off the highest flame once the glaze goes on. The sugar in soy and pineapple will scorch.

How it is used

Jointed, with rice. Rice is the usual starch. The soy sauce already makes the dish not gluten-free.

Field notes

  • Huli means turn, which is what the cook does to the chickens.
  • Ernest Morgado is credited in Hawaii with this chicken in the 1950s.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 4 pounds, halved
  • 1/2 cup soy sauce
  • 1/2 cup pineapple juice
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar
  • 2 tablespoons ketchup
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar

Steps

  1. Stir soy sauce, pineapple juice, brown sugar, ketchup, ginger, garlic, and vinegar. Soy sauce brings soy and wheat. Reserve some sauce that has not touched raw chicken for a final brush.
  2. Salt is already in the soy. Put the chicken on a rotisserie or over indirect grill heat. Turn it often.
  3. Baste with the portion that touched the raw chicken only while the bird is still cooking. Cook until the thigh, away from bone, reaches 165°F (74°C).
  4. Brush with the clean reserved sauce in the last minutes, rest, and serve. Do not serve the chicken rare.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Halves on a grill, turned often, are the home stand-in for a long rotisserie.

Pairings

  • rice
  • macaroni salad
  • cabbage
