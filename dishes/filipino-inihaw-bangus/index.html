Filipino Inihaw Bangus

Next stop: Filipino Isaw · continuing the voyage

Pronounced ee-nee-HOW BAHNG-oos

Grilled milkfish stuffed with tomato, onion, and calamansi.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Inihaw na bangus is grilled milkfish, a Filipino fish often stuffed with tomato, onion, and calamansi before it meets the charcoal. Dagupan, in Pangasinan, is a city tied to bangus farming and to this fish on the grill. The flesh is cooked until it flakes. This stuffing has no soy sauce, so the written fish has no wheat; it is a grill, not a fried bangus.

Cultural background

Pangasinan cooks and home grills across the Philippines stuff and grill milkfish. There is fish, and no dairy and no wheat in this recipe. Tomato and onion go inside the cavity. The skin needs a hot grate and a careful turn.

Why it tastes this way

Milkfish is rich, and tomato, onion, and calamansi steam inside the cavity and cut that richness. The grill chars the skin. A stuffing that is only salt would miss the fresh contrast Filipino cooks put in the fish.

How it is made

Stuff a cleaned milkfish with tomato, onion, and calamansi, then grill it until the center flakes.

Authentic touch

Dry the skin well before it hits the grate. Wet skin sticks and tears, and the stuffing falls out.

How it is used

Eat it with rice and a vinegar dip. Watch for bones. Milkfish has many.

Field notes

  • Bangus is milkfish. Dagupan in Pangasinan is closely tied to the fish.
  • The usual grill stuffing is tomato, onion, and a sour citrus.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole milkfish, about 2 pounds, cleaned and scaled
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 3 tablespoons calamansi juice, or lime juice
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Dry the fish. Fill the cavity with tomato, onion, calamansi juice, salt, and pepper. Rub the skin with oil. The fish is still raw. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  2. Heat a grill and oil the grate. Tie the cavity closed with a skewer or kitchen string if it gapes. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  3. Grill, turning once, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve hot, and mind the bones. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of soy sauce in the stuffing is common and would add soy and wheat. This stuffing has none.
  • Fillets work if a whole fish is not sold. Cook them to the same fish temperature.

Pairings
