Korean Grilled Mackerel

Next stop: Korean Gopchang · continuing the voyage

Pronounced goh-DOONG-uh gwee

Oily mackerel, salted and grilled until the skin blisters and the flesh flakes.

15 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Godeungeo-gui is Korean grilled mackerel, a common fish on coastal tables and in homes. Busan, a port city, lands a great deal of it. The fish is salted and grilled, sometimes after a short cure in salt, and it is eaten with rice and a sharp banchan. This recipe salts it the same day and grills it; it is not a dried fish and not a curry; the flesh is cooked until it flakes.

Cultural background

Korean cooks grill mackerel because the fish is oily enough to stand up to high heat. There is fish, salt, and a little oil. There is no wheat, dairy, or sesame in this written fish. Serve kimchi beside it if you want the sour contrast.

Why it tastes this way

Mackerel fat crisps under the skin on a hot grate, and salt is enough to season an oily fish. A light char adds bitterness that keeps the richness in check. Rice and a fermented vegetable on the side do the rest. A heavy sauce would cover the point of a simple salt grill.

How it is made

Salt mackerel and grill it until the center is opaque and flakes.

Authentic touch

Score the skin so the thick part cooks before the tail burns. Oil the grate. Mackerel sticks.

How it is used

Eat it with rice, a lemon or a grill lemon is less usual than a Korean table of kimchi, so put kimchi beside it.

Field notes

  • Godeungeo-gui is salted grilled mackerel, a regular Korean fish.
  • Busan is a port city where this fish is part of the local catch.

Ingredients

  • 4 mackerel fillets, or 2 small whole mackerel, split open
  • 1.5 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 lemon, cut in wedges

Steps

  1. Pat the fish dry, rub it with salt and oil, and let it stand in the refrigerator for 15 minutes. The fish is still raw. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  2. Heat a grill and oil the grate well. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  3. Grill, turning once, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve hot with rice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook fish to 145°F (63°C) until it is opaque and flakes.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A light sprinkle of sesame oil after grilling would add sesame. It is not in this recipe.
  • Fillets cook faster than a butterflied whole fish. Use the thermometer either way.

Pairings

  • rice
  • kimchi
  • lettuce
