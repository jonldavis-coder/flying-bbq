Peruvian Anticuchos

Next stop: Peruvian Pollo a la Brasa · continuing the voyage

Pronounced ahn-tee-KOO-chos

Beef heart skewers with vinegar, garlic, and a dried red chile.

30 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Anticuchos are beef-heart skewers. Enslaved Africans in coastal Peru, and the Afro-Peruvian cooks who followed them, turned this offal into a skilled street food, and Lima still grills it at night. Aji panca, a dried Peruvian red chile, is the usual pepper. Another dried red chile can be used if you say it is a substitute and not aji panca. The heart is whole muscle and is cooked to at least 145°F (63°C).

Cultural background

This is offal, treated as a regular cook. Vinegar, garlic, cumin, and the dried chile are the marinade. The skewers are street food, eaten with potato and aji.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar and aji panca taste sharp and dried-chile sweet, which is how Afro-Peruvian cooks seasoned beef heart for the grill. Charcoal browns the outside while the heart stays a whole muscle. The flavor is that marinade and that fire, tied to a history of labor, not to a garnish.

How it is made

Marinate beef heart in aji panca, vinegar, and garlic, then grill it on skewers to an offal temperature.

Authentic touch

Cut the heart across the grain into even cubes so the skewers finish together and stay tender enough to chew.

How it is used

Eat the skewers with boiled potato and a spoon of the same chile sauce.

Field notes

  • Anticuchos are beef-heart skewers associated with Afro-Peruvian cooking.
  • Aji panca is a dried Peruvian red chile. Another dried red chile is a substitute and not the same pepper.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds beef heart, trimmed and cubed
  • 3 tablespoons aji panca paste, or 2 tablespoons ground dried red chile named as a substitute
  • 1/4 cup red wine vinegar
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • Metal skewers

Steps

  1. Mix the chile, vinegar, garlic, cumin, salt, and oil. Coat the heart. It is raw and is not ready to eat. Refrigerate it if it waits. This offal is not ready to eat until it reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and rests 3 minutes.
  2. Thread the cubes onto skewers, leaving a little space between them. This offal is not ready to eat until it reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and rests 3 minutes.
  3. Grill over hot coals, turning, until the center of a cube reads at least 145°F (63°C). Rest the skewers 3 minutes.
  4. The heart should have reached at least 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Serve it hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook beef offal to at least 145°F (63°C) with a rest. Cook poultry offal, including chicken hearts and livers, to 165°F (74°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • If aji panca is not sold, use 2 tablespoons of another ground dried red chile and name it.
  • A hotter aji can be served on the side. The skewer in this recipe stays mild.

Pairings

  • boiled potatoes
  • aji sauce
  • corn
