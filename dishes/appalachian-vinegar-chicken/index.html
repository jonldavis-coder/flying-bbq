Appalachian Vinegar Chicken

Next stop: Virginia Tomato-Vinegar Pork · continuing the voyage

Grilled half chickens, sharp with vinegar and black pepper.

20 min prep · 55 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Half chickens with a vinegar mop are a mountain cookout habit in the southern Appalachians, kin to the region's vinegar-and-pepper seasoning for pork but cooked as poultry. This mop has no egg and no mayonnaise. It is not Cornell chicken and not Alabama white sauce.

Cultural background

Grilled half chickens basted with vinegar, salt, and pepper. A mountain plate, not a campus baste and not a Decatur sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar and pepper cut chicken fat the way they cut hog fat in Carolina pits, and the mountains had vinegar on hand as a preserve and a seasoning. Direct and indirect heat together brown the skin without burning the mop. There is no sugar to scorch.

How it is made

Grill half chickens over coals and baste with a vinegar, salt, and pepper mop.

Authentic touch

Mop over a slightly cooler part of the grill. Vinegar on a roaring flame mostly evaporates and does not season the meat.

How it is used

Half a chicken on the plate, with more mop if you want it sharper.

Field notes

  • This mop is vinegar, salt, and pepper, with no egg and no mayonnaise.
  • It is an Appalachian cookout chicken, distinct from Cornell's egg baste and Alabama's white sauce.

Ingredients

  • 2 chickens, halved, about 4 pounds each
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt
  • 1 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt, for the mop
  • 1 teaspoon crushed red pepper

Steps

  1. Salt the chicken halves. Stir vinegar, water, pepper, mop salt, and red pepper.
  2. Heat a grill with a hot side and a cooler side.
  3. Cook the chickens mostly on the cooler side, mopping now and then, until the thickest part of the thigh, not touching bone, reaches 165°F (74°C).
  4. Rest a few minutes. Do not serve the chicken rare, and discard mop that sat against raw chicken unless you boil it.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pinch more cayenne makes the mop hotter. The base stays vinegar.

Pairings
