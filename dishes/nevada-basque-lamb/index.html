Nevada Basque Lamb Chops

Next stop: Colorado Ranch Lamb Chops · continuing the voyage

Lamb chops with garlic, salt, and a hot-grill crust.

15 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Basque immigrants worked as sheepherders in Nevada through the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and lamb stayed on the table in towns such as Elko. These chops are a simple grill: salt, pepper, and garlic. This page does not invent a festival and does not claim a single boarding house wrote the recipe.

Cultural background

Grilled lamb chops in the Basque American sheep-camp and boarding-house orbit of northern Nevada. A home grill, not a invented fair.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb from that sheep economy is the ingredient, and garlic and salt are enough because the fat browns on a hot fire. The Great Basin's sheep camps are why Basque cooking is part of rural Nevada rather than only an imported restaurant theme. A short grill keeps the chop pink at the safety line used here.

How it is made

Rub lamb chops with garlic, salt, and pepper and grill them over a hot fire.

Authentic touch

Pull chops at 145°F and rest them. Lamb fat flares, so keep a cooler side of the grill.

How it is used

Whole chops, with beans or a salad. No special sauce is required.

Field notes

  • Basque sheepherders worked flocks in Nevada in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
  • Elko is one of the northern Nevada towns where that lamb table is still part of local cooking.

Ingredients

  • 8 lamb rib or loin chops
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Rub the chops with garlic, oil, salt, and pepper. Keep them cold until the grill is hot, and cook them the same day.
  2. Heat the grill hot and leave a cooler zone for flare-ups.
  3. Grill, turning once, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes. Serve without a sweet glaze.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A small roast uses the same seasoning and must still reach 145°F with a 3-minute rest, with a longer cook.

Pairings

  • beans
  • green salad
  • bread
