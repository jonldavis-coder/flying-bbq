Kansas City Spareribs

Next stop: Alabama White-Sauce Chicken · continuing the voyage

Pork spareribs with a thick, sweet, smoky sauce lacquered on at the end.

25 min prep · 5 hr 30 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Kansas City ribs are pork spareribs smoked and then painted with a thick, sweet sauce. The sauce is the city's signature, thicker and more molasses-like than a Carolina dip. Henry Perry is the early seller local history credits, and later kitchens built the sweet sauce associated with the city. These are not Memphis dry ribs.

Cultural background

Spareribs with sauce at the end. The sauce goes on late so the bark exists underneath it.

Why it tastes this way

A long smoke sets pepper and paprika on the rib, and the thick sauce adds molasses sweetness and tomato acid only after the meat is tender. Putting it on at the end keeps the sugar from burning through a five-hour cook. The result is sticky, which is the Kansas City idea.

How it is made

Smoke rubbed spareribs, then glaze them at the end with a thick sweet sauce.

Authentic touch

Sauce in the last 20 to 30 minutes, then again at the table. Early saucing makes a bitter sugar crust.

How it is used

By the slab or half slab, with bread and pickles. Extra sauce on the side.

Field notes

  • Kansas City spareribs take a thick sweet sauce at the end, unlike Memphis dry ribs.
  • Molasses, not honey, is the dark sweetener in this sauce.

Ingredients

  • 2 racks pork spareribs
  • 2 tablespoons kosher salt
  • 2 tablespoons paprika
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • 1 cup ketchup
  • 1/4 cup molasses
  • 2 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 tablespoons apple cider vinegar

Steps

  1. Rub the ribs with salt, paprika, pepper, and brown sugar. Smoke indirectly at about 250°F (121°C).
  2. Simmer ketchup, molasses, Worcestershire, and vinegar until slightly thick. Ordinary Worcestershire contains anchovy and usually wheat, so this rib is not gluten-free and is not vegetarian.
  3. Cook the ribs until they reach about 195–203°F (90–95°C) between the bones. Pork ribs are safe at 145°F (63°C) after a 3-minute rest; these stay on until tender.
  4. Brush with sauce in the last 20 minutes and serve the rest on the side.

Pork ribs are safe at 145°F (63°C) after a 3-minute rest. For tender ribs, cook until the meat has pulled back from the ends of the bones, often around 195–203°F (90–95°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • St. Louis-cut spareribs are the same idea with the rib tips squared off.

Pairings

  • white bread
  • pickles
  • baked beans
