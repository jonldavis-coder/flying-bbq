Thai Moo Ping

Next stop: Thai Kor Moo Yang · continuing the voyage

Pronounced moo ping

Sweet, salty pork skewers with garlic, white pepper, and a little coconut milk.

30 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Moo ping is a Thai street skewer of pork, sold from small grills in Bangkok and other cities. The marinade is sweet and salty, with garlic, white pepper, fish sauce or salt, sugar, and often a spoon of coconut milk so the edges brown. The pork is cut in thin pieces, which makes it a whole-muscle skewer rather than a sausage. It is everyday street food, cooked to order.

Cultural background

Street cooks thread the pork and grill it in front of you. Coconut milk is not a dairy milk. Fish sauce, if used, contains fish. This recipe uses fish sauce. There is no wheat and no peanut in the marinade.

Why it tastes this way

Sugar and coconut milk caramelize on a hot grate, and fish sauce salts the pork. White pepper and garlic stay sharp under that sweetness. Thin slices cook before the sugar turns black, which is why the skewers are small.

How it is made

Marinate thin pork in coconut milk, sugar, garlic, and fish sauce, then grill it on skewers.

Authentic touch

Keep the pieces thin and do not let the sugar flame up. A black skewer tastes bitter.

How it is used

Eat the skewers hot, with sticky rice or a sour chili dip.

Field notes

  • Moo ping is a sweet pork skewer sold from small street grills in Thailand.
  • Coconut milk helps the edges brown. It is not dairy.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds pork shoulder, sliced thin
  • 1/4 cup coconut milk
  • 1 tablespoon palm sugar
  • 2 tablespoons fish sauce
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon white pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Mix the coconut milk, palm sugar, fish sauce, garlic, white pepper, and oil. Coat the pork and refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The pork is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Thread the slices on small skewers. Heat a grill until it is hot. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning often, until the center of the thickest slice reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the pork 3 minutes. Serve hot and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Palm sugar and white sugar are both used. This recipe uses palm sugar.
  • A chili dip on the side adds heat. The skewer itself is mild.

Pairings

  • sticky rice
  • cucumber
  • chili dip
