Upper Peninsula Venison Steak

Next stop: Santa Maria Tri-Tip · continuing the voyage

Lean venison with salt, pepper, and a hot-grill crust.

10 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Deer is a major food in Michigan's Upper Peninsula, and a steak on a hot grill is a common way to cook a hindquarter. Wild venison can carry parasites, so some cooks take it to 160°F even though a whole-muscle steak is otherwise guided to 145°F and a rest. This page gives both. It is not a sausage cure and not a claim about a particular tribal hunt.

Cultural background

Grilled venison steak. Give wild meat the extra temperature caution. Farmed venison from a inspected seller is a different risk from a deer you shot.

Why it tastes this way

Venison is lean, with a flavor stronger than beef, so salt, pepper, and a short sear are the whole cook. A long stay on the grill dries it. Northern woods and a cold season are why the meat is on Upper Peninsula tables in the fall.

How it is made

Grill venison steaks quickly and choose 145°F with a rest, or 160°F for wild meat.

Authentic touch

If the venison is wild, consider 160°F. If it is inspected farmed meat and you accept the 145°F rest, say which one you cooked.

How it is used

Sliced against the grain. A sharp jelly is common and is not in this ingredient list.

Field notes

  • Wild venison can carry parasites, which is why some cooks take it to 160°F.
  • A whole-muscle steak is otherwise guided to 145°F and a 3-minute rest.

Ingredients

  • 4 venison steaks, about 1 inch thick
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Oil, salt, and pepper the steaks. Heat the grill hot.
  2. Grill, turning once. Venison dries out if it stays over the fire too long.
  3. For inspected farmed steaks, cook to 145°F (63°C) and rest at least 3 minutes. For wild venison, some cooks continue to 160°F (71°C) because of parasite risk.
  4. Slice against the grain and serve.

Cook whole-muscle venison to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. Wild venison can carry parasites, so some cooks take it to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Backstrap medallions use the same choice of temperatures and cook even faster.

Pairings

  • potatoes
  • cabbage
  • onion
