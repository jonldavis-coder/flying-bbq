Australian Damper

Next stop: Torres Strait Kup Murri · continuing the voyage

A plain wheat loaf, crusty outside and dense inside, with salt and the smell of the fire.

History

Damper is a settler campfire bread of wheat flour, water, and salt, baked in the coals or in a camp oven by stockmen and colonial travelers. Aboriginal Australians milled native seeds and cooked their own breads long before wheat arrived. This recipe is the settler loaf. It is not an Aboriginal ceremony, and it is not a native-seed bread; Ballarat and other inland camps are one historical setting for flour-and-water bread on a journey; the loaf contains wheat.

Cultural background

Settler cooks mixed this dough when yeast and an oven were not at hand. There is wheat, salt, and water. There is no dairy, egg, or meat, so the written loaf is vegan. It is camp bread, not a ritual. Do not present it as Aboriginal food.

Why it tastes this way

Wheat flour and salt, cooked against heat, make a dense crumb and a hard crust. A campfire dries the outside faster than a kitchen oven and can leave a little ash and smoke. That plainness is the point of a ration bread. Native-seed breads have their own flavors and their own makers. This one tastes of wheat and fire because that is what the settlers carried.

How it is made

Mix flour, salt, and water, shape a round, and bake it in a camp oven or a home oven until the center is cooked.

Authentic touch

Cook it through. A handsome crust can hide a wet center of raw dough. Break one open and look.

How it is used

Tear it and eat it warm with grilled meat or with nothing but salt.

Field notes

  • Damper is a settler campfire bread of wheat flour, water, and salt.
  • It is not an Aboriginal ceremony. Aboriginal Australians have their own breads from native seeds.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups wheat flour, plus more for the bench
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 cup water, plus more if the dough is dry

Steps

  1. Stir the flour, salt, and baking powder. Add the water and mix a soft dough. There is no meat in this bread.
  2. Shape a round loaf and dust it with flour. Heat a camp oven by a fire, or heat a home oven to 400°F (200°C) and call it an oven.
  3. Bake until the crust is firm and the center is no longer wet dough, about 30 minutes. If you used a camp oven, keep the coals around it, not only under it, so the bottom does not burn.
  4. Cool it a few minutes, then break it open. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours if the weather is hot. This is not a canning recipe.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A camp oven in the coals is the older method. A home oven is the kitchen method and should be named as an oven.
  • A spoon of dairy fat would change the loaf. This one uses water.

Pairings
