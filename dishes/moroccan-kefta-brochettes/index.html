Moroccan Kefta Brochettes

Next stop: South African Pap · continuing the voyage

Pronounced KEF-tah broh-SHET

Minced lamb with cumin, paprika, and parsley, grilled on a stick.

25 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 skewers

History

Moroccan kefta brochettes are spiced minced lamb grilled on skewers in Fez, Marrakesh, and at home. They stay distinct from Egyptian kofta: this mix leads with cumin and paprika, and it is served as a brochette. The Egyptian plate on its own page leans on parsley, coriander, and a beef-and-lamb mix. Both are grilled mince. This one is ground and cooked through. It is not mechoui, the celebration roast.

Cultural background

Moroccan street and home grillers make kefta as a quick skewer. North Africa is the region. It is not a lamb shoulder and not the Egyptian kofta recipe copied under another name.

Why it tastes this way

Paprika and cumin toast on the outside of a small mince skewer, and parsley keeps it from tasting only of spice. Charcoal dries the surface. The brochette shape means more crust than a thick patty. That crust, plus the spice balance, is what separates it from the Egyptian mix even though both are called by related names.

How it is made

Mix minced lamb with cumin and paprika, mold it onto skewers, and grill until the center is cooked.

Authentic touch

Keep the mince cold and the onion squeezed dry so the brochettes do not slip off the skewers.

How it is used

With salad and cumin on the side. It is a skewer, not the whole roast.

Field notes

  • Moroccan kefta brochettes are spiced minced lamb on skewers.
  • They are distinct from Egyptian kofta in the spice and in the brochette habit.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb ground lamb
  • 1 small onion, grated and squeezed dry
  • 2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 2 teaspoons paprika
  • 1/2 cup parsley, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 4 metal skewers

Steps

  1. Mix the lamb, onion, cumin, paprika, parsley, and salt. Chill 15 minutes.
  2. Mold onto skewers. Heat the grill to medium-hot.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C). Ground lamb is cooked through.
  4. Serve with salad. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little beef in the mince, still ground.
  • Patties on the grill if you have no skewers, cooked to the same temperature.

Pairings

  • Tomato salad
  • Cumin
  • Onion
