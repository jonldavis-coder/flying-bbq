Fijian Lovo

Next stop: Samoan Umu · on Pacific earth ovens by boat, by Boat

Pronounced LOH-voh

Chicken steamed with coconut, garlic, and sweet potato, mild and rich.

30 min prep · 1 hr 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

A lovo is a Fijian earth oven, a pit of heated stones used for feasts, with meat, root vegetables, and sometimes a coconut parcel in leaves. This page is a home covered pot, not a full lovo and not a ceremony. Chicken, canned coconut milk, spinach in place of leaves that may not be sold, and sweet potato are the foods. There is no raw cassava; the chicken is cooked to a poultry temperature.

Cultural background

Fijian cooks prepare a lovo for community meals. The home pot borrows the coconut and the closed steam. There is chicken and coconut. Coconut milk is not dairy. There is no wheat and no peanut. Name the pot as a pot.

Why it tastes this way

Coconut milk and closed steam make the chicken rich, and sweet potato soaks up the juice. Garlic and a little chili perfume the pot. In a lovo the leaves and the stones add their own smell. At home the coconut and the chicken are the tastes you can actually make, without pretending the kitchen floor is a pit.

How it is made

Cook chicken, sweet potato, spinach, and canned coconut milk in a covered pot until the chicken is done.

Authentic touch

Use canned coconut milk, shaken, and do not try to process raw cassava for this meal.

How it is used

Serve the chicken, greens, and sweet potato with the coconut juices.

Field notes

  • A lovo is a Fijian earth oven. This page is a covered home pot, not a pit.
  • The recipe uses sweet potato and canned coconut milk, not raw cassava.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds chicken pieces
  • 1 can coconut milk, 13.5 ounces
  • 1 pound sweet potato, peeled and chunked
  • 4 cups spinach
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 mild chili, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Put the chicken, coconut milk, sweet potato, spinach, garlic, chili, and salt in a heavy pot. Do not add cassava. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Cover the pot. This is a home cook, not a Fijian lovo pit and not a ceremony. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Cook over medium heat until the thickest chicken piece reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone, and the sweet potato is tender. Do not serve the chicken rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest a few minutes and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Taro leaves can replace the spinach when a shop sells them as a food leaf, not a random garden leaf.
  • A little more chili makes the pot hotter. This one is mild.
