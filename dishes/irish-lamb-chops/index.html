Irish Lamb Chops

Next stop: Finnish Loimulohi · continuing the voyage

Pronounced lamb chops

Grilled lamb with browned fat, salt, pepper, and a little garlic.

15 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Lamb is everyday meat in Ireland because the grass grows well in a wet climate. Grilled chops are a simple way to cook it at home. This page does not invent a national barbecue festival for them. The seasoning stays light so the lamb tastes like lamb. Rosemary and garlic are common, not required.

Cultural background

Irish households cook these chops without a special costume or a made-up contest. It is weeknight and Sunday food. It is not a smoked leg and not a minced kebab.

Why it tastes this way

Pasture-fed lamb carries a mild, grassy fat that browns over a hot fire. Salt and a short cook keep that taste in the foreground. Smoke from charcoal is a light extra, not a thick bark. The climate that grows the grass is the reason the meat is here at all.

How it is made

Season lamb chops and grill them over a hot fire, turning once.

Authentic touch

Take the chops out of the cold a short while before cooking so they grill evenly. Rest them before you cut.

How it is used

With potatoes, cabbage or a green salad, and mint sauce if you like it sharp.

Field notes

  • Ireland's wet climate grows the grass that feeds most of its lamb.
  • Grilled lamb chops are ordinary food, and this page does not invent a barbecue festival for them.

Ingredients

  • 8 lamb chops, about 1 inch thick
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Rub the chops with garlic, oil, salt, and pepper.
  2. Grill over a hot fire, turning once, until the fat edge is browned.
  3. Cook whole lamb to at least 145°F (63°C) and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with potatoes. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A lemon and olive-oil marinade for an hour.
  • Loin chops or thicker barn chops from the shoulder, with the time adjusted.

Pairings

  • Mint sauce
  • Boiled potatoes
  • Cabbage
