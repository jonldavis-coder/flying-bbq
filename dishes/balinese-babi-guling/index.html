Balinese Babi Guling

Next stop: Lombok Ayam Taliwang · continuing the voyage

Pronounced BAH-bee GOO-ling

Pork with turmeric, lemongrass, garlic, and chili, cooked until it can be sliced or pulled.

40 min prep · 4 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Babi guling is spit-roasted pig in Hindu Balinese cooking, prepared for temple ceremonies and family rites and also sold in shops, especially around Gianyar. The spice paste is turmeric, lemongrass, chili, garlic, and other aromatics rubbed into the meat. A whole pig on a spit is the ceremony and the shop version. This page uses a pork shoulder at home and says so; it is not a ceremony, and it is not a joke about the feast.

Cultural background

Hindu Balinese cooks tend this pork for important days, and specialist shops roast it for people who are not holding a rite that week. The home shoulder is a substitute. Candlenut in the paste is a tree nut. There is no dairy and no wheat.

Why it tastes this way

Turmeric and lemongrass perfume the fat, and chili warms the paste that steams inside the meat. A long cook softens the shoulder after it is already safe to eat. On a real spit the skin crackles over coals. A home oven can still give you the spice and the tender pork, which is the part a cook can reach without the ceremony.

How it is made

Rub a pork shoulder with a Balinese spice paste and roast it on a spit if you have one. Otherwise use a covered home oven and call it a home roast, not a ceremonial spit.

Authentic touch

Rub the paste into deep cuts. A paint of spice on the outside leaves the center plain.

How it is used

Slice or chop it and eat it with rice and a sambal on the side.

Field notes

  • Babi guling is spit-roasted pig in Hindu Balinese feast cooking.
  • A home pork shoulder is a substitute. It is not the ceremonial spit.

Ingredients

  • 1 boneless pork shoulder, about 4 pounds
  • 6 garlic cloves, peeled
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, tender parts chopped
  • 1 tablespoon chopped fresh turmeric, or 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 2 hot chilies
  • 4 candlenuts
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Blend the garlic, lemongrass, turmeric, chilies, candlenuts, coriander, salt, and oil. Cut slits in the pork and rub the paste in. Refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The pork is still raw. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  2. If you have a spit and can cook on it safely, use it. Otherwise heat a home oven to 325°F (165°C). This oven roast is not a ceremonial babi guling. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  3. Cook until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C). For pork you want to slice tender or pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C). Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  4. Rest it, then slice or chop. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A real spit, if you have one and know how to run it, is the shop method. The oven is the home method.
  • Leaving out the candlenut would remove the tree nut and change the paste.

Pairings

  • rice
  • sambal
  • long beans
