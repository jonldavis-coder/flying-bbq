Lao Ping Kai

Next stop: Malaysian Chicken Satay · continuing the voyage

Pronounced ping kai

Grilled chicken with lemongrass, garlic, and fish sauce, chopped for sticky rice.

30 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Ping kai is Lao grilled chicken. On the Thai side of the same border, gai yang is the cousin: both are charcoal chickens eaten with sticky rice and a chili dip, from shared border cooking along the Mekong. They are not one recipe. This marinade uses lemongrass with the garlic and fish sauce; the bird is cooked through, then chopped.

Cultural background

Lao cooks grill ping kai for markets and home meals in Vientiane and in towns down the river. Fish sauce contains fish. There is no dairy and no wheat. It is the Lao chicken, related to Isan gai yang and seasoned on this page with lemongrass.

Why it tastes this way

Lemongrass and garlic char on the skin and perfume the fat, and fish sauce salts the meat. Sticky rice and a sour dip are the rest of the plate, because the chicken itself is rich from the skin. Shared ingredients along the border explain the family resemblance. The lemongrass here is the Lao tilt.

How it is made

Marinate a butterflied chicken with lemongrass, garlic, and fish sauce, and grill it until the thigh is done.

Authentic touch

Open the chicken flat so the thighs meet the heat. A whole closed bird burns at the wing before the joint is done.

How it is used

Chop it and eat it with sticky rice and a chili dip.

Field notes

  • Ping kai is Lao grilled chicken, a cousin of Thai gai yang from shared Mekong cooking.
  • The two chickens are related and they are not the same recipe.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 3.5 pounds, backbone removed
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, tender parts minced
  • 5 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tablespoons fish sauce
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Mix the lemongrass, garlic, fish sauce, oil, pepper, sugar, and salt. Rub the chicken and refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Heat a charcoal grill for medium heat. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone. Do not serve it rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest 5 minutes, then chop it. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A hotter dip is served on the side. The marinade here is mild.
  • Thighs alone can be grilled when a whole bird is too much. They still need 165°F (74°C).

Pairings
