New Mexico Green Chile Pork

Next stop: Nevada Basque Lamb Chops · continuing the voyage

Pork simmered with roasted Hatch or other New Mexico green chile, savory and hot to the degree the chiles are.

History

Pork with roasted green chile is a New Mexico plate, tied to the Hatch Valley and to other New Mexico chile fields. It is not Texas barbecue, and it does not use a brisket rub. Heat varies with the field and the year. This version roasts the chiles, then cooks pork shoulder in them until it is tender.

Cultural background

New Mexico green chile pork. A chile stew, not a Lockhart smoke and not a sweet glaze. The chile's heat is part of the ingredient, not a fixed number.

Why it tastes this way

Roasting green chile brings out a grassy, slightly bitter sweetness that canned jalapeño does not copy. Pork shoulder fat stands up to that chile. High-desert cultivation and a late-summer harvest are why the dish belongs to New Mexico rather than to a generic pepper sauce.

How it is made

Roast New Mexico green chiles, then simmer pork shoulder in them until the meat is tender.

Authentic touch

Taste the chile before you add all of it. Hatch and other New Mexico chiles can be mild or very hot in the same bushel.

How it is used

In a bowl, with tortillas or rice. Flour tortillas would add wheat; corn tortillas keep this batch gluten-free.

Field notes

  • Hatch, New Mexico, is a center of the green-chile harvest, and this pork is not a Texas barbecue.
  • The heat of New Mexico green chile changes with the crop, so taste before you commit the whole batch.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds pork shoulder, cut into chunks
  • 1 1/2 pounds Hatch or other New Mexico green chiles
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 2 cups water

Steps

  1. Roast the chiles on a hot grill or under a broiler until blistered. Steam them in a covered bowl, peel them, and chop them. Taste one before you add them all.
  2. Salt the pork and brown it in a heavy pot. Add onion, garlic, cumin, chiles, and water.
  3. Cover and simmer until the pork reaches about 195–205°F (90–96°C) and is easy to shred. Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest; this stew goes further so the shoulder softens.
  4. Taste for salt. Serve with corn tortillas, not as a Texas brisket plate.

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Frozen roasted New Mexico green chile is a workable stand-in when fresh Hatch is out of season.

Pairings
