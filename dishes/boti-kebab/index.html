Boti Kebab

Next stop: Paneer Tikka · continuing the voyage

Pronounced BOH-tee keh-BAB

Lamb chunks with yogurt, chili, and ginger, charred at the edges.

30 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Boti kebab is lamb cut in chunks and threaded on skewers, not minced. It sits in the same North Indian kebab tradition as seekh, and the difference is the cut of meat. Yogurt, ginger, and chili are the usual marinade in restaurant and home grills. The chunks are whole muscle, so they are cooked and rested like other whole lamb, not like ground meat.

Cultural background

This is cubed lamb from a leg or shoulder, grilled by North Indian kebab cooks. Yogurt makes it a dairy dish. It is not seekh, which is mince, and it is not a whole roasted leg.

Why it tastes this way

A chunk of lamb keeps a juicy center while the yogurt marinade spots and browns. Ginger and chili season the surface. On charcoal the fat at the edge crisps. That is a different bite from minced seekh, where the spice is mixed all the way through.

How it is made

Marinate lamb chunks in yogurt and spice, skewer them, and grill them to a whole-muscle temperature with a rest.

Authentic touch

Cut the cubes the same size so they finish together. A thick cube next to a thin one will leave one of them overcooked.

How it is used

Eat the chunks with onion, a green herb chutney if you have one made fresh, and lemon.

Field notes

  • Boti is cut lamb on a skewer. Seekh is the minced kebab in the same grill tradition.
  • Whole lamb chunks are rested after they leave the fire.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds boneless lamb leg, cut in 1.5-inch chunks
  • 3/4 cup plain yogurt
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon Kashmiri chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1.5 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Mix the yogurt, ginger, garlic, chili, cumin, salt, and oil. Coat the lamb and refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The lamb is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Thread the chunks on skewers. Heat a grill or a tandoor until it is hot. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center of the thickest chunk reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the lamb 3 minutes before serving. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Shoulder cubes have more fat. Leg cubes are leaner and still work if they are not overcooked.
  • A hotter chili powder is common in some kitchens. This marinade is medium.

Pairings

  • sliced onion
  • lemon
  • mint
