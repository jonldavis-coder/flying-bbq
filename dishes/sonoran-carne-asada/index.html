Sonoran Carne Asada

Next stop: Monterrey Cabrito · continuing the voyage

Pronounced kar-neh ah-SAH-dah

Beef grilled over mesquite, salted, with a flour tortilla as the wrap rather than a dough in the meat.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Carne asada in Sonora is a northern Mexican grill: beef over mesquite, eaten in flour tortillas. Wheat tortillas belong to the north as a pairing. They are not mixed into the steak. The same table is part of life in southern Arizona, which is why this stop also carries the United States. It is not the Salvadoran plate of the same Spanish name.

Cultural background

Sonoran carne asada is a ranch and backyard steak, often thin, often mesquite. The meat itself is beef and salt. Beans, salsa, and grilled onions sit beside it. This page does not put wheat in the rub.

Why it tastes this way

Mesquite coals are hot and a little sweet, and they mark beef faster than a mild hardwood. Northern Sonora is cattle and desert country, so the meal is steak and a wheat tortilla rather than a long chile stew. The lime and salt on the meat stay sharp because the cook is short.

How it is made

Salt a thin steak and grill it hot over mesquite coals to a whole-muscle temperature, then rest it.

Authentic touch

Burn mesquite down to coals before the steak goes on. Flames from fresh logs will taste like soot, not like Sonoran mesquite.

How it is used

Slice it and eat it with flour tortillas, grilled spring onions, and a fresh salsa. The tortilla is a pairing.

Field notes

  • Mesquite is the usual grilling wood of Sonora.
  • Flour tortillas are the northern wrap; the steak in this recipe has no wheat in it.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds thin beef steak, such as ranchera or skirt
  • 2 teaspoons coarse salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 limes, cut into wedges
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • Mesquite wood or charcoal, burned to coals

Steps

  1. Oil the steak lightly and season it with salt and pepper. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. There is no flour in this step. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Burn mesquite or charcoal to a bed of coals. A flaming log is not the grilling stage. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill the steak until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice across the grain. The steak should have reached 145°F (63°C) and rested. Squeeze lime at the table. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A gas grill with a mesquite chunk on the coals is a home stand-in, and it is not a mesquite fire.
  • Ranchera, flank, or skirt all appear on this grill. Cook each one as a whole cut.

Pairings

  • flour tortillas
  • grilled spring onions
  • fresh salsa
  • beans
