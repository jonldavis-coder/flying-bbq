Ramp-Season Smoked Trout

Next stop: Country Ham Steak · continuing the voyage

Smoked trout with the garlic-onion smell of spring ramps.

20 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Ramps are Allium tricoccum, a wild leek of the eastern mountains, dug in spring. This plate is home cooking: trout with ramps, smoked or grilled. It is not a Cherokee ceremony, and it is not a claim on anyone's seasonal rite. Harvest ramps only where it is legal, and do not pull a whole patch.

Cultural background

A spring home plate of trout and ramps. Cherokee and other mountain nations have their own foods; this recipe does not speak for them.

Why it tastes this way

Ramps are sharper than planted onion and milder than raw garlic, so a few leaves season an oily trout without a sauce. Smoke clings to the fish skin. Spring is the only honest season for the plant, which is why the plate is not a year-round pantry dish.

How it is made

Hot-smoke or grill trout and toss it with ramps that have been wilted in a little oil.

Authentic touch

Use a few ramps, not a sack. If you cannot harvest legally, use a small amount of cultivated leek and do not call it ramps.

How it is used

Flaked warm, with the greens. Bread is optional and would add wheat.

Field notes

  • Ramps are a wild allium, Allium tricoccum, and digging them is illegal in some parks and on some private land.
  • This is a home cook of trout and ramps, not a Cherokee ceremony.

Ingredients

  • 4 small trout, dressed, or 1 1/2 pounds trout fillets
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 6 ramps, cleaned, or 1 small leek if ramps cannot be harvested legally
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Confirm the ramps were harvested legally. Rinse them well and slice the bulbs and leaves.
  2. Salt and pepper the trout. Wilt the ramps in oil in a pan until soft, then take them off the heat.
  3. Hot-smoke or grill the trout over indirect or gentle direct heat until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh flakes.
  4. Spoon the ramps over the fish and serve. This is home cooking, not a ceremony.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A hot grill instead of a smoker works if you still cook the fish to temperature.

Pairings

  • cornbread
  • lemon
  • boiled potatoes
