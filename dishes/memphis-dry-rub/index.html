Memphis Dry Rub

Paprika, salt, black pepper, and a little brown sugar.

History

A Memphis dry rub is the seasoning on ribs that are served without sauce on the meat. Paprika, salt, pepper, and sugar are the core, and the sugar helps the bark color. It is not the wet Memphis sauce, and it is not a salt-and-pepper-only Texas rub. The sugar here is ordinary brown sugar, not honey.

Cultural background

A dry spice mix for Memphis ribs. No sauce is part of this rub. It is a pantry mix, not a cooked condiment.

Why it tastes this way

Paprika and a little sugar brown in hickory smoke and make the crust people expect on a dry rib. Salt seasons the pork underneath. Leaving out tomato and molasses is the decision that keeps a dry rib dry.

How it is made

Stir paprika, salt, pepper, sugar, and garlic powder. Store the mix dry.

Authentic touch

Rub the meat and then leave it alone. Do not oil it so heavily that the spices steam instead of crusting.

How it is used

Pressed onto pork ribs or shoulder before a smoke. Sauce, if any, stays off the dry-rib plate.

Field notes

  • A Memphis dry rub uses paprika, salt, pepper, and sugar, and the rib under it is served without sauce.
  • The sugar in this rub is brown sugar, not honey.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons paprika
  • 2 tablespoons kosher salt
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • 2 teaspoons garlic powder
  • 2 teaspoons onion powder

Steps

  1. Stir paprika, salt, pepper, brown sugar, garlic powder, and onion powder in a bowl.
  2. Store the rub in a dry jar. It is not cooked, and it is not a canning recipe.
  3. Press it onto pork before smoking. Do not add sauce to a dry rib.
  4. Cook those ribs to about 195–203°F (90–95°C) for tenderness. Pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) after a 3-minute rest. The rub itself has no internal temperature.

These ingredients are vegetables, bread, or a sauce. Keep them clean, and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. This is not a canning recipe.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little cayenne makes the rub hotter. The base stays paprika and pepper.

Pairings

  • pork ribs
  • pickles
  • white bread
