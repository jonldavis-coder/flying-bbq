Balochistan Sajji

Pronounced SUH-jee

Whole chicken, mostly salt, with dry heat from coals set beside the bird.

History

Sajji is a Balochistan roast, especially associated with Baloch cooks. A whole chicken is salted and cooked slowly beside coals, not over a bed of flame and not in a yogurt tandoor marinade. The seasoning is salt, which is the point. It is a Pakistani dish from Balochistan; a covered grill with the coals pushed to one side is the home version of that beside-the-fire cook.

Cultural background

Baloch cooks salt a whole chicken and give it time next to the coals. There is no chili paste and no yogurt. It is a different bird from Delhi tandoori chicken.

Why it tastes this way

Salt and slow side heat leave the chicken tasting like chicken, with a little smoke from the coals and a drier skin than a steamed bird. Balochistan's cooking here is spare on purpose. Spice would hide the thing sajji is known for.

How it is made

Salt a whole chicken and cook it on a spit or a rack beside coals until the thigh is done.

Authentic touch

Keep the coals to the side. If the skin burns while the thigh is still raw, the fire is under the bird instead of beside it.

How it is used

Break it into pieces and eat it with lemon and flatbread only if you want wheat beside it. The chicken itself is only salt and fire.

Field notes

  • Sajji from Balochistan is a salted whole chicken cooked slowly beside coals.
  • The seasoning is salt. A yogurt chili marinade is a different dish.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 3.5 pounds
  • 2 tablespoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 lemon, cut in wedges
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Pat the chicken dry. Rub it with oil, salt, and pepper, including a little salt in the cavity. Refrigerate it until the fire is ready. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Bank a charcoal fire to one side of a covered grill, or set the bird on a spit with the coals beside it, not piled under the breast. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Cook with the lid on, turning as needed, until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone. Do not serve it rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest it 10 minutes, then cut it up. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lamb sajji is also cooked in Balochistan. This page is the chicken.
  • A covered kettle grill with the coals banked to one side is the home setup.

Pairings

  • lemon
  • sliced onion
  • a green salad
