Iranian Koobideh

Next stop: Iranian Joojeh Kabab · continuing the voyage

Pronounced koo-bee-DEH

Minced lamb and onion, salted, with a smoky grilled crust.

30 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 skewers

History

Koobideh is an Iranian minced-lamb kebab pressed on a wide skewer. The name is widely linked to the verb for pounding, because the mince is worked until it stays on the metal. Onion and salt are the core seasoning. It is restaurant and home food in Tehran and across Iran. It is not the saffron chicken kebab, and it is not Lebanese kafta with a handful of parsley. The meat is ground and cooked through.

Cultural background

Iranian kebab houses grill koobideh next to barg and joojeh. This page is only the mince. It does not use a chile-heavy Adana mix.

Why it tastes this way

Grated onion kept in the mince makes the kebab moist and slightly sweet, and charcoal dries the outside so it does not taste boiled. Salt is enough because the lamb is the flavor. Working the meat changes the texture: it holds, and it bites smoothly. That texture is as important as any spice.

How it is made

Knead minced lamb with onion and salt, form it on wide skewers, and grill until the center is cooked.

Authentic touch

Grate the onion and squeeze out a little liquid so the kebab is moist but not soggy. Press it onto a wide skewer with wet hands.

How it is used

With rice, grilled tomato, and raw onion. Sumac is a common sprinkle.

Field notes

  • Koobideh is minced lamb worked until it holds on a wide skewer.
  • The name is widely linked to pounding, which describes how the meat is prepared.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb ground lamb
  • 1 small onion, grated
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • A pinch of baking soda
  • 4 wide flat metal skewers

Steps

  1. Mix the lamb, onion, salt, pepper, and baking soda until the paste is sticky. Chill 20 minutes.
  2. Press onto wide skewers. Heat charcoal until it is hot.
  3. Grill, turning often, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C).
  4. Serve with rice and tomato. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A blend of beef and lamb, still ground.
  • A pinch of turmeric, which some home cooks add.

Pairings

  • Rice
  • Grilled tomato
  • Raw onion
