Uzbek Lamb Shashlik

Next stop: Georgian Mtsvadi · continuing the voyage

Pronounced shash-LEEK

Lamb cubes with salt, onion, and the fat that bastes them over coals.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 skewers

History

This Central Asian skewer is a major source of the shashlik tradition. In Uzbekistan the usual meat is lamb, cut in cubes, salted, and often alternated with fat, with little marinade beyond onion. Samarkand and other cities grill it over coals as everyday and feast food. Later versions in Russia and elsewhere are descendants and neighbors, not the start of the practice. The meat is whole muscle.

Cultural background

Uzbek shashlik cooks at markets and at home are the people behind this page. It is not a heavily sauced kebab and not the Russian dacha version, which has its own entry as a later home cook.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb fat dripping onto coals makes the smoke that seasons the cubes. A short salt and onion rest, rather than a long wet marinade, leaves the lamb tasting like lamb. Hot coals brown the edges fast. The Central Asian grill, simple and fatty, is why shashlik spread with that character instead of as a wet curry.

How it is made

Skewer lamb and fat, salt it, and grill over hot coals, then rest.

Authentic touch

Put a small piece of lamb fat between lean cubes. Salt them. Do not bury the meat in a sweet sauce.

How it is used

With raw onion and green tea.

Field notes

  • Uzbek lamb shashlik is a Central Asian skewer and a major source of the wider shashlik tradition.
  • The usual seasoning is light: salt, onion, and fat, not a long marinade.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb boneless lamb leg, cut in 1 1/2 inch cubes
  • 4 oz lamb fat, cut in small pieces
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 4 metal skewers

Steps

  1. Toss the lamb and fat with onion, salt, and pepper. Rest 20 minutes. Thread lamb and fat on skewers.
  2. Heat coals until they are hot and gray.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest cube reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Give the skewers a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with onion. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • All lean cubes if you skip the extra fat, with a closer watch so they do not dry.
  • A sprinkle of cumin after cooking, which some tables like and some do not.

Pairings

  • Raw onion
  • Tomato
  • Green herbs
