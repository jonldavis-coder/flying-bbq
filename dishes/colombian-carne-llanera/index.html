Colombian Carne a la Llanera

Next stop: Colombian Chuzos · continuing the voyage

Pronounced KAR-neh ah lah yah-NEH-rah

Beef grilled over wood, salted, with a charred edge and a juicy center.

20 min prep · 1 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Carne a la llanera is beef from the Colombian llanos, the cattle plains east of the Andes, grilled over wood. Villavicencio is a gateway to that country. The cook is an open fire, often a large cut on a frame or a grate. It shares a landscape with Venezuelan carne en vara and it is not the same recipe. Salt is the seasoning.

Cultural background

A home-size roast stands in for the very large cuts of a llanero fire. Say so. The meat is whole beef. Yuca, plantain, and aji are the usual sides, listed here as pairings.

Why it tastes this way

Wood on the open plains burns hot and clean, and the beef tastes of that dry heat and of salt. There is no long spice paste. The flavor is the animal and the fire, which is the point of calling it a la llanera rather than a city steak with a sauce.

How it is made

Salt a beef roast and grill it over wood coals to a whole-beef temperature.

Authentic touch

Burn the wood to coals before a thick roast goes on. Flames will crust the outside while the center is still raw.

How it is used

Slice it and eat it with yuca, plantain, and a fresh aji salsa.

Field notes

  • The llanos are the cattle plains of eastern Colombia.
  • The beef is cooked over wood, which is why it is called a la llanera.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds boneless beef roast
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • Wood burned to coals

Steps

  1. Oil the roast and salt it. It is raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Spread wood coals under a grate. Keep a flame from touching the meat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested beef. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A thin steak on the same coals cooks faster and still needs 145°F (63°C) and a 3-minute rest.
  • A gas grill is a home substitute. It is not a wood fire on the llanos.

Pairings

  • yuca
  • fried plantain
  • aji salsa
