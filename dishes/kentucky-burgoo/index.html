Owensboro Burgoo

Next stop: Hickory-Smoked Turkey · continuing the voyage

Pronounced BUR-goo

A thick stew of chicken, beef, corn, and lima beans, peppery and long-cooked.

40 min prep · 3 hr cook · Serves 10 · about 10 servings

History

Burgoo is a community kettle associated with western Kentucky barbecues, including the mutton picnics around Owensboro. It is not a single nineteenth-century recipe this page can verify, so none is claimed. This pot uses chicken and beef with vegetables. Cooks have used other meats, including mutton, when that was what the picnic had.

Cultural background

A Kentucky barbecue stew, not a settled antique formula. Chicken and beef are both cooked through. It often sits beside mutton rather than replacing it.

Why it tastes this way

Long simmering turns several meats and a sack of vegetables into one spoonable pot, which is how a picnic feeds a line. Black pepper and tomato keep it savory instead of sweet. The same western Kentucky fires that cook mutton are why burgoo belongs on that table.

How it is made

Simmer chicken and beef with vegetables until the meats are safe to eat and the stew is thick.

Authentic touch

Simmer until the vegetables collapse and the stew thickens. A thin soup is not yet burgoo.

How it is used

In bowls, beside mutton or on its own, with bread if you want it. Bread would add wheat.

Field notes

  • Burgoo is a community kettle associated with western Kentucky barbecues, not a single verified nineteenth-century recipe.
  • Owensboro picnic tables have served it beside mutton.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds bone-in chicken thighs
  • 1 pound beef stew meat
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 potatoes, diced
  • 1 can crushed tomato, 15 ounces
  • 2 cups frozen lima beans
  • 2 cups frozen corn
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 6 cups water

Steps

  1. Put chicken, beef, onion, potato, tomato, salt, pepper, and water in a large pot.
  2. Simmer until the chicken reaches 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part and the beef is at least 145°F (63°C) and tender. Keep simmering so the meats soften in the stew.
  3. Add lima beans and corn and cook until the vegetables are tender and the stew is thick. Frozen limas still need to be heated through in the pot.
  4. Remove chicken bones, shred the meat back in, and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lamb can replace the beef if you are cooking it next to a mutton barbecue. Give it the same long simmer.

Pairings

  • mutton
  • white bread
  • onion
