Japanese Yakiniku

Next stop: Japanese Unagi Kabayaki · continuing the voyage

Pronounced yah-kee-NEE-koo

Thin beef and onion grilled at the table, with a soy dip.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Yakiniku is the Japanese restaurant practice of grilling meat at the table. It grew in the twentieth century in the hands of Korean cooks in Japan, including Zainichi Korean communities, and it became a widespread night out in cities such as Osaka and Tokyo. It is not an ancient Japanese court dish. The meat is often beef, grilled in small pieces and dipped; ordinary soy sauce in the dip contains wheat; the beef is a whole cut, cooked and rested.

Cultural background

Yakiniku restaurants set a grill in the table and bring raw plates the guests cook. That restaurant form is twentieth-century and tied to Korean cooks in Japan. This home version grills beef and onion and serves a soy dip. There is beef, soy, wheat, and sesame. There is no dairy.

Why it tastes this way

Small pieces of beef brown fast, and a sweet-salty dip seasons them after the grill, so you taste both the meat and the sauce. Onion sweetens on the same grate. The style traveled through Korean restaurants in Japanese cities, which is why the dip resembles Korean barbecue sauces and the setting is a Japanese restaurant grill. Calling it a court recipe would erase that history.

How it is made

Grill thin beef and onion over high heat, rest the beef, and dip it in a soy and sesame sauce.

Authentic touch

Cook a few pieces at a time. A crowded table grill steams the beef and misses the char.

How it is used

Eat it with rice and the dip, straight from the grill.

Field notes

  • Yakiniku restaurants in Japan grew in the twentieth century among Korean cooks, including Zainichi Korean communities.
  • It is a restaurant grill. It is not an ancient Japanese court dish.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds beef sirloin, sliced thin
  • 1 onion, sliced into rounds
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame seeds

Steps

  1. Stir the soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, garlic, and sesame seeds for the dip. Keep it separate from the raw beef. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Heat a grill or a table grill. The beef is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill the beef and onion until the center of the beef reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the beef 3 minutes. Serve with the dip and rice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Tongue, liver, and other cuts are on a full restaurant menu. This page is beef and onion.
  • Beef offal, if you add it, needs its own temperature and a careful source.
