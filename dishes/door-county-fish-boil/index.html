Door County Fish Boil

Next stop: Minnesota Grilled Walleye · continuing the voyage

Whitefish and potatoes boiled over a fire, salty, with butter at the table.

20 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

The Door County fish boil is a Scandinavian American kettle cook over an open fire: potatoes and whitefish in heavily salted water, often finished with a dramatic boil-over that throws off fish oils. It is not a grill. Immigrant fishing communities on the peninsula made the boil a public supper. This recipe keeps the kettle and skips any claim about a single first restaurant.

Cultural background

An open-fire fish boil, not a grilled fillet. Whitefish and potatoes are the core. Butter is served at the table.

Why it tastes this way

A hard salted boil seasons the potato skins and keeps the whitefish moist in a way a dry grill does not. The boil-over, when cooks throw fuel or fat on the fire so the pot erupts, carries off some of the fish oil. Butter at the end is the supper habit, dairy and all.

How it is made

Boil potatoes, then whitefish, in salted water over an open fire until the fish flakes.

Authentic touch

This is a kettle over fire, not a grate. Do not call it a grilled fish. Stand back if you force a boil-over.

How it is used

On plates with the potatoes, melted butter, and lemon. Cherry pie is a local supper ending and is not part of this pot.

Field notes

  • Door County fish boils are cooked in a kettle over open fire, not on a grill grate.
  • The custom is tied to Scandinavian American fishing communities on the Door Peninsula.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds whitefish steaks
  • 2 pounds small red potatoes
  • 1/2 cup kosher salt
  • 4 quarts water
  • 4 tablespoons butter
  • 1 lemon

Steps

  1. Set a heavy kettle over a steady open fire, or over a burner if you are indoors. This is a boil, not a grill.
  2. Boil the potatoes in the salted water until they are nearly tender.
  3. Add the whitefish and cook until the thickest steak reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque and flakes. A vigorous boil-over is a traditional way to throw off oil; stand back if you do it.
  4. Lift out fish and potatoes. Serve with melted butter and lemon.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lake trout is sometimes boiled the same way when whitefish is scarce. Cook it to the same temperature.

Pairings

  • butter
  • lemon
  • boiled potatoes
