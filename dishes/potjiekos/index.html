Chicken Potjiekos

Next stop: Cape Snoek Braai · continuing the voyage

Pronounced POY-kee-kos

Chicken and vegetables simmered in a covered pot over coals, lightly spiced.

25 min prep · 1 hr 30 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Potjiekos is food cooked in a three-legged cast-iron pot set over coals. The pot style came with European settlers and became a South African camp, farm, and backyard dish. It is not a grill. The lid stays on, and the pot simmers. This version is a chicken and vegetable pot. The chicken still has to reach a poultry temperature. You do not stir it like a stew on the stove.

Cultural background

South African camp cooks and households make potjie when there is time to leave a pot alone. It is a covered coal cook, not a chop on a grid. The chicken and vegetables are layered.

Why it tastes this way

Steam trapped in iron softens onions, potatoes, and chicken together, and a little smoke leaks in from the coals under the pot. Paprika and garlic season the juice. Because you do not grill the meat in the open, the flavor is rounded and soupy rather than charred. That is the point of using a pot instead of a grid.

How it is made

Brown chicken, layer it with vegetables in a cast-iron pot, and simmer over coals with the lid on. This is not a grill.

Authentic touch

Layer the ingredients and do not stir. Check the chicken temperature without turning the pot into a boil.

How it is used

From the pot, with rice or pap. The juices are the sauce.

Field notes

  • Potjiekos is cooked in a cast-iron pot over coals, and it is not a grill.
  • The lid stays on so the pot simmers instead of roasting in the open.

Ingredients

  • 6 chicken thighs, bone in
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 4 potatoes, chunked
  • 3 carrots, chunked
  • 4 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon paprika
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 cup water

Steps

  1. Brown the chicken in oil in the pot. Take it out. Soften the onion in the same pot. This cook is a pot over coals, not a grill.
  2. Layer potatoes, carrots, garlic, tomatoes, paprika, salt, and the chicken. Add the water. Cover.
  3. Set the pot over a small bed of coals and cook gently until the thickest thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), not touching bone, and the vegetables are tender.
  4. Rest 10 minutes. Do not stir it into mush. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lamb neck in place of chicken, cooked as a whole muscle to at least 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest, and longer if you want it tender.
  • A Dutch oven on a grate if you do not own a three-legged potjie.

Pairings

  • Rice
  • Pap
  • Green salad
