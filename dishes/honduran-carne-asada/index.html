Honduran Carne Asada with Chimol

Next stop: Nicaraguan Churrasco · continuing the voyage

Pronounced chee-MOHL

Grilled beef with a raw, bright chop of tomato, onion, and cilantro.

20 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Honduran carne asada is grilled beef with chimol, a finely chopped mix of tomato, onion, and cilantro. Chimol is fresh, not roasted like Guatemalan chirmol and not the parsley sauce of an Argentine parrilla. The plate often includes fried plantains, beans, and tortillas. It is its own Central American grill.

Cultural background

The chimol is a relish, chopped small, with lime and salt. The steak is a whole cut. Neither one is a bottled sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Raw onion and cilantro stay sharp against beef that has taken a direct char. Because the chimol is not cooked, it tastes like a salad spooned over a hot steak. Lime juice is the acid, so the plate is fresher and wetter than a dry salted grill.

How it is made

Grill a steak to a whole-muscle temperature and spoon raw chimol over the slices.

Authentic touch

Chop the chimol fine and salt it just before serving so the tomato does not weep into a puddle.

How it is used

Serve with tortillas, beans, and fried plantains.

Field notes

  • Chimol is the Honduran chopped relish of tomato, onion, and cilantro.
  • It is a different relish from Guatemalan chirmol and from Argentine chimichurri.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds beef steak
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped fine
  • 1/2 onion, chopped fine
  • 1/2 cup chopped cilantro
  • 1 lime
  • 1/2 green bell pepper, chopped fine

Steps

  1. Oil and salt the steak. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Mix tomato, onion, bell pepper, cilantro, lime juice, and a pinch of salt. This chimol stays raw. Keep it cold. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill the steak until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested steak. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Spoon on the chimol. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little minced green chile makes the chimol hotter. This amount stays mild.
  • Chicken on the same grill would need 165°F (74°C). This recipe is beef.

Pairings
