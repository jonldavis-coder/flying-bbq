Cook Islands Umu Pork

Next stop: Central Texas Salt-and-Pepper Brisket · continuing the voyage

Pronounced OO-moo

Pork shoulder with garlic, ginger, cabbage, and sweet potato, steamed in a covered pot.

25 min prep · 4 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

An umu in the Cook Islands is an earth oven used for community meals, and pork is one of the foods put in it, with root vegetables and greens. Avarua, on Rarotonga, is the town on this page. The home version is a covered pot with a pork shoulder, garlic, ginger, cabbage, and sweet potato. It is not a pit and not a ceremony; there is no raw cassava; the pork is cooked until it is safe and tender.

Cultural background

Cook Islands families prepare umu food for gatherings. This pot is the home substitute: pork and sweet potato, with ginger and cabbage, which makes it a different plate from the Samoan coconut spinach pot and the Tongan lamb pot. There is no wheat and no dairy.

Why it tastes this way

Pork, ginger, and cabbage in a closed pot taste savory and slightly sweet once the shoulder softens. Sweet potato takes the drippings. An umu pit would add leaf and stone heat. The pot still gives a tender pork and a soft vegetable, which is a meal you can cook without claiming the gathering.

How it is made

Cook a pork shoulder with ginger, cabbage, and sweet potato in a covered pot until it is safe and tender.

Authentic touch

Keep the cabbage in large wedges so it does not dissolve before the pork is tender.

How it is used

Slice the pork and serve it with the cabbage and sweet potato.

Field notes

  • Cook Islands umu food often includes pork. This page cooks a shoulder in a covered pot.
  • The pot is not a ceremony, and it does not use raw cassava.

Ingredients

  • 1 boneless pork shoulder, about 4 pounds
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1/2 small cabbage, cut in wedges
  • 1.5 pounds sweet potato, peeled and chunked
  • 1/2 cup water

Steps

  1. Rub the pork with ginger, garlic, and salt. Put it in a heavy pot with cabbage, sweet potato, and water. Do not add cassava. The pork is still raw. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  2. Cover the pot. This is a home cook, not a Cook Islands umu ceremony. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  3. Cook over low heat until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C). For pork you want tender, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C). Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  4. Rest it, then slice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Coconut milk can be added and would move the flavor closer to other island pots. This one is ginger and cabbage.
  • A home oven, with the pot covered, is still a home cook.
