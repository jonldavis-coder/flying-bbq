Colorado Ranch Lamb Chops

Next stop: Owensboro Burgoo · continuing the voyage

Plain grilled lamb, salty and peppery, with a browned fat edge.

10 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Colorado ranch country raises lamb, especially on the western slope and in the northwest of the state. These chops are salt and pepper on a hot grill. They are not the garlic-heavy Nevada Basque chop, and this page does not invent a festival to explain them. The distinction is the plain seasoning and the ranch supply of lamb.

Cultural background

Salt-and-pepper lamb chops from Colorado ranch country. No fabricated fair. Not the Basque garlic marinade used in the Nevada entry.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb fat and a hot grate make the crust, and salt and pepper stay out of the way. High, dry air and a quick grill brown the surface before the lean meat dries. The plate tastes like the animal because nothing sweet is added.

How it is made

Grill lamb chops with salt and pepper only.

Authentic touch

Do not borrow the Basque garlic rub if you want this plate to stay the Colorado one. Salt and pepper are the recipe.

How it is used

Whole chops. Beans or potatoes can sit beside them.

Field notes

  • Colorado ranch country raises lamb, and this chop is only salt and pepper on a hot grill.
  • It is written as a different plate from Nevada Basque lamb, which uses garlic.

Ingredients

  • 8 lamb chops
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Oil the chops and season them with salt and pepper only.
  2. Heat a grill hot, with a cooler zone.
  3. Grill, turning once, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes. Do not add a garlic marinade if you are cooking this Colorado plate.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Shoulder chops need a slightly longer cook than loin chops. Use the thermometer, not the minute count.

Pairings

  • potatoes
  • beans
  • green salad
