Lombok Ayam Taliwang

Next stop: Malaysian Ikan Bakar · continuing the voyage

Pronounced AH-yum tah-lee-WAHNG

Grilled chicken covered in a very hot chili paste with garlic and candlenut.

30 min prep · 35 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Ayam taliwang is grilled chicken from Taliwang, in West Lombok, now cooked across the island and in Mataram. The paste is built on a large amount of hot chili, with garlic, shrimp paste, and candlenut. It is meant to be extra-hot. Sasak cooks grill the chicken and often fry or grill it again with more paste; this is a home grill of that Lombok chicken.

Cultural background

Lombok cooks, Sasak kitchens in particular, make this chili paste for chicken. Shrimp paste is shellfish. Candlenut is a tree nut. The chicken has to be cooked through. It is not a mild coconut curry.

Why it tastes this way

The chili is the dish. Garlic and shrimp paste make the paste savory under the heat, and candlenut thickens it. Grilling dries the paste onto the skin so it tastes toasted, not like a boiled sauce. A mild marinade would miss why Taliwang is famous.

How it is made

Blend a very hot chili paste with shrimp paste and candlenut, rub it on chicken, and grill the chicken until the thigh is done.

Authentic touch

Seed some of the chilies only if you must. Putting the chilies in and then sweetening the paste turns it into a different chicken.

How it is used

Eat it with rice and extra paste on the side for people who want still more heat.

Field notes

  • Ayam taliwang is named for Taliwang in West Lombok and is known for its heat.
  • Candlenut in the paste is a tree nut, and shrimp paste is shellfish.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 3.5 pounds, cut into serving pieces
  • 10 hot red chilies
  • 4 garlic cloves, peeled
  • 1 teaspoon shrimp paste
  • 3 candlenuts
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 teaspoon sugar

Steps

  1. Blend the chilies, garlic, shrimp paste, candlenuts, salt, oil, and sugar. Rub the paste on the chicken and refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Heat a grill to medium. The paste can burn if the fire is only a blast of flame. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest piece reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone. Do not serve it rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest a few minutes and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A second brush of paste near the end is how some cooks deepen the crust.
  • Fewer chilies make it merely hot. The written amount is extra-hot.

Pairings

  • rice
  • cucumber
  • lime
