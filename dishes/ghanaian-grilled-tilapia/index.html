Ghanaian Grilled Tilapia

Pronounced tih-LAH-pee-ah

Charred tilapia with ginger, garlic, and chili.

20 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 fish

History

Grilled tilapia is a common fish plate in Ghana, often eaten with banku and shito. Banku is a fermented corn and cassava dough, and shito is a hot sauce whose recipes vary. This page grills the fish and names those pairings without copying a full shito or banku recipe, because the sauces and the dough are their own crafts. The fish is seasoned with ginger and chili and cooked until it flakes.

Cultural background

Ghanaian chop-bar and street grill cooks make this. The recipe is the fish. Shito is mentioned, not reproduced. It is not a fried fillet in batter.

Why it tastes this way

Tilapia is a mild freshwater fish, so ginger, garlic, and chili do the seasoning that an oily sea fish would not need. A hot grill dries the skin into a crust. Banku on the side is sour from fermentation, and shito is hot, which is why the plate is a contrast even though only the fish is written out here. The lake and pond fisheries are why tilapia is the fish on the grate.

How it is made

Rub whole tilapia with ginger, garlic, and chili, and grill until the flesh flakes.

Authentic touch

Score the fish so the ginger paste reaches the flesh. Oil the skin or it will stick and tear.

How it is used

With banku and shito as pairings. Shito recipes differ and are not copied in full here. Read a bought sauce for fish and other allergens.

Field notes

  • Grilled tilapia in Ghana is commonly paired with banku and shito.
  • This page does not copy a full shito recipe, because those sauces vary.

Ingredients

  • 4 whole tilapia, gutted and scaled
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 fresh hot chile, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Cut slashes in the fish. Mix ginger, garlic, chile, salt, and oil and rub it inside and out.
  2. Heat a grill and oil the grate.
  3. Grill, turning once, until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve with the pairings you have. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A whole fish from another freshwater species, named as what it is.
  • Less chili in the rub, with the heat left to the sauce on the side.

Pairings

  • Banku
  • Shito
  • Sliced onion
