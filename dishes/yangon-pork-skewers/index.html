Yangon Pork Skewers

Next stop: Cantonese Char Siu · continuing the voyage

Pronounced YANG-gon pork skewers

Pork skewers stained with turmeric, garlic, and chili, grilled over a street fire.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

In Yangon, street grills sell small pork skewers seasoned with turmeric, garlic, and chili and cooked over charcoal. This page uses that plain description. It does not invent a classical or royal name for a street skewer. The pork is cut in pieces, grilled, and rested like other whole pork; it is everyday food from a city sidewalk, seasoned the way those stalls season it.

Cultural background

Yangon street cooks thread the pork and grill it to order. Turmeric colors the meat. There is no wheat, dairy, or peanut in this mix. The name on this page is a description of the food and the city, not a fake classical title.

Why it tastes this way

Turmeric, garlic, and chili make a direct, savory crust, and pork fat browns over a small charcoal fire. The skewers are small, so the seasoning stays on the surface where you taste it. A long sauce would turn them into a different dish. The street version is the spice and the grill.

How it is made

Toss pork chunks with turmeric, garlic, and chili, grill them on skewers, and rest them.

Authentic touch

Use a light hand with the oil. Too much oil drips, flares, and coats the pork in soot.

How it is used

Eat them hot, with rice and a lime wedge.

Field notes

  • These are Yangon street pork skewers seasoned with turmeric, garlic, and chili.
  • The page uses a plain name for a street food and does not invent a classical title.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds pork shoulder, cut in 1-inch chunks
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 tablespoon lime juice

Steps

  1. Toss the pork with turmeric, garlic, chili, salt, oil, and lime juice. Refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The pork is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Thread the chunks on skewers. Heat a charcoal grill. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center of the thickest chunk reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the pork 3 minutes. Serve hot and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A hotter chili is easy to add. This batch is medium.
  • Chicken pieces would need a poultry temperature. This skewer is pork.

Pairings
