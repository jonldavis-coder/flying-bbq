Cape Snoek Braai

Next stop: Kenyan Nyama Choma · continuing the voyage

Pronounced SNOOK

Oily snoek with apricot jam, a little chili, and braai char.

15 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Snoek is an oily fish braaied on the Cape west coast, often split open and painted with a little apricot and chili near the end. Lambert's Bay is one of the harbors tied to the catch. If snoek is unavailable, a firm oily fish is a different dish. This recipe is written for snoek. The fish is cooked until it flakes. Apricot on snoek is a Cape habit, not a dessert glaze.

Cultural background

West-coast and Cape Town braai cooks grill snoek when the boats land it. A mackerel cooked the same way should be called mackerel. It is not the fish this page is naming.

Why it tastes this way

Snoek is rich and firm, so a hot grid chars the skin while the flesh stays moist. A thin apricot and chili brush sweetens the oil without burying it. Sea fish and a beach or harbor fire are the setting. Substituting another species changes the fat and the flake, which is why the name should change with the fish.

How it is made

Season split snoek, grill it, and brush it lightly with apricot and chili at the end.

Authentic touch

Grill snoek skin-side carefully and add the apricot late so the sugar does not blacken. If you cannot buy snoek, cook a different oily fish and do not call it snoek.

How it is used

With a salad. Sweet potato is a common Cape side.

Field notes

  • Snoek is an oily fish braaied on the Cape coast, often with a little apricot.
  • If snoek is unavailable, a firm oily fish is a different dish.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb snoek, split open
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 2 tablespoons apricot jam
  • 1 teaspoon chili flakes
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar

Steps

  1. Pat the snoek dry. Oil and salt it. Mix jam, chili, and vinegar for a late brush. This recipe is for snoek.
  2. Grill skin-side down over a medium-hot braai, then turn.
  3. Cook until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh flakes. Brush with the apricot mix in the last minute.
  4. Serve hot. A different oily fish would be a different dish. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Mackerel, labeled as mackerel, if snoek cannot be bought. That plate is not snoek.
  • Salt and pepper only, without the apricot brush.

Pairings

  • Sweet potato
  • Green salad
  • Lemon
