Kalua Pig

Next stop: Fijian Lovo · on Pacific earth ovens by boat, by Boat

Pronounced kah-LOO-ah

Very salty, tender shredded pork.

History

Kalua pig is pork cooked in an imu, a Native Hawaiian earth oven of heated stones, traditionally seasoned with salt. An imu is a specific oven and a specific practice. This recipe is a covered pot or oven roast, labeled as not an imu. It is a respectful home stand-in for the salt and the tenderness, and it does not claim that a Dutch oven is the ceremony.

Cultural background

A covered-pot home version of kalua pig. It is not an imu. Native Hawaiian earth-oven cooking is the source tradition.

Why it tastes this way

Salt and a long enclosed cook turn shoulder collagen soft and leave the pork tasting of pork, not of a sweet glaze. A real imu also tastes of the wood, the leaves, and the steam in the ground, which a pot does not reproduce. Saying so is more useful than pretending liquid smoke is a pit.

How it is made

Salt a pork shoulder and cook it covered, with a little water, until it shreds. This is not an imu.

Authentic touch

Call the pot a pot. If you are invited to a real imu, that is someone else's oven and someone else's lead.

How it is used

Shredded, with its juices, beside rice or cabbage. It is salty on purpose.

Field notes

  • An imu is a Hawaiian earth oven of heated stones. A covered pot is not an imu.
  • Kalua pig is seasoned with salt. This home version does not add a sweet glaze.

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in pork shoulder, about 6 pounds
  • 1 tablespoon Hawaiian salt or kosher salt for the fat side
  • 1 tablespoon Hawaiian salt or kosher salt for the meat side
  • 1 cup water

Steps

  1. Score the fat lightly and rub the salt over the shoulder. This covered pot is the home version. It is not an imu.
  2. Put the shoulder in a heavy pot or a roasting pan with the water. Cover tightly.
  3. Cook at 325°F (163°C) until the center reaches about 195–205°F (90–96°C) and the pork shreds. Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest; kalua-style shoulder is taken higher for tenderness.
  4. Rest, shred, and moisten with the pot juices. Tell the table this was an oven, not an earth oven.

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Banana or ti leaves are used in a real imu. They are not required for this covered pot, and the pot is still not an imu if you add them.

Pairings

  • rice
  • cabbage
  • poi
