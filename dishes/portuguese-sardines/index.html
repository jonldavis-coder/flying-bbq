Lisbon June Sardines

Next stop: Greek Easter Spit Lamb · on Mediterranean by balloon, by Hot air balloon

Pronounced sar-DEEN-yehs

Charred sardines, coarse salt, and olive oil, eaten with bread.

15 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Grilled sardines are the street food of Lisbon's June festivals, especially around Santo Antonio. Neighborhoods set up grills, and the fish are salted and cooked whole. The habit is seasonal because sardines are in good condition then. Bread and a simple pepper or tomato salad usually share the plate. It is a city party food with a parish calendar behind it.

Cultural background

Lisbon street and backyard cooks are who make this in June. It is grilled sardines, not a canned-fish salad and not the Malaga beach skewer, though both are sardines over fire.

Why it tastes this way

June sardines are fat enough to baste themselves on a hot grate. Coarse salt and a little smoke are the flavor. The festival timing matches the fish, which is why the month matters. Olive oil after grilling adds fruitiness without a sauce.

How it is made

Grill whole salted sardines over charcoal until the skin chars and the flesh flakes.

Authentic touch

Salt the fish and leave it. Turn once. A crowded grill of sardines is normal at these feasts, so work in batches you can watch.

How it is used

With country bread, grilled peppers, and a salad. Eat them with your hands if the table is a street table.

Field notes

  • Lisbon grills sardines in the street during the June saints festivals.
  • The fish are cooked whole with salt, not in a tomato stew.

Ingredients

  • 12 fresh sardines, gutted
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 lemon
  • Black pepper

Steps

  1. Rinse and dry the sardines. Salt them on both sides.
  2. Heat a charcoal grill until the grate is very hot. Oil the fish lightly.
  3. Grill, turning once, until they reach 145°F (63°C) and flake. The skin should char in spots.
  4. Serve with lemon, bread, and salad. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A home charcoal grill in the same month, when the fish are fresh.
  • A slice of raw onion and a pepper on the side.

Pairings

  • Country bread
  • Grilled peppers
  • Tomato salad
