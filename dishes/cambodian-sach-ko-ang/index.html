Cambodian Sach Ko Ang

Next stop: Yangon Pork Skewers · continuing the voyage

Pronounced sah ko ahng

Beef skewers with lemongrass, fish sauce, and sugar, served with lime.

25 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Sach ko ang is Cambodian grilled beef, often cut small and cooked on skewers over charcoal. Lemongrass, garlic, fish sauce, and a little sugar are a usual marinade in Phnom Penh grill stands. The beef is whole muscle, rested after it reaches temperature. It is street and restaurant food, eaten with rice or as a snack with a sour dip.

Cultural background

Cambodian cooks grill this beef at street fires and in open-front shops. Fish sauce contains fish. There is no wheat and no dairy in the marinade. The pieces stay in chunks, not a mince.

Why it tastes this way

Lemongrass and a little sugar brown on a hot skewer, and fish sauce salts the beef. Lime at the table brightens the crust. The grill is quick because the pieces are small, so the outside chars before the center dries out.

How it is made

Marinate beef with lemongrass, garlic, and fish sauce, grill it on skewers, and rest it.

Authentic touch

Cut the beef across the grain and keep the pieces even. Mixed sizes finish at different times.

How it is used

Eat it with lime, rice, and a small dish of fish sauce with chili if you want heat.

Field notes

  • Sach ko ang is Cambodian grilled beef, often sold on skewers.
  • Lemongrass and fish sauce are a usual marinade at Phnom Penh grills.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds beef sirloin, cut in 1-inch chunks
  • 1 stalk lemongrass, minced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tablespoons fish sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Toss the beef with lemongrass, garlic, fish sauce, sugar, oil, and pepper. Refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The beef is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Thread it on skewers. Heat a grill until it is hot. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center of the thickest chunk reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the beef 3 minutes. Serve with lime. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A prahok dip is served by some cooks and would add more fermented fish. It is not in this marinade.
  • Short ribs can be used if they are cut small enough to grill as a whole cut.

Pairings
