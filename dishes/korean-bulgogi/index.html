Korean Bulgogi

Next stop: Korean Grilled Mackerel · continuing the voyage

Pronounced BOOL-goh-gee

Very thin beef, sweet and savory with soy, pear, and sesame, seared in a minute.

30 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Bulgogi is thinly sliced Korean beef, marinated with soy, sugar, garlic, sesame, and often grated pear or onion, then grilled or seared. The name means fire meat. Home cooks and restaurants both make it, and the thin cut is the modern everyday form. It is not galbi, which is a short rib, and it is not an ancient court menu this page will invent a date for; ordinary soy sauce contains wheat; the beef is a whole muscle, sliced thin, and it is still rested after it reaches temperature.

Cultural background

Korean households keep this marinade for celebrations and ordinary dinners. There is beef, soy, wheat, and sesame. There is no dairy. The slices are thin enough to cook in a very short time on a hot pan or grill.

Why it tastes this way

A thin slice takes the marinade on almost all of its surface, so the soy and pear taste are in every bite. Sesame oil aromatically browns in the fat. High heat sears it before the juice floods the pan. That wide seasoned surface is why bulgogi tastes sweeter and softer than a thick grilled steak with the same soy.

How it is made

Slice beef thin, marinate it in soy, pear, garlic, and sesame, and grill or sear it quickly, then rest it.

Authentic touch

Freeze the beef for 30 minutes so you can slice it thin. Thick slices are a steak, not bulgogi.

How it is used

Eat it with rice, lettuce, and a little ssamjang.

Field notes

  • Bulgogi is thinly sliced beef. Galbi is the short-rib grill.
  • The sweet soy marinade covers almost the whole surface because the slices are thin.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds beef sirloin, sliced very thin
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce
  • 1/2 ripe pear, grated
  • 1/2 onion, grated
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame seeds

Steps

  1. Mix the soy sauce, pear, onion, sugar, garlic, sesame oil, and sesame seeds. Coat the beef and refrigerate it for 20 minutes. The beef is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Heat a grill or a heavy pan until it is very hot. Cook in batches so the meat sears. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Cook until the slices reach at least 145°F (63°C). Thin meat moves fast. Check it. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the beef 3 minutes. Serve with rice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A grill pan on the stove is a fair home method. A charcoal grill is the outdoor one.
  • Onion in the marinade is as common as pear. This recipe uses both.
