Nicaraguan Churrasco

Next stop: Costa Rican Carne Asada · continuing the voyage

Pronounced choo-RAHS-koh

Grilled beef with a simple garlic sauce, beside rice and beans.

20 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Nicaraguan churrasco is a grilled beef steak, often a thin cut, eaten with gallo pinto and a simple garlic sauce. Gallo pinto, rice and beans cooked together, is the everyday plate of Nicaragua. The garlic sauce is a Nicaraguan table sauce of garlic, onion, and acid. It is not the parsley chimichurri of an Argentine asado, and this page does not copy that sauce.

Cultural background

The beef is the grill. The sauce is garlic and sour orange or lime, used the day it is mixed. Gallo pinto is a pairing, not a second recipe buried inside the steak.

Why it tastes this way

Garlic and acid cut a simply grilled steak, and the beans and rice beside it make the meal filling in a way a sauce alone does not. The grill taste stays light because the meat is not buried in herbs. What you remember is beef, garlic, and gallo pinto.

How it is made

Grill beef to a whole-muscle temperature and serve it with a fresh garlic sauce and gallo pinto.

Authentic touch

Mix the garlic sauce the day you eat it. Do not store garlic in oil at room temperature.

How it is used

Serve with gallo pinto, fried plantains, and a cabbage salad. A crumb of queso fresco is a common extra at the table.

Field notes

  • Gallo pinto, rice and beans cooked together, is the usual plate beside this steak.
  • The garlic sauce on this page is a Nicaraguan table sauce, written separately from Argentine chimichurri.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds beef steak
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/4 small onion, minced
  • 3 tablespoons sour orange juice or lime juice
  • 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil, for the sauce

Steps

  1. Salt and oil the steak. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Stir garlic, onion, juice, cilantro, oil, and a pinch of salt. Refrigerate the sauce and use it the same day. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill the steak until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested steak. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Spoon on the garlic sauce and eat it with gallo pinto. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sour orange is the acid when it is sold. Lime juice is the home substitute.
  • A thinner steak cooks faster and still needs the same center temperature.

Pairings

  • gallo pinto
  • fried plantains
  • cabbage salad
