West Texas Mesquite Beef

Next stop: South Texas Barbacoa de Cabeza · continuing the voyage

Seared beef with the sharp, sweet smoke of mesquite.

15 min prep · 1 hr 15 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

West Texas beef over mesquite is a direct-heat cook. Mesquite is the scrub wood of the dry country, and it burns hot. This is not the long offset smoke of a Lockhart brisket, and it should not be stretched into an all-day cook or the outside will bitter before the center is done.

Cultural background

A sirloin roast grilled over mesquite coals. It is a hot-fire beef plate, not Central Texas barbecue and not a smoked sausage.

Why it tastes this way

Mesquite coals run hotter and sweeter-bitter than oak, so the crust picks up a sharper smoke in a short cook. Direct heat browns the surface the way an open West Texas fire does. Salt and pepper are enough because the wood is already loud.

How it is made

Grill a salted beef roast over mesquite coals with the lid managed so the fire stays hot but not flaming.

Authentic touch

Wait until the mesquite has burned down to coals. Flaming mesquite will scorch the roast and taste harsh.

How it is used

Sliced after a rest, with the juices. It can sit beside beans, but it is not chopped into sauce.

Field notes

  • Mesquite burns hot, which is why this West Texas cook is direct heat rather than a long offset smoke.
  • A sirloin roast at this temperature stays sliceable, unlike a brisket taken to tenderness.

Ingredients

  • 1 beef sirloin roast, about 3 pounds
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt
  • 2 teaspoons coarse black pepper
  • Mesquite wood or mesquite charcoal, burned down to coals

Steps

  1. Salt and pepper the roast and let it sit at room temperature no more than a short while before cooking.
  2. Burn mesquite to coals. Spread the coals for direct grilling and keep a cooler edge.
  3. Grill the roast, turning as the crust browns, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Rest at least 3 minutes before slicing. Do not cook this roast to brisket tenderness or it will dry out.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Thick sirloin steaks use the same coals and cook in less time than a roast.

Pairings

  • charred onion
  • salsa
  • beans
