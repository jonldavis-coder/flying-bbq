Chilote Curanto

Next stop: Peruvian Anticuchos · continuing the voyage

Pronounced koo-RAHN-toh

Mussels, pork, chicken, and potatoes cooked together, smoky in a pit and softer in a pot.

40 min prep · 1 hr 30 min cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Curanto is a Chilote earth oven with a long Indigenous history on the archipelago. Chono and Huilliche practice is part of that older root, and later Chilote families kept the pit as a feast of shellfish, meat, and potatoes. Curanto en olla, the home pot on this page, is a pot and is not the earth oven. The foods overlap. The fire does not.

Cultural background

The pot holds pork, chicken, and mussels, so the temperatures are not the same. Pork is a whole cut, chicken is poultry, and mussels are shellfish. Discard any mussel that stays shut. This is a Chilote meal, written for a kitchen pot.

Why it tastes this way

In the ground, hot stones and leaves steam shellfish liquor into the meat and the potatoes. A pot keeps those juices and loses the stone and the leaf. The taste is still sea and pork and potato, which is why the pot is worth cooking, and why it should not be described as the pit.

How it is made

Cook pork, chicken, potatoes, and mussels in a covered pot, each to its own temperature. Call it curanto en olla, not a pit.

Authentic touch

Layer potatoes under the meat so they take the drippings, and do not stir once the mussels go in.

How it is used

Bring the pot to the table with a green salad. The broth at the bottom is part of the meal.

Field notes

  • Curanto is a Chilote earth oven with a long Indigenous history on the Chiloé archipelago.
  • Curanto en olla is a home pot and is not that earth oven.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds pork shoulder, in large chunks
  • 1 1/2 pounds bone-in chicken thighs
  • 2 pounds mussels, scrubbed, beards removed
  • 2 pounds potatoes, in large chunks
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 4 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup water

Steps

  1. Put potatoes, onion, garlic, salt, and water in a heavy pot. Add the pork and chicken. The meats are raw and are not ready to eat. This pot is curanto en olla, not an earth oven. Pork needs at least 145°F (63°C) and a 3-minute rest, chicken needs 165°F (74°C), and mussels need to open with opaque flesh at 145°F (63°C).
  2. Cover and simmer until the pork reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and the chicken reaches 165°F (74°C) away from the bone. Rest the pork 3 minutes if you remove it. Keep the chicken at 165°F (74°C).
  3. Add the mussels, cover again, and cook until they open and the flesh is opaque and pearly, 145°F (63°C). Discard any mussel that stays closed.
  4. Check the pork again at 145°F (63°C) or hotter and the chicken at 165°F (74°C) before serving. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest it 3 minutes. Cook chicken to 165°F (74°C). Cook mussels until the shells open and the flesh is opaque, about 145°F (63°C), and discard any that stay closed.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Clams can join the mussels. They are shellfish and are done when open and opaque, at 145°F (63°C).
  • A real pit is a different project. This recipe does not pretend to be one.
