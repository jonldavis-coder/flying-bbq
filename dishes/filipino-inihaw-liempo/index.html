Filipino Inihaw Liempo

Next stop: Bacolod Chicken Inasal · continuing the voyage

Pronounced ee-nee-HOW LYEM-poh

Grilled pork belly with soy, calamansi, garlic, and a sour dip.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Inihaw na liempo is Filipino grilled pork belly. Inihaw means grilled, and liempo is the belly. Home cooks and roadside grills in Manila marinate it with soy sauce, calamansi or vinegar, and garlic, then cook it over charcoal. Soy sauce brings soy and, in ordinary brands, wheat; the belly is a whole cut, rested after it reaches temperature; it is not a whole lechon.

Cultural background

Filipino cooks grill belly for family meals and small stands. The marinade has soy and wheat. There is no dairy. A vinegar dip is served at the table so each person can sour the slice.

Why it tastes this way

Belly is fat and meat in layers, so a hot grill crisps the fat while the lean stays juicy. Soy and calamansi season that fat with salt and acid. Garlic chars at the edge. The dip is sharp on purpose, because the meat is rich.

How it is made

Marinate pork belly in soy, calamansi, and garlic, grill it, and rest it before slicing.

Authentic touch

Score the skin side lightly and watch for flare-ups. Burning the fat black makes the belly bitter.

How it is used

Slice it and eat it with rice and a vinegar dip.

Field notes

  • Liempo is pork belly. Inihaw means it is grilled.
  • Ordinary soy sauce contains wheat, so this marinade is not gluten-free.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds skin-on pork belly, in a slab
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce
  • 3 tablespoons calamansi juice, or lime juice
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Score the skin lightly. Rub the belly with soy sauce, calamansi, garlic, pepper, and oil. Refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The pork is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Heat a charcoal grill to medium, with a cooler side for flare-ups. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill, turning, until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the belly 3 minutes, then slice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sprite or lemon-lime soda is a popular sweet marinade and is not in this one.
  • A gluten-free soy sauce would change the wheat allergen. This uses ordinary soy sauce.
