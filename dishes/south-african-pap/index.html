South African Pap

Next stop: South African Chakalaka · continuing the voyage

Pronounced pap

Stiff white maize porridge, mild and ready for a relish or a grill.

5 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Pap is the South African name, from Afrikaans, for a maize porridge served beside a braai. Stiff pap is firm enough to eat with the fingers or to set under a relish. Neighboring staples such as sadza, nshima, and ugali are related and are not the same plate. This recipe is maize meal, water, and salt, with a little oil. It is a side, not the chop.

Cultural background

South African households make pap for the braai and for ordinary meals. It is vegetarian food as written. It is not a sweet breakfast porridge unless you change it on purpose.

Why it tastes this way

Maize meal swells in salted water into a mild, toasty starch that carries chakalaka, gravy, or juices from the grill. The stiffness is a choice: loose pap is a different texture. The grain is a southern African staple with a long history in the region, and the braai made the stiff version a cookout side. Oil at the end keeps it from drying into crumbs.

How it is made

Boil salted water, stir in maize meal, and cook until the porridge is thick and the raw taste is gone.

Authentic touch

Add the meal in a stream while stirring so it does not lump, then let it steam covered. Stir stiff pap with a strong spoon.

How it is used

Beside the grill or with chakalaka. It is a side.

Field notes

  • Pap is a maize porridge and a standard braai side in South Africa.
  • Sadza, nshima, and ugali are related neighboring staples and are not the same plate.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups white maize meal
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Boil the water with the salt. Pour in the maize meal in a stream, stirring.
  2. Lower the heat, cover, and cook, stirring often, until the porridge is thick and no longer tastes raw. This can take about 30 minutes.
  3. Stir in the oil. There is no animal-protein thermometer target. The side is done when it is thick and cooked through.
  4. Serve hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. This is not a canning recipe.

Variations

  • A softer porridge with more water, for a different texture.
  • A spoon of pan juices on top, which would change the pot and is not in this recipe.
