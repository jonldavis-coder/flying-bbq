Samoan Umu

Next stop: Tongan Umu · on Pacific earth ovens by boat, by Boat

Pronounced OO-moo

Pork shoulder steamed with canned coconut milk and spinach, rich and salty.

30 min prep · 4 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

An umu is a Samoan earth oven, used for Sundays and family gatherings, with pork and parcels of coconut and leaves. This home pot uses canned coconut milk and spinach when taro leaves are not sold, and it does not use raw cassava. A covered pot is not an umu pit. The pork is a shoulder, cooked until it is safe and tender enough to slice.

Cultural background

Samoan cooks run an umu for household gatherings. This recipe is the home covered cook: pork, canned coconut, and spinach. There is no wheat and no dairy. Coconut is not a tree nut in this allergen list. Do not grate raw cassava into the pot.

Why it tastes this way

Pork fat and coconut milk are both rich, and a closed pot keeps them together so the greens taste savory. Taro leaves have a mineral taste. Spinach is the shop substitute and is milder, which is an honest swap when the leaf is not sold. The umu pit adds smoke and leaf. The pot still gives you the coconut pork that is the edible idea of the parcel.

How it is made

Cook a pork shoulder with canned coconut milk and spinach in a covered pot until it is safe and tender. Do not use raw cassava.

Authentic touch

Shake the can of coconut milk so the fat is mixed through. A separated can makes a watery pot and a lump of coconut fat.

How it is used

Slice the pork and serve it with the greens and the coconut juice.

Field notes

  • A Samoan umu is an earth oven. This recipe is a covered pot with canned coconut milk.
  • Spinach stands in for taro leaves when the leaves are not sold. Raw cassava is not used.

Ingredients

  • 1 boneless pork shoulder, about 4 pounds
  • 1 can coconut milk, 13.5 ounces
  • 8 cups spinach
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1/2 cup water

Steps

  1. Rub the pork with oil and salt. Put it in a heavy pot with onion, spinach, coconut milk, and water. Do not add cassava. The pork is still raw. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  2. Cover the pot. This is a home cook, not a Samoan umu pit. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  3. Cook over low heat until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C). For pork you want tender, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C). Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  4. Rest it, then slice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Taro leaves can replace the spinach when you can buy them as food. Do not use an unknown garden leaf.
  • A Tongan umu uses different foods. It is a different page.
