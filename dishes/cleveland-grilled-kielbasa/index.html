Cleveland Grilled Kielbasa

Next stop: Indiana Fair Pork Chop · continuing the voyage

Pronounced keel-BAH-sah

Grilled kielbasa with sharp mustard and sour sauerkraut.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Cleveland's Polish American tables grill kielbasa and eat it with mustard and sauerkraut. This plate does not require a beer simmer, which is how it differs from Sheboygan brats and from the Pennsylvania coal-region simmer. Sausage labels vary. Use a kielbasa whose label lists no wheat if you need the plate to stay gluten-free, and still read every line of the package.

Cultural background

Grilled sausage with mustard and kraut, no beer in the recipe. A Cleveland Polish American plate, not a Pennsylvania water simmer.

Why it tastes this way

Mustard and fermented cabbage are the sharp side of a salty smoked sausage, a pairing carried by Polish American groceries and church festivals. The grill only browns the casing. Without beer, nothing in the written method adds wheat besides what the sausage package might already contain.

How it is made

Grill kielbasa and serve it with mustard and warmed sauerkraut. No beer is used.

Authentic touch

Heat a cooked kielbasa through. Do not assume every brand is fresh, gluten-free, or free of milk. The label decides.

How it is used

Sliced, with kraut and mustard. A rye bun would add wheat; this recipe does not include the bun.

Field notes

  • This Cleveland plate is grilled sausage with mustard and sauerkraut, and the recipe uses no beer.
  • It is a different cook from Pennsylvania's simmered kielbasa and from Sheboygan's beer brats.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds smoked kielbasa whose label lists no wheat and no cheese
  • 1 pound sauerkraut, rinsed
  • 2 tablespoons yellow mustard
  • A grill

Steps

  1. Read the sausage label. This recipe is gluten-free only when the package lists no wheat. Brands differ. No beer is used.
  2. Warm the sauerkraut in a small pot. Heat a grill to medium.
  3. Grill the kielbasa until it browns. If it is fully cooked smoked sausage, heat the center to 165°F (74°C). If it is fresh and raw, cook it to 160°F (71°C).
  4. Serve with mustard and kraut. Mustard is an allergen.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • If the only kielbasa you can buy lists wheat, drop the gluten-free claim and follow the label.
