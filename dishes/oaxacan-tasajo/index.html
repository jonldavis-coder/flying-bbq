Oaxacan Tasajo

Next stop: Yucatán Tikin Xic · continuing the voyage

Pronounced tah-SAH-ho

Thin salted beef, grilled quickly, chewy at the edge and beefy in the middle.

20 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Tasajo is thin salted beef sold in Oaxaca markets and grilled to order. Salting and drying were how butchers kept beef before refrigeration, and the grilled slice became a market breakfast and a fonda plate. This home recipe does not ask you to cure meat in the sun. Buy market tasajo if you can, or salt a thin steak briefly in the refrigerator.

Cultural background

Oaxacan tasajo is beef, not pork cecina from the same market stall, though the two are often grilled side by side. It is a hot, fast grill. It is not a long smoke.

Why it tastes this way

Salt concentrates the beef, and the thin cut takes a hard sear before the inside dries out. The Central Valleys grill is charcoal, close to the meat, so the surface browns while the slice stays small. Onion and salsa are the fresh contrast buyers expect at the stand.

How it is made

Grill thin salted beef hot and fast to a whole-muscle temperature. Do not sun-dry meat at home.

Authentic touch

Keep the slice thin. A thick steak salted for an hour is only a seasoned steak, not market tasajo.

How it is used

Eat it with corn tortillas, grilled spring onion, salsa, and a radish. Cecina and chorizo on the same grill are separate meats.

Field notes

  • Tasajo is beef that Oaxaca markets sell already salted and thin for the grill.
  • This recipe does not include home curing or sun-drying.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds beef skirt or flank, sliced 1/4 inch thick
  • 2 teaspoons salt, for a short refrigerator seasoning, or 1 pound purchased tasajo
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 spring onions
  • Lime wedges

Steps

  1. If you are not using market tasajo, salt the thin beef and refrigerate it for 1 hour, then pat it dry. That is seasoning, not a cure. Do not dry it in the sun. The meat is still raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Oil the slices lightly. Grill the spring onions beside them. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill the beef until the thickest slice reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. The rested slices should have reached 145°F (63°C). Eat them hot with lime. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • If you buy prepared tasajo, rinse the surface salt if it looks heavy, then grill it.
  • A skirt steak sliced thin is the home stand-in, and it should be named as a stand-in.

Pairings

  • corn tortillas
  • grilled spring onion
  • salsa
  • radishes
