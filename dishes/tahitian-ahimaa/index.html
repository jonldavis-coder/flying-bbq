Tahitian Ahimaa

Next stop: Vanuatu Laplap Packet · continuing the voyage

Pronounced ah-hee-MAH

Pork shoulder with coconut milk, sweet potato, and ripe banana, steamed in a covered pot.

30 min prep · 4 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

An ahima'a is a Tahitian earth oven, a pit of heated stones used to steam pork, breadfruit, and banana for gatherings. This page is a covered home pot, not that pit and not a ceremony. The foods are pork shoulder, canned coconut milk, sweet potato, and a ripe eating banana. There is no raw cassava; breadfruit can replace the sweet potato when you can buy it ripe or already cooked, not as a raw project; the pork is cooked until it is safe and tender.

Cultural background

Tahitian cooks prepare an ahima'a for feasts. The home pot uses the same idea of pork, coconut, and a sweet root or fruit. There is no wheat and no dairy. Name it as a home cook. Papeete is the city on this page, not the whole of the islands' practice.

Why it tastes this way

Pork fat, coconut, and a ripe banana make a sweet and savory steam, and sweet potato drinks the juice. An earth oven adds the smell of leaves and hot stone. A covered pot keeps the moisture, which is the part a kitchen can do. The banana should be ripe and eaten as a fruit, not a starch you processed from a bitter root.

How it is made

Cook pork shoulder with canned coconut milk, sweet potato, and ripe banana in a covered pot until the pork is safe and tender.

Authentic touch

Use a ripe yellow banana, peeled. A green bitter starch is not the fruit this pot is asking for.

How it is used

Slice the pork and serve it with the sweet potato and banana.

Field notes

  • An ahima'a is a Tahitian earth oven. This recipe is a covered home pot.
  • The pot uses sweet potato, canned coconut, and a ripe banana, not raw cassava.

Ingredients

  • 1 boneless pork shoulder, about 4 pounds
  • 1 can coconut milk, 13.5 ounces
  • 1.5 pounds sweet potato, peeled and chunked
  • 2 ripe bananas, peeled and halved
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1/2 cup water

Steps

  1. Put the pork, coconut milk, sweet potato, bananas, onion, salt, and water in a heavy pot. Do not add cassava. The pork is still raw. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  2. Cover the pot. This is a home cook, not a Tahitian ahima'a pit and not a ceremony. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  3. Cook over low heat until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C). For pork you want tender, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C). Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).
  4. Rest it, then slice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook the pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. For meat you want to pull, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C).

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ripe breadfruit can replace the sweet potato when a shop sells it ready to cook.
  • A home oven with the pot covered is still a pot, not a pit.
