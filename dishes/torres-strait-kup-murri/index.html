Torres Strait Kup Murri

Next stop: Maori Hangi · continuing the voyage

Pronounced kup MUR-ee

Chicken and sweet potato steamed with chili, garlic, and leaf, in a covered pot.

30 min prep · 1 hr 30 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Kup murri is a Torres Strait earth oven: food layered with hot stones and leaves and covered so it steams. Torres Strait Islander cooks prepare it for community gatherings. A backyard pot is not that oven, and it is not a ceremony. This page uses a heavy covered pot with chicken, sweet potato, and chili, and it says so; the chicken is cooked to a poultry temperature; there is no raw cassava in the pot.

Cultural background

Torres Strait Islander families on islands such as Thursday Island have their own earth-oven practice. The written recipe is a home covered cook for people who are not holding that ceremony. There is chicken, and no wheat, dairy, or peanut. Treat the pot as dinner, not as the community oven.

Why it tastes this way

A closed pot steams chicken and sweet potato together, so the tuber takes on the meat juices and the chili and garlic perfume the steam. An earth oven does this against hot stones and leaves. The home pot can echo the steam and the seasoning. It cannot stand in for the gathering, and it should not be described as if it did.

How it is made

Season chicken and sweet potato, seal them in a heavy covered pot, and cook until the chicken is done. Call this a home pot, not a ceremony.

Authentic touch

Keep the lid on. If you stir it often, you are boiling a stew, not steaming a closed cook.

How it is used

Serve the chicken and sweet potato together, with the juices.

Field notes

  • Kup murri is a Torres Strait earth oven used for community gatherings.
  • A home covered pot is not that oven and is not a ceremony.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds chicken pieces
  • 2 pounds sweet potato, peeled and cut in chunks
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 hot chili, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1/2 cup water

Steps

  1. Toss the chicken with onion, garlic, chili, salt, and oil. The chicken is still raw. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. Put the chicken and sweet potato in a heavy pot with the water. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Cover the pot tightly. This is a home pot. It is not a Torres Strait ceremony and it is not an earth oven. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Cook over medium heat, lid on, until the thickest chicken piece reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone, and the sweet potato is tender. Do not serve the chicken rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest a few minutes and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Banana leaves can line the pot when they are sold. Parchment is only a liner.
  • Fish can replace chicken in some cooks and would need a fish temperature. This pot is chicken.
