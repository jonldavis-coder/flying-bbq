Venezuelan Carne en Vara

Pronounced KAR-neh en VAH-rah

Beef cooked on sticks beside a wood fire, salted and smoky at the edge.

20 min prep · 1 hr 10 min cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Carne en vara is beef cooked on sticks beside a wood fire in the Venezuelan llanos. The sticks hold large pieces next to the heat rather than directly in a flame. It shares the plains with Colombian carne a la llanera and it is written here as its own dish, with its own salt and its own pace. Yuca and a mashed avocado sauce are common at the table.

Cultural background

A home roast on skewers or on a spit beside coals is the workable size. A whole side of beef is the countryside version and is not what this ingredient list buys. The meat is whole beef.

Why it tastes this way

Cooking beside the fire, instead of over it, dries the surface slowly and keeps a beef taste without a heavy crust of spices. Wood smoke is light. Salt is the seasoning. The llanos are cattle country, and the plate tastes like that plain fire.

How it is made

Salt large pieces of beef and cook them on sticks beside a wood fire to a whole-beef temperature.

Authentic touch

Plant the sticks so you can turn them. A piece that faces the fire the whole time will burn before the far side is cooked.

How it is used

Slice the beef and eat it with yuca. Guasacaca, an avocado sauce, is a pairing and is not required for the meat to be done.

Field notes

  • In the Venezuelan llanos the meat is cooked on sticks beside a wood fire.
  • It is a plains beef cook, related in landscape to Colombian carne a la llanera and written as its own dish.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds beef, in large thick pieces
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • Strong sticks, long skewers, or a spit

Steps

  1. Oil the beef and salt it. It is raw and is not ready to eat. Fix it on sticks or a spit. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Build a wood fire and cook the meat beside the coals, not in the flames. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Turn the sticks until the center of the thickest piece reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested beef. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A rotisserie beside a fire is a fair home tool. Say if you are using one.
  • A thinner steak over the coals is closer to a grill than to carne en vara. Keep the pieces large.

Pairings

  • yuca
  • guasacaca
  • arepa
