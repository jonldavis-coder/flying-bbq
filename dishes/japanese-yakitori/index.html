Japanese Yakitori

Pronounced yah-kee-TOH-ree

Chicken thigh on small skewers, salted, with a clean charcoal taste.

History

Yakitori is chicken grilled on small skewers over charcoal, a specialty of Japanese shops and of alley stands in Tokyo. Salt and a sweet soy tare are the two usual finishes. This page is the salted chicken, shio, so the meat itself has no soy sauce. The tare is a separate sauce; the chicken is cooked through; thigh is the usual piece because it stays juicy on a small skewer.

Cultural background

Yakitori cooks tend a narrow charcoal grill and turn the skewers constantly. There is chicken, salt, and a little oil. There is no wheat and no dairy on this salted version. It is not the ground chicken tsukune and not a whole roast bird.

Why it tastes this way

Small pieces of thigh pick up charcoal bitterness at the skin and stay juicy inside. Salt lets that chicken taste show. A tare would add sweetness and soy. Serving the salt skewers plain is how a shop shows the fire. The tare can still sit on the counter for people who want it.

How it is made

Salt chicken thigh pieces, skewer them, and grill them over charcoal until the center is done.

Authentic touch

Use flat or thin skewers and leave a little space between pieces. Packed meat steams.

How it is used

Eat the skewers hot. A tare can be offered from the separate recipe if you want the sweet glaze.

Field notes

  • Yakitori shops usually offer both salt and a sweet soy tare.
  • This page is the salted skewer, so the chicken itself has no soy sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds boneless chicken thighs, cut in bite-size pieces
  • 1.5 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 8 small bamboo or metal skewers
  • 4 scallions, cut in 1-inch lengths

Steps

  1. Toss the chicken with salt and oil. Thread it on skewers with a piece of scallion here and there. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Heat a charcoal grill until the coals are hot and the grate is close to them. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Grill, turning often, until the center of the thickest piece reaches 165°F (74°C). Do not serve it rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest a minute and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Scallion between the chicken pieces is a common skewer called negima.
  • Brushing on tare makes the other classic finish and adds soy and wheat.

Pairings

  • scallion
  • rice
  • pickled ginger
