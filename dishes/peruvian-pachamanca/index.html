Andean Pachamanca

Next stop: Bolivian Pacumutu · continuing the voyage

Pronounced pah-chah-MAHN-kah

Pork and chicken steamed with herbs, potato, and corn, concentrated and soft.

40 min prep · 2 hr 30 min cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Pachamanca is an Andean earth oven. The name is Quechua. Quechua communities and other Andean communities cook it, and the meats, herbs, and hot stones vary by valley. A covered pan in a home oven is not a pachamanca. This page says so, and it still gives a workable packet with temperatures for pork and for chicken.

Cultural background

Huacatay and chincho are herbs some valleys use. Mint is a home stand-in and is not those herbs. The pork and the chicken have different safe temperatures. Potatoes and corn cook in the same pot and do not change those numbers.

Why it tastes this way

An earth oven steams meat against hot stones and herbs, so the flavor is juicy and herbal rather than charred. Valley by valley, the herb changes. A home pot keeps garlic, cumin, and mint against the meat and misses the stone. That is a real difference, and it is the reason to name the method honestly.

How it is made

Cook pork and chicken with potatoes in a covered home pot until each meat is at its own temperature. Do not call the pot a pachamanca.

Authentic touch

Keep the lid on. Every time you check early, you lose the steam that makes the packet taste like anything but a roast.

How it is used

Open the pot at the table and eat the meats with the potatoes and corn.

Field notes

  • Pachamanca is an Andean earth oven, and the name is Quechua.
  • Practices vary by community. A home oven packet is not a pachamanca.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds pork shoulder, in large chunks
  • 2 pounds bone-in chicken thighs
  • 2 pounds potatoes, in chunks
  • 2 ears of corn, cut into rounds
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup chopped mint, or huacatay if you know that herb
  • 1 cup water

Steps

  1. Toss the pork and chicken with garlic, cumin, salt, and mint. They are raw and are not ready to eat. This covered pot is not a pachamanca. The pork is not ready to eat until it reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and rests 3 minutes, and the chicken is not ready until it reaches 165°F (74°C).
  2. Layer potatoes and corn in a heavy pot, add the meat and the water, and cover tightly. The pork is not ready to eat until it reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and rests 3 minutes, and the chicken is not ready until it reaches 165°F (74°C).
  3. Cook at a steady simmer or in a 325°F (165°C) oven until the pork reads at least 145°F (63°C) and the chicken reads 165°F (74°C) away from the bone. Rest the pork 3 minutes.
  4. Both temperatures should already be met before you serve. For pork you want softer, continue past 145°F (63°C). Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest it 3 minutes. Cook chicken to 165°F (74°C). A home pot is not a pachamanca.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lamb in the same pot would be a whole cut at 145°F (63°C) and a 3-minute rest. This written recipe is pork and chicken.
  • Huacatay, if you know the herb, can replace the mint. Say which leaf you used.
