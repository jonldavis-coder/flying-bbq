Roosterkoek

Pronounced ROO-ster-kook

Grilled bread dough with a browned crust and a soft interior.

History

Roosterkoek is bread dough cooked on a braai grid in South Africa. The Afrikaans name means grill cake. It is a side at cookouts, torn and eaten beside the fire. The dough is wheat, yeast, water, salt, and oil. It is vegetarian. It is not a vetkoek fried in deep fat, which is a different bread.

Cultural background

South African braai cooks bake these on the grid while the coals are still useful. The food is bread. It is not the chop and not a sweet cake, despite the word koek.

Why it tastes this way

Dough on a grid browns where it touches the metal and stays soft inside if the coals are moderate. A little smoke clings to the crust. Wheat and salt are the taste, which is why it works next to a salty grill and a sharp relish. It is camp bread: made because the fire is already going.

How it is made

Make a simple yeast dough, shape rounds, and grill them on a braai grid until they are cooked through.

Authentic touch

Cook over coals, not flames, and turn the cakes when they have a proper crust. Split one to be sure the center is not doughy.

How it is used

Hot, torn open, beside the braai. A dairy spread is not in this dough.

Field notes

  • Roosterkoek is bread dough cooked on a South African braai grid.
  • The Afrikaans name means grill cake, and the bread is not the same as deep-fried vetkoek.

Ingredients

  • 4 cups wheat flour
  • 1 1/2 cups water
  • 1 teaspoon instant yeast
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 teaspoon sugar

Steps

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, salt, sugar, oil, and water into a dough. Knead until smooth. Cover and let it rise until puffy, about an hour.
  2. Divide into eight rounds. Let them rest 15 minutes. Heat a braai to moderate coals.
  3. Grill the rounds, turning, until both sides are browned and the center is cooked through, not raw dough. There is no animal-protein thermometer target.
  4. Serve hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. This is not a canning recipe.

Variations

  • A little crushed garlic on the hot bread after grilling.
  • Smaller rounds if the grid is crowded.

Pairings
