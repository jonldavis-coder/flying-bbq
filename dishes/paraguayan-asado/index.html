Paraguayan Asado

Next stop: Patagonian Cordero al Asador · continuing the voyage

Pronounced ah-SAH-doh

Beef grilled over wood or coals, salted, with yuca on the plate.

20 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

A Paraguayan asado is beef on a grill, eaten with yuca and often with sopa paraguaya or chipa at the same table. It belongs to Paraguay's own Sunday fire, in a country that also speaks Guaraní and Spanish. It is not a renamed Argentine parrilla, even though the word asado is shared. The meat is salted and cooked over coals.

Cultural background

The recipe is the beef. Yuca is the pairing that makes the plate Paraguayan in this file. Chipa and sopa paraguaya are corn and cheese breads and are not cooked here, so this meat recipe has no wheat and no dairy.

Why it tastes this way

Coals and salt give the beef a clear grilled taste. Boiled or roasted yuca beside it is mild and filling, which changes the meal from a steak-and-chimichurri plate. The fire is the same family of southern South American grills, and the starch beside it is the local difference.

How it is made

Salt beef and grill it over coals to a whole-beef temperature, then serve it with yuca.

Authentic touch

Let the beef rest before you slice it. The juices matter because the plate is not covered in sauce.

How it is used

Slice the beef and eat it with boiled yuca and a simple onion and tomato salad.

Field notes

  • A Paraguayan asado is commonly eaten with yuca.
  • It is a grill in Paraguay, not a renamed Argentine parrilla.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds beef steak or a small roast
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • Charcoal or wood burned to coals

Steps

  1. Oil and salt the beef. It is raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Grill it over coals. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Cook until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested beef. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Eat it with yuca. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A rack of beef ribs uses the same salt and the same 145°F (63°C) line.
  • Chipa at the table is a cheese bread and would add dairy and, depending on the recipe, wheat. It is not in this meat.

Pairings

  • boiled yuca
  • onion and tomato salad
  • chipa
