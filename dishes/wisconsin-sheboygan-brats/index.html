Sheboygan Bratwurst

Next stop: Door County Fish Boil · continuing the voyage

Pork bratwurst with a beer simmer and a browned grill casing.

5 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Sheboygan's brat custom is a simmer in beer, then a turn on the grill. German-speaking sausage makers shaped Wisconsin bratwurst, and the two-step cook became the local picnic method. This recipe uses ordinary beer, so it is not gluten-free. It is a different plate from Pennsylvania kielbasa simmered in water and from Cleveland sausage with mustard and kraut.

Cultural background

Fresh pork brats, beer-simmered and grilled. The beer is regular beer. Packaged brats should be plain, without cheese.

Why it tastes this way

Beer adds a grain bitterness that water does not, and the simmer keeps the casing from splitting before the grill browns it. Pork and spice in the brat stay the main flavor. The Lake Michigan picnic habit is a long afternoon of this two-step, not a smokehouse cure.

How it is made

Simmer fresh brats in beer and onions, then grill them to a safe center temperature.

Authentic touch

Discard the beer after simmering. It has touched raw sausage and is not a broth to serve.

How it is used

On a hard roll with mustard and onion if you want them. The roll and the beer both contain wheat. Mustard would add a mustard allergen.

Field notes

  • Sheboygan brats are simmered in beer and then grilled, which is the local two-step.
  • Ordinary beer contains gluten, so this recipe is not gluten-free.

Ingredients

  • 8 plain fresh pork bratwursts, no cheese
  • 2 bottles regular beer, 12 ounces each
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 8 hard rolls

Steps

  1. Put the brats, beer, and onion in a pot and simmer about 15 minutes. Do not boil so hard that the casings split. The beer contains wheat.
  2. Discard the beer and onion. Heat a grill to medium.
  3. Grill the brats, turning, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C) and the casing is browned.
  4. Serve on rolls, which also contain wheat. This plate is not gluten-free.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A gluten-free beer would change the allergen claim only if the brat label is also free of wheat. This written recipe uses regular beer.
