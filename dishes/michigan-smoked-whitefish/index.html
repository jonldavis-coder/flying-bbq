Michigan Smoked Whitefish

Next stop: Cleveland Grilled Kielbasa · continuing the voyage

Hot-smoked whitefish, salty at the surface and moist inside.

15 min prep · 1 hr 30 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Anishinaabe communities and later commercial smokehouses both have relationships with Great Lakes whitefish, and those practices are not the same. This recipe is a home hot-smoke to 145°F. It is not a treaty fishery, not a commercial license, and not a traditional preparation claimed for any nation. Leland's fish shacks are one place a traveler sees the commercial smoke.

Cultural background

A home hot-smoked whitefish. It does not stand in for Anishinaabe fishing practice or for a shop's cold-smoke formula.

Why it tastes this way

Whitefish is rich enough to take smoke without a heavy glaze. Salt on the surface and a moderate pit set the flake. A hot smoke cooks the fish; a cold smoke would be a preserving method this page does not teach.

How it is made

Salt whitefish and hot-smoke it until it flakes and reaches a safe temperature.

Authentic touch

Buy the fish from a legal seller. Smoking it at home does not make the cook part of a treaty harvest.

How it is used

Warm or slightly cooled, with potatoes or lemon. Bread would add wheat and is not in this recipe. Eat it within a few days from the refrigerator.

Field notes

  • Great Lakes whitefish is both an Anishinaabe food and a later commercial smokehouse fish, and those practices differ.
  • This page is a home hot-smoke to 145°F, not a treaty fishery and not a curing manual.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds whitefish fillets
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 lemon
  • Hardwood chunks for a hot smoke

Steps

  1. Salt and pepper the fillets. Refrigerate them while the smoker comes to about 250°F (121°C). This is a hot smoke, not a cure.
  2. Smoke over indirect heat, with a clean fire rather than wet, smoldering chips.
  3. Cook until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the fish is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve warm with lemon. This cook is not a commercial or treaty fishery.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Fillets smoke faster than a dressed whole fish. Measure the thickest part either way.

Pairings

  • potatoes
  • lemon
  • green salad
