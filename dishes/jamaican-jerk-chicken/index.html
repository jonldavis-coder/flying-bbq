Jamaican Jerk Chicken

Next stop: Haitian Boukannen · on Caribbean by boat, by Boat

Chicken with allspice, thyme, and Scotch bonnet, smoky if the fire has pimento wood.

35 min prep · 55 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Jerk is tied to Maroon communities in Jamaica, people of African descent who escaped slavery and cooked seasoned meat over pimento wood in the hills. Allspice is the berry of that pimento tree, native to the Caribbean region, and Scotch bonnet is a New World chile. Chicken is the piece most cooks grill now. The older pork shoulder is a separate, longer cook.

Cultural background

The paste is scallion, thyme, allspice, garlic, ginger, and Scotch bonnet. Pimento wood is the traditional smoke. A charcoal grill without that wood is a home fire, and it is not the same smoke. The chicken still has to reach a poultry temperature.

Why it tastes this way

Pimento wood is the allspice tree, so the smoke repeats the spice ground into the paste. Thyme and scallion are green and sharp, and Scotch bonnet brings a high, fruity heat. Chicken fat carries those flavors across a shorter cook than a pork shoulder can stand.

How it is made

Rub chicken with a jerk paste and grill it over coals until the thickest meat reaches a poultry temperature.

Authentic touch

Burn pimento wood down to coals if you can get it. If you cannot, say the grill is not a pimento fire, and keep the allspice in the paste.

How it is used

Eat it with festival dumplings or bread, roasted breadfruit, and a cold drink. The heat is in the pepper.

Field notes

  • Scotch bonnet is a Caribbean chile, Capsicum chinense.
  • Pimento is the Jamaican name for the allspice tree, and its wood is the traditional jerk smoke.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 4 pounds, cut into 8 pieces
  • 6 scallions, chopped
  • 4 sprigs fresh thyme, leaves stripped
  • 1 tablespoon ground allspice
  • 1 Scotch bonnet, seeded
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tablespoon fresh ginger
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 2 tablespoons lime juice

Steps

  1. Blend scallions, thyme, allspice, Scotch bonnet, garlic, ginger, salt, oil, and lime juice into a paste. Wear gloves with the pepper. Coat the chicken. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. The poultry is not ready to eat until the thickest part reaches 165°F (74°C).
  2. Refrigerate the coated chicken if it sits. Do not leave the garlic paste at room temperature. The poultry is not ready to eat until the thickest part reaches 165°F (74°C).
  3. Grill over coals, ideally with pimento wood if you have it, turning often, until the thickest piece reads 165°F (74°C) away from the bone. Juices should run clear.
  4. Rest the chicken a few minutes. Every piece should have reached 165°F (74°C). Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Seed the Scotch bonnet for a hot paste. Leaving the seeds in pushes it hotter.
  • A covered charcoal grill is the home tool. It is not a pimento-wood pit unless that wood is on the fire.

Pairings

  • festival dumplings
  • roast breadfruit
  • rice and peas
