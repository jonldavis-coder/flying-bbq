Kakori Kebab

Next stop: Bihari Kebab · continuing the voyage

Pronounced kuh-KOH-ree keh-BAB

Very finely minced lamb, pale and soft, with cream, white pepper, and warm spice.

40 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Kakori kebab is named for Kakori, near Lucknow, and it belongs to Awadhi cooking. The lamb is minced very fine, finer than a standard seekh, and it is seasoned with warm spice rather than a harsh chili heat. Cream is part of many kitchen versions, which makes the kebab dairy. It can be cooked on a skewer or on a flat griddle; this is the home version of that Lucknow kebab, not a single undisputed origin tale.

Cultural background

Awadhi cooks in Kakori and Lucknow are the people tied to this kebab. It is ground lamb, cooked through, with cream. It is not a chunky boti and not a coarse seekh.

Why it tastes this way

Fine mincing changes the texture: the kebab stays soft instead of coarse. Cream and lamb fat keep it tender, and cardamom and white pepper perfume it without a red-chili crust. On a skewer or a hot griddle the outside browns just enough to smell like a grill.

How it is made

Work lamb and cream into a fine paste, shape it on skewers or as small cakes, and cook it on a grill or a griddle until the center is done.

Authentic touch

Mince twice, or chop by hand until the meat is almost a paste. Coarse mince is a seekh, not a kakori.

How it is used

Serve it with onion and a little mint. The chili heat is mild on purpose.

Field notes

  • The kebab is associated with Kakori, a town near Lucknow, and with Awadhi cooking.
  • The meat is minced finer than a typical seekh kebab.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds lamb, minced twice until very fine
  • 3 tablespoons heavy cream
  • 1 small onion, boiled and mashed, water pressed out
  • 1 teaspoon ground cardamom
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground white pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced

Steps

  1. Mix the fine lamb, cream, onion, cardamom, white pepper, salt, ginger, and garlic into a smooth paste. Keep it cold. It is still raw. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  2. Shape the paste on flat skewers, or form small cakes for a griddle. Refrigerate them until the pan or grill is hot. Do not leave the paste at room temperature. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  3. Cook on a hot grill or griddle, turning carefully, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C). Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  4. Rest 3 minutes and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Skewers are traditional. A hot griddle is a fair home method for the same paste.
  • Cashew is used in some kitchens and would add a tree nut. This paste uses cream instead.

Pairings

  • sliced onion
  • mint
  • lemon
