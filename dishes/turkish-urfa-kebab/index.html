Urfa Kebab

Next stop: Erzurum Cag Kebab · continuing the voyage

Pronounced OOR-fah keh-BAHB

Mild minced lamb with pepper paste, fat, and charcoal, without Adana's chile heat.

30 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 wide skewers

History

Urfa kebab is the milder cousin of Adana kebab, associated with Sanliurfa. It is still hand-minced lamb on a wide skewer, but the seasoning is not the hot pepper load of Adana. Many cooks use a mild pepper or isot paste for color and a rounded taste rather than burn. Treating it as the same recipe with the heat turned down misses the point: the balance is different. The meat is ground and cooked through.

Cultural background

Sanliurfa kebab houses and home cooks make this as its own skewer. It is not Adana with fewer flakes, and it is not a stew. The mildness is deliberate.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb fat and a mild pepper paste give a sweet-smoky surface instead of a chile blast. Charcoal still marks the crust. Because the heat is low, you taste the meat and the pepper's fruit more clearly. That is the distinction worth keeping on a separate page.

How it is made

Knead mildly seasoned minced lamb onto wide skewers and grill until the center is cooked through.

Authentic touch

Use a mild pepper, not the hot flakes from the Adana mix. Press the meat firmly so a soft, fatty mince does not drop into the fire.

How it is used

With onion, parsley, and grilled pepper. It can share a table with Adana, and the two should still taste different.

Field notes

  • Urfa kebab is the milder minced-lamb cousin associated with Sanliurfa.
  • It is a distinct kebab from Adana, not the same formula with the chile removed in name only.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb ground lamb, coarse
  • 3 oz lamb fat, finely chopped
  • 2 tablespoons mild red pepper paste or mild paprika
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 small onion, grated and squeezed dry
  • 4 wide flat metal skewers

Steps

  1. Knead the lamb, fat, mild pepper paste, salt, and onion until sticky. Do not add hot pepper flakes. Chill 15 minutes.
  2. Press the meat onto wide skewers.
  3. Grill over hot coals, turning carefully, until the center reaches 160°F (71°C).
  4. Serve with onion and herbs. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of mild tomato and pepper paste if you have no isot pepper.
  • A little more onion in the mix for a softer texture.

Pairings

  • Onion and parsley
  • Grilled pepper
  • Sumac
