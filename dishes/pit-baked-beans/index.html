Pit Baked Beans

Next stop: Grilled Peaches · continuing the voyage

Soft navy beans in a sweet-tangy molasses and mustard sauce.

20 min prep · 3 hr cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Baked beans sweetened with molasses and sharpened with mustard are a common side at Southern pits. This pot uses no bacon, so it is vegetarian and vegan. Molasses is not honey. The beans are dried navy beans, boiled until tender before the sweet sauce goes in, because sugar and acid keep beans from softening.

Cultural background

A meatless baked-bean side. No bacon. Mustard is an allergen. The beans are fully boiled, then baked. This is not a canning recipe.

Why it tastes this way

Molasses sweetens and darkens the pot, and mustard keeps it from tasting like dessert. Navy beans take on that sauce once they are already tender. Pits adopted the dish because a bean pot feeds a line next to more expensive meat, and a meatless version still does that job.

How it is made

Boil dried navy beans until tender, then bake them with molasses and mustard. No bacon.

Authentic touch

Boil the dried beans until they are soft before you add molasses, ketchup, or vinegar. Sweetness added too early leaves them chalky.

How it is used

Warm, beside ribs or brisket. Tell people there is no bacon in this batch.

Field notes

  • This bean pot has no bacon, so the written recipe is vegetarian and vegan.
  • Molasses, not honey, is the dark sweetener, and mustard is in the sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1 pound dried navy beans, picked over and rinsed
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1/4 cup molasses
  • 2 tablespoons yellow mustard
  • 1/4 cup ketchup
  • 2 tablespoons brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 8 cups water

Steps

  1. Cover the beans with water, bring them to a full boil at 212°F (100°C), and boil for several minutes. Then simmer until they are completely tender. Add water if needed. Do not add the sweet sauce yet.
  2. Drain most of the liquid, leaving about a cup. Stir in onion, molasses, mustard, ketchup, brown sugar, vinegar, and salt. There is no bacon and no honey.
  3. Bake covered at 325°F (163°C) until the sauce is thick and the beans are still tender, about 1 hour.
  4. Serve warm. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Mustard is an allergen. This is not a canning recipe.

Boil dried beans until they are completely tender. Kidney beans must be boiled hard, not held warm from raw. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A piece of bacon is a common pit addition. It would add meat and remove the vegetarian claim. It is not used here.

Pairings

  • ribs
  • brisket
  • white bread
