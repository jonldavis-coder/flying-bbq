Filipino Isaw

Pronounced ee-SOW

Grilled chicken intestine, salted, with a sour vinegar dip.

30 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Isaw is chicken intestine, cleaned and grilled on skewers, sold at Filipino street grills and night markets. It is ordinary offal cooking, the same family of thrift as other organ meats on those carts. The intestines have to be fresh, cleaned, and cooked to a poultry-offal temperature. A vinegar dip is the usual partner; this page treats the work as cooking, with a thermometer, and without a joke.

Cultural background

Street cooks in Manila and other cities grill isaw beside pork and chicken parts. Buy the intestines fresh from a butcher, preferably already turned and cleaned. There is no wheat and no dairy in this seasoning. Cook them the day you buy them.

Why it tastes this way

Chicken intestine is rich and slightly chewy when it is cooked through and not burned. Salt and a little garlic season it, and the vinegar dip cuts the fat the way it does for grilled pork. Charcoal adds a light bitterness at the edge. The point is a clean, hot skewer, not a dare.

How it is made

Clean fresh chicken intestines, simmer them until they reach a safe temperature, then grill them on skewers to brown.

Authentic touch

Rinse the intestines in cold water until the water runs clean, and discard any that smell sour. Do not try to rescue a spoiled tray with extra garlic.

How it is used

Eat them hot with a vinegar dip and rice.

Field notes

  • Isaw is chicken intestine grilled on a skewer, a regular item at Filipino street grills.
  • Poultry offal is cooked to 165°F (74°C), the same floor as other chicken.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds fresh chicken intestines, from a butcher, cleaned
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • water to cover, for the simmer

Steps

  1. Rinse the intestines in several changes of cold water. If the butcher has not cleaned them, turn them and rinse until the water is clear. Discard any that smell spoiled. The offal is still raw. Cook poultry offal to 165°F (74°C).
  2. Put them in a pot with salt, garlic, and water to cover. Simmer until a skewer through the thickest part reads 165°F (74°C). Drain. Cook poultry offal to 165°F (74°C).
  3. Thread them on skewers, brush very lightly with vinegar and pepper, and grill over a hot fire only until browned. Check that they are still at 165°F (74°C) before serving. Cook poultry offal to 165°F (74°C).
  4. Serve hot with a vinegar dip. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry offal to 165°F (74°C).

Cook beef offal to at least 145°F (63°C) with a rest. Cook poultry offal, including chicken hearts and livers, to 165°F (74°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pork intestine is a different skewer and is not this recipe.
  • A chili in the vinegar dip adds heat. The skewers here are mild.
