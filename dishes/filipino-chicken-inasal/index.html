Bacolod Chicken Inasal

Next stop: Filipino Inihaw Bangus · continuing the voyage

Pronounced ee-nah-SAHL

Tangy grilled chicken with annatto oil, garlic, lemongrass, and cane vinegar.

30 min prep · 35 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Chicken inasal is grilled chicken from Bacolod, on the island of Negros. The marinade uses vinegar, garlic, lemongrass, pepper, and annatto oil, which stains the meat a warm color. It is a city dish of Bacolod, cooked on open grills and in homes. The chicken is cooked through; this recipe does not add soy sauce, so the written marinade has no wheat.

Cultural background

Bacolod cooks baste the chicken with annatto oil as it grills. Vinegar is the sour note, different from a soy-heavy liempo. There is no dairy and no peanut. Annatto is a seed color, not a chili heat.

Why it tastes this way

Cane vinegar and garlic cut the richness of chicken skin, and annatto oil bastes it so the surface stays fragrant and colored. Lemongrass adds a citrus-herbal note. Grilling over coals browns what a boiled vinegar chicken would never brown. The taste is Bacolod's sour grill, not a sweet glaze.

How it is made

Marinate chicken in vinegar, garlic, lemongrass, and annatto, then grill it until the thickest piece is done.

Authentic touch

Baste with the annatto oil near the end as well as at the start. The color and aroma sit on the skin that way.

How it is used

Chop it and eat it with rice and a vinegar dip with chili if you want heat.

Field notes

  • Chicken inasal is associated with Bacolod in Negros.
  • Annatto oil and vinegar are the usual flavor, more than a soy glaze.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds chicken thighs and legs
  • 1/3 cup cane vinegar
  • 6 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 stalk lemongrass, minced
  • 2 tablespoons annatto oil
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Mix the vinegar, garlic, lemongrass, annatto oil, sugar, salt, and pepper. Coat the chicken and refrigerate it. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Heat a grill to medium. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Grill, turning and basting with any leftover marinade only in the first half of cooking, until the thickest piece reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone. Do not serve it rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest a few minutes and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Legs and thighs are the usual pieces. A whole bird works if every joint reaches temperature.
  • Soy sauce is a common add-in and would bring soy and wheat. It is left out here.
