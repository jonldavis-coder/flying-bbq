Zimbabwean Braai Chicken

Pronounced braai chicken

Chicken with garlic, paprika, and charcoal.

20 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Grilled chicken is an ordinary Zimbabwean braai, cooked in Harare yards and at weekend gatherings. The seasoning is often garlic, salt, and a little paprika or chili. This page does not add a ceremony the cookout does not need. Sadza, the maize porridge, is a usual side and is kin to other regional porridges without being the same plate. The bird is poultry and is cooked through.

Cultural background

Zimbabwean households braai chicken as normal food. It is not a ritual dish on this page. It is not peri-peri by another name unless you add that chile on purpose.

Why it tastes this way

Chicken skin over charcoal is smoky and salty, and garlic browns in the fat. A mild paprika colors it without turning the meal into a chile contest. The braai is social in the way many southern African cookouts are social: people, fire, and time. The taste is that plain fire, which is enough.

How it is made

Season chicken pieces and grill them over charcoal until the thigh is cooked.

Authentic touch

Cut the chicken into pieces so the thighs finish. Check the thick meat, not the wing tip.

How it is used

With sadza or a salad. The maize side is separate.

Field notes

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 4 lb, cut into pieces
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon paprika
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Rub the chicken with garlic, paprika, salt, pepper, and oil.
  2. Grill over medium charcoal, turning so the skin browns without burning.
  3. Cook until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), not touching bone.
  4. Rest 5 minutes. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A hotter chili rub if that is how your household likes it.
  • A whole bird spatchcocked, still checked at the thigh.
