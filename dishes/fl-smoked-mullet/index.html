Florida Panhandle Smoked Mullet

Next stop: Oklahoma Smoked Bologna · continuing the voyage

Oily, smoky mullet with salt and a little pepper.

20 min prep · 1 hr 30 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Smoked mullet is a Florida Panhandle fish-camp food, sold from smokehouses and cooked at open fires along that coast. Mullet is an oily local fish that takes smoke well. This is a hot smoke for the table, not a long cure and not a barbecue of pork.

Cultural background

Hot-smoked mullet from the Panhandle smokehouse habit. It is fish, eaten the day it is cooked.

Why it tastes this way

Mullet oil carries smoke the way a lean white fish cannot. Salt firms the surface. A short hot smoke keeps it moist; a cold smoke would be a different, preserving project and is not what this recipe does.

How it is made

Salt mullet and hot-smoke it until the flesh flakes.

Authentic touch

Smoke it the day you buy it. Oily fish turns if it waits.

How it is used

Warm, with lemon. Crackers or bread would add wheat and are not in this recipe. Hot sauce is also optional and not included.

Field notes

  • Smoked mullet is a Florida Panhandle fish-camp and smokehouse plate.
  • Mullet is oily, which is why a short smoke seasons it without drying it the way a lean fillet might.

Ingredients

  • 2 whole mullet, dressed, about 1 1/2 pounds each, or 2 pounds mullet fillets
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges
  • Oak or hickory for a hot smoke

Steps

  1. Pat the fish dry and salt and pepper it. Refrigerate it while the fire settles.
  2. Heat a smoker or a covered grill for indirect heat around 250°F (121°C). This is a hot smoke, not a cure.
  3. Cook until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve warm with lemon. Eat it the day it is smoked.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • An open hardwood fire with the fish off to the side works if you do not have a smokehouse.

Pairings

  • crackers
  • lemon
  • sliced tomato
