Madeira Beef Espetada

Next stop: Greek Easter Spit Lamb · continuing the voyage

Pronounced esh-peh-TAH-dah

Beef cubes with bay, garlic, salt, and a light char.

20 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 skewers

History

Espetada on Madeira is beef on a skewer with bay leaves, grilled over wood or charcoal. The island grows bay, and the leaves are threaded between the cubes rather than used only as a background herb. It is a feast and restaurant grill around Funchal and in the parishes. The meat is a whole-muscle cut, not a mince. Garlic and salt are the usual partners.

Cultural background

Madeiran households and grill houses cook this for gatherings. It is not Brazilian churrasco and not a pork espeto. The bay is part of the skewer, not a garnish.

Why it tastes this way

Bay releases a resinous scent when it heats next to beef fat. Large cubes give a browned edge and a juicy center if the fire is hot. Madeira's laurel is a local plant in daily cooking, so the skewer tastes of the island's hedges as well as of the cattle. Salt and garlic keep it direct.

How it is made

Skewer beef with bay and garlic, then grill over a hot fire and rest the meat.

Authentic touch

Use whole bay leaves between the cubes, and do not grind them into the meat. A hot fire and a short cook suit the cut.

How it is used

With milho frito, the fried cornmeal cake, or plain bread and salad. A tomato salad is enough.

Field notes

  • Madeira espetada threads bay leaves between cubes of beef.
  • Bay is grown on the island and is part of the skewer, not only a soup herb.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb beef sirloin, cut in 1 1/2 inch cubes
  • 12 bay leaves
  • 3 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 4 metal skewers

Steps

  1. Toss the beef with garlic, salt, and oil. Thread the cubes on skewers with a bay leaf between pieces.
  2. Heat a grill until it is hot. Shake off loose garlic so it does not burn.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest cube reaches at least 145°F (63°C). Give the skewers a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with bread or cornmeal cake. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A laurel branch used as the skewer when you have a fresh one thick enough.
  • A squeeze of lemon after grilling.

Pairings

  • Fried cornmeal cake
  • Bread
  • Tomato salad
