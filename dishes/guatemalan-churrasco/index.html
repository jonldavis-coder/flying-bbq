Guatemalan Churrasco with Chirmol

Next stop: Salvadoran Carne Asada · continuing the voyage

Pronounced choo-RAHS-koh, cheer-MOHL

Grilled beef with a roasted tomato and onion chirmol.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

In Guatemala, churrasco is a grilled steak, and chirmol is the roasted tomato, onion, and cilantro relish that comes with it. The Spanish word also names the Argentine mixed grill, which is a different meal in a different country. This page stays with the Guatemalan plate. Corn tortillas and a simple salad are the usual company.

Cultural background

Chirmol is chopped or lightly roasted, fresh, and seasoned with lime. It is not Argentine chimichurri and not Honduran chimol, though both are herb and onion relishes. The steak is a whole cut of beef.

Why it tastes this way

A hot grill gives the beef a browned crust, and the chirmol stays raw-tasting and sharp from lime and cilantro. Roasting the tomatoes first makes them sweeter than a fully raw salsa. The contrast is the point: charred meat, bright relish, warm tortillas.

How it is made

Grill a steak to a whole-muscle temperature and serve it with a roasted tomato chirmol.

Authentic touch

Char the tomatoes and onion before you chop them. A fully raw sauce is closer to another country's chimol than to this chirmol.

How it is used

Eat the steak with chirmol, corn tortillas, and a little cabbage or lettuce.

Field notes

  • Chirmol is a fresh roasted tomato and onion relish, not a bottled steak sauce.
  • Guatemalan churrasco is a Central American plate, not the Argentine mixed grill that shares the Spanish word.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds beef steak, about 1 inch thick
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 3 ripe tomatoes
  • 1/2 white onion
  • 1/2 cup chopped cilantro
  • 1 lime
  • 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced

Steps

  1. Salt and oil the steak. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Char the tomatoes and onion on the grill, then chop them with cilantro, jalapeño, lime juice, and a pinch of salt. Keep this chirmol cold until you eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill the steak until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested steak. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Spoon on the chirmol. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some cooks leave the chirmol fully chopped and raw. This one roasts the tomato and onion first.
  • A thin ranch steak or a thicker sirloin both work if the center reaches the same temperature.

Pairings

  • chirmol
  • corn tortillas
  • cabbage salad
