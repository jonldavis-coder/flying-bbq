Portuguese Charcoal Chicken

Pronounced FRAHN-goo shoo-RASH-koo

Charcoal chicken with garlic, lemon, and a hot piri-piri chile sauce.

30 min prep · 45 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Charcoal chicken with piri-piri is a Portuguese grill, famous in towns such as Guia in the Algarve and also cooked at home. The chile came from the Americas. Portuguese trade carried it to Africa, and the chile came back, through African cooking, to Portuguese grills. A British restaurant chain is not the origin. The bird is split, basted, and cooked over coals.

Cultural background

Portuguese piri-piri houses and home grillers cook this. It is not a chain recipe and not a Mozambican plate, though the chile history is shared and that coast has its own page.

Why it tastes this way

Chicken skin needs a hot grate to render, and a chile-garlic baste sticks to that fat. Lemon keeps it sharp. The heat of the small chile is the point, and that heat is a trade story: an American plant, African kitchens, and Portuguese charcoal. Smoke from the coals is the other half.

How it is made

Marinate a spatchcocked chicken in chile, garlic, and lemon, then grill it over charcoal until the thick meat is cooked.

Authentic touch

Split the bird so it lies flat, and baste near the end so the chile does not burn into bitterness.

How it is used

Cut into pieces, with fries or rice and a salad. The sauce can be passed at the table.

Field notes

  • The piri-piri chile came from the Americas and reached Portuguese grills through trade with Africa.
  • A British restaurant chain did not invent this charcoal chicken.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 4 lb, backbone removed and flattened
  • 4 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 2 fresh hot red chiles, chopped, or 2 tablespoons piri-piri sauce you trust
  • 1 lemon, juiced
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Mix garlic, chile, lemon juice, oil, and salt. Rub the mixture under and over the skin. Rest 30 minutes in the refrigerator.
  2. Heat a charcoal grill for indirect cooking, with a hotter side for browning.
  3. Grill with the lid down, turning as needed, until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), not touching bone.
  4. Rest 5 minutes, then cut. Baste with any remaining sauce only if it has not touched raw chicken, or boil that sauce first. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Thighs only, which are easier to cook evenly than a whole bird.
  • A milder paprika baste if the chile is hotter than your table wants, still named as milder.

Pairings

  • Rice
  • Fries
  • Tomato salad
