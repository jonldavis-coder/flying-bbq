South Carolina Mustard Barbecue Sauce

Next stop: Alabama White Sauce · continuing the voyage

Yellow mustard, vinegar, and a little sugar, tangy rather than sweet.

5 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 12 · about 2 cups

History

The mustard sauce of the South Carolina Midlands is widely linked to German settlement there. That is an association, not a proven single origin, and this recipe does not crown an inventor. It is the yellow sauce for pork shoulder, different from vinegar-only eastern Carolina and from Lexington's ketchup dip. Mustard is an allergen.

Cultural background

A Midlands mustard sauce. Yellow mustard is the body of it. It is not a tomato barbecue sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Mustard brings acid and a sharp aroma that cuts pork fat, and vinegar keeps the sauce pourable. A little sugar rounds the raw edge of the mustard without turning the bowl into molasses. The yellow color is how the Midlands plate is recognized.

How it is made

Stir yellow mustard with vinegar and sugar and warm it briefly.

Authentic touch

Warm it only until the sugar dissolves. A long boil dulls the mustard.

How it is used

On chopped pork and in a bowl beside the meat. It also seasons some pots of hash.

Field notes

  • Midlands mustard sauce is widely linked to German settlement, which is not a documented single inventor.
  • Yellow mustard is an allergen in this sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup yellow mustard
  • 1/2 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Stir mustard, vinegar, brown sugar, salt, and pepper in a saucepan.
  2. Warm over low heat about 10 minutes, until the sugar dissolves. Do not boil it hard.
  3. Cool and refrigerate. Use within a week. This is not a canning recipe. Mustard is an allergen.
  4. Serve with pork cooked to about 195–205°F (90–96°C). The sauce itself contains no meat.

Variations

  • A pinch of cayenne makes it hotter. This written sauce has no chile.
