Cypriot Souvla

Next stop: Bistecca alla Fiorentina · continuing the voyage

Pronounced SOOV-lah

Large pieces of pork with oregano, lemon, and a slow charcoal crust.

30 min prep · 1 hr 30 min cook · Serves 8 · about 8 servings

History

Souvla is the large Cypriot spit of meat cut in big pieces, bigger than souvlaki, turned over charcoal at family gatherings. Pork is common, and lamb is also used. The seasoning is usually oregano, oil, lemon, and salt. It is weekend and feast food rather than a snack on a stick. This recipe uses pork shoulder chunks and says so.

Cultural background

Cypriot households set up the souvla when there is time to tend a spit. It is not Greek street souvlaki and not a minced sheftalia, which is a different Cypriot grill.

Why it tastes this way

Large pieces keep their juice over a long, moderate fire. Oregano and lemon scent the fat as it renders. Charcoal, tended slowly, browns the outside without the rush of a thin skewer. The size of the piece is the difference you taste: more roast, less snack.

How it is made

Season large pork pieces, thread them on a spit, and roast over charcoal until the center is safe and the outside is browned.

Authentic touch

Cut the meat large, about two inches, and keep the spit turning. A still spit burns one face.

How it is used

With village salad, bread, and potatoes. People eat it as the main meat of a long meal.

Field notes

  • Souvla uses larger pieces than souvlaki and is cooked on a spit.
  • Pork is common on Cypriot souvla, and lamb is also used.

Ingredients

  • 4 lb pork shoulder, cut in 2 inch chunks
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 2 tablespoons dried oregano
  • 1 lemon, juiced
  • 4 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon salt

Steps

  1. Toss the pork with oil, oregano, lemon, garlic, and salt. Thread the chunks on a spit, leaving a little space.
  2. Light a charcoal bed under the spit. Turn the meat steadily over moderate heat.
  3. Cook whole pork to at least 145°F (63°C) in the center of a chunk and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Carve off the spit. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lamb shoulder in the same cut, still cooked as a whole muscle.
  • A rotisserie attachment if a traditional souvla frame is not at hand.

Pairings

  • Village salad
  • Bread
  • Potatoes
