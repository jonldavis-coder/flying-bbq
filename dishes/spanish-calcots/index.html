Catalan Calcots

Pronounced kal-SOTS

Sweet grilled spring onions with a blackened jacket and a soft, juicy center.

History

A calcot is a spring onion grown and grilled for the Catalan calcotada. Valls is the town most closely tied to the season. The onions are laid on a hot fire, often of vine cuttings, until the outside is black and the inside is sweet. Romesco is a traditional sauce beside them and is kept separate here so the onion itself stays a simple plant food. The burnt layer is peeled away at the table.

Cultural background

Catalan friends and families cook this in late winter and early spring. The recipe on this page is the onion. A separate sauce can sit beside it and is not in the pot. It is not a mixed animal grill.

Why it tastes this way

High heat turns the outer leaves to carbon and steams the core in its own juice. That contrast is the dish. Vine wood burns hot and fast, which suits a vegetable that needs char more than a long roast. Salt and oil after peeling are enough.

How it is made

Grill whole calcots or large spring onions over a hot wood fire until the jackets are black, then wrap and steam them a few minutes before peeling.

Authentic touch

Char them harder than looks polite. The black outside is a wrapper, and you peel it off with your hands.

How it is used

Peeled and eaten hot. A pepper sauce may be served beside them and is not part of this recipe.

Field notes

  • The calcotada is a Catalan fire for grilled spring onions, and Valls is closely associated with it.
  • The blackened outside is peeled away; the sweet core is what you eat.

Ingredients

  • 24 calcots or large spring onions, roots trimmed
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Newspaper or a clean towel for wrapping
  • Vine cuttings or hardwood for the fire

Steps

  1. Build a hot wood fire and let it form a bed of coals. Leave the onions whole.
  2. Lay the onions on a grill over the fire and turn them until the outer layers are black.
  3. Wrap them so they steam for a few minutes. There is no animal-protein thermometer target. The center should be soft when you squeeze it.
  4. Peel the charred layer, salt and oil the cores, and eat them hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. This is not a canning recipe.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Large spring onions if true calcots are not sold where you live, named as a stand-in.
  • A separate pepper sauce beside them, made as its own recipe.

Pairings

  • Grilled leeks
  • Red wine
  • Olive oil
