Lao Sai Oua

Next stop: Lao Ping Kai · continuing the voyage

Pronounced sai oo-ah

Grilled pork sausage heavy with lemongrass, galangal, lime leaf, and chili.

30 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Sai oua is a Lao sausage of ground pork mixed with lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaf, shallot, and chili, then grilled. It is a fresh sausage, cooked the day it is mixed, not a cured or dried link. Luang Prabang and other Lao towns sell it beside sticky rice. Ground pork has to reach a ground-meat temperature; this recipe forms the meat into logs without a curing salt.

Cultural background

Lao cooks grill sai oua for markets and family meals. The herbs are in the meat, not only in a dip. Fish sauce contains fish. There is no wheat and no dairy. Do not cure it or hang it to dry.

Why it tastes this way

Lemongrass, galangal, and lime leaf are strong enough to stand up to pork fat, and chili makes the link hot. Grilling browns the outside so the herbs smell toasted. Sticky rice cools the heat. A plain garlic sausage would miss the herb pile that defines sai oua.

How it is made

Mix ground pork with lemongrass, galangal, lime leaf, and chili, shape logs, and grill them until the center is done.

Authentic touch

Mince the lemongrass very fine. Long fibers stay tough inside the sausage.

How it is used

Slice the sausage and eat it with sticky rice and herbs.

Field notes

  • Sai oua is a fresh Lao pork sausage seasoned with lemongrass and lime leaf.
  • It is cooked the day it is mixed. This recipe does not cure the meat.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds ground pork
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, tender parts minced fine
  • 1 tablespoon minced galangal
  • 4 lime leaves, minced
  • 2 shallots, minced
  • 2 hot chilies, minced
  • 1 tablespoon fish sauce
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Mix the pork with lemongrass, galangal, lime leaf, shallot, chili, fish sauce, and salt. Keep it cold. Do not add curing salt. The meat is still raw. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  2. Shape four logs. Refrigerate them while the grill heats. Cook them the same day. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  3. Grill, turning, until the center of each log reaches 160°F (71°C). Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).
  4. Rest 3 minutes, then slice. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook ground meat to 160°F (71°C).

Cook ground meat, including fresh sausages, to 160°F (71°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A natural casing makes a tighter link. A log shape without casing is the home method here.
  • Fewer chilies make it milder. The written sausage is hot.

Pairings

  • sticky rice
  • herbs
  • cucumber
