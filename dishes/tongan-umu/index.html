Tongan Umu

Next stop: Maori Hangi · on Pacific earth ovens by boat, by Boat

Pronounced OO-moo

Lamb steamed with canned coconut milk, tomato, and sweet potato.

History

A Tongan umu is an earth oven, used for Sunday meals and gatherings, and the foods are not the same as a Samoan umu. One Tongan parcel, lu sipi, cooks lamb with coconut and onion in leaves. This page follows that lamb-and-coconut pattern, with sweet potato and tomato, in a covered home pot. It uses canned coconut milk; it does not use raw cassava, and it does not use the pork shoulder from the Samoan page; the lamb is a whole cut and is rested.

Cultural background

Tongan cooks prepare umu food for family Sundays. The home pot is not an earth oven and not a ceremony. There is lamb and coconut, and no wheat, dairy, or peanut. The different meat is deliberate: lamb here, pork on the Samoan page.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb and coconut are a richer, sharper pot than a mild white fish, and tomato adds acid so the coconut does not sit flat. Sweet potato takes up the juice. Leaves in a real umu perfume the parcel. The covered pot keeps the steam. The Tongan pattern here is the lamb, which is why it does not copy the Samoan pork.

How it is made

Cook lamb chunks with canned coconut milk, tomato, and sweet potato in a covered pot, then rest the lamb.

Authentic touch

Cut the lamb into similar chunks so the pot finishes together. A whole uncut leg will still be cold in the center when the potato is done.

How it is used

Serve the lamb and sweet potato with the coconut juices.

Field notes

  • A Tongan umu is an earth oven, and its usual foods differ from a Samoan umu.
  • This pot is lamb with coconut and sweet potato, not the pork on the Samoan page.

Ingredients

  • 2.5 pounds boneless lamb shoulder, cut in large chunks
  • 1 can coconut milk, 13.5 ounces
  • 1 pound sweet potato, peeled and chunked
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 4 cups spinach

Steps

  1. Put the lamb, coconut milk, sweet potato, tomato, onion, salt, and spinach in a heavy pot. Do not add cassava. The lamb is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Cover the pot. This is a home cook, not a Tongan umu ceremony. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Cook until the center of the thickest lamb chunk reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and the sweet potato is tender. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Rest the lamb 3 minutes. Serve it with the vegetables. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Taro leaves can wrap the lamb when they are sold as food. Spinach is a fair stand-in.
  • A home pot is not a ceremony and not a pit of stones.
