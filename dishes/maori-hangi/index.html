Maori Hangi

Pronounced HAH-ngee

Chicken, sweet potato, and cabbage steamed in a covered pot, salty and soft.

30 min prep · 2 hr cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

A hangi is a Maori earth oven. Heated stones, baskets of meat and vegetables, and wet coverings steam the food in the ground, including at gatherings on the marae. Rotorua is one place visitors see public hangi. A backyard imitation is not a marae hangi; this page uses a heavy covered pot with chicken, kumara, and cabbage, and it names that limit; the chicken is cooked to a poultry temperature; do not dig a pit in a lawn and call it the ceremony.

Cultural background

Maori cooks run a hangi for hui and for manuhiri, the guests. The foods often include chicken, pork, kumara, potato, and cabbage. This pot uses chicken and vegetables only, so one temperature rules the meat. There is no wheat and no dairy. It is a home steam, not a marae hangi.

Why it tastes this way

Steam from a closed oven makes the chicken and the kumara taste of each other, without a hard crust. Cabbage sweetens in that steam. Stones and wet sacks in the ground hold the heat for hours at a real hangi. A covered pot on a stove can borrow the softness and cannot borrow the marae. The honesty is part of cooking it at home.

How it is made

Pack chicken, kumara, and cabbage in a heavy covered pot and cook until the chicken is done. Say plainly that this is not a marae hangi.

Authentic touch

Season the food before it goes in the pot. A hangi seasons the baskets, not the table afterward only.

How it is used

Serve the chicken, kumara, and cabbage together.

Field notes

  • A hangi is a Maori earth oven, including the one used for gatherings on the marae.
  • A backyard covered pot is not a marae hangi.

Ingredients

  • 3 pounds chicken pieces
  • 2 pounds kumara or sweet potato, peeled and chunked
  • 1/2 small cabbage, cut in wedges
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1/2 cup water

Steps

  1. Toss the chicken with onion, salt, and oil. Layer it with kumara and cabbage in a heavy pot. Add the water. The chicken is still raw. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  2. Cover the pot. A backyard imitation is not a marae hangi. Do not present this pot as one. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  3. Cook over medium heat, lid on, until the thickest chicken piece reaches 165°F (74°C), away from the bone, and the kumara is tender. Do not serve the chicken rare. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  4. Rest a few minutes and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pork is common in a hangi and would need its own temperature if you add it. This pot is chicken.
  • Potato can sit beside the kumara. Keep the lid on either way.
