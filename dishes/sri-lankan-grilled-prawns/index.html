Sri Lankan Grilled Prawns

Pronounced sri-LAN-kan prawns

Prawns with chili, garlic, lime, and curry leaf, cooked just until they turn opaque.

20 min prep · 8 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Grilled prawns are a coastal Sri Lankan plate, seasoned with chili, garlic, lime, and curry leaf and cooked quickly over a hot fire. Negombo and other west-coast towns land the prawns that make this a local grill. The heat is from chili, and the cook is short because shellfish toughen when they stay on the grate. This is Sri Lankan coastal cooking, with its own spice and its own shore.

Cultural background

Coastal cooks grill whole prawns in the shell or peeled, with a chili paste. There is shellfish in the dish. There is no wheat batter and no dairy. It is a grill, not a fry.

Why it tastes this way

Prawns are sweet, and lime and chili cut that sweetness. Curry leaf, torn into the oil, adds the herbal note associated with Sri Lankan cooking. A hot grate chars the spice paste in a few minutes, which is all the time the prawns need.

How it is made

Toss prawns with chili, garlic, lime, and curry leaf, then grill them until the flesh is opaque.

Authentic touch

Pull them when they turn opaque. Another five minutes on a hot grate makes them rubbery even if the chili paste smells good.

How it is used

Eat them with lime and a little extra chili on the side if you want more heat.

Field notes

  • Curry leaf, chili, and lime are the coastal seasoning on these prawns.
  • Shellfish are ready when the flesh is opaque, not when the shells have blackened.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 pounds large prawns, peeled and deveined
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon chili powder
  • 8 curry leaves, torn
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons lime juice
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Toss the prawns with oil, garlic, chili, curry leaves, salt, lime juice, and pepper. Refrigerate them for 15 minutes. Do not leave garlic in oil at room temperature. The prawns are still raw. Cook shellfish until the flesh is opaque and reaches 145°F (63°C).
  2. Heat a grill until the grate is hot. Oil the grate lightly. Cook shellfish until the flesh is opaque and reaches 145°F (63°C).
  3. Grill the prawns, turning once, until the flesh is opaque and the center reaches 145°F (63°C). Cook shellfish until the flesh is opaque and reaches 145°F (63°C).
  4. Serve at once with lime. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook shellfish until the flesh is opaque and reaches 145°F (63°C).

Cook shrimp, lobster, and crab until the flesh is opaque and pearly. An internal temperature of 145°F (63°C) is the target for lobster and shrimp.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Leave the shells on if you want more protection from the fire. Peel them to eat.
  • A milder chili powder lowers the heat. This batch is hot.

Pairings

  • lime
  • rice
  • cucumber
