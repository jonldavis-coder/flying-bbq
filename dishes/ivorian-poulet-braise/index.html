Ivorian Poulet Braise

Next stop: Cameroonian Soya · continuing the voyage

Pronounced poo-LAY breh-ZAY

Charcoal chicken with garlic, onion, and chili, served beside fried plantain.

20 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Poulet braisé is grilled chicken in Côte d'Ivoire, a street and maquis staple in Abidjan. The bird is seasoned with garlic, onion, and chili and cooked over charcoal. Alloco, fried ripe plantain, is the usual pairing and is named here rather than turned into a second full recipe. The chicken is poultry and is cooked through. It is everyday grill food.

Cultural background

Ivorian grill cooks at open-air restaurants make this to order. It is not a groundnut stew and not a festival ceremony. Alloco sits beside it.

Why it tastes this way

Chicken skin over charcoal picks up smoke and chili, and garlic browns in the fat. The heat is present without being the only note. Sweet fried plantain, if you make it separately, balances the char. The street fire is close and a little uneven, which is why the skin blisters in spots.

How it is made

Rub chicken with garlic, onion, and chili, and grill it over charcoal until the thick meat is cooked.

Authentic touch

Spatchcock or cut the chicken so the thighs are not still raw when the breast is done. Check the thigh, not the wing.

How it is used

With alloco, the fried plantain pairing, and sliced onion. The plantain is a side, fried in its own pan.

Field notes

  • Poulet braisé is grilled chicken sold widely in Côte d'Ivoire.
  • Alloco, fried ripe plantain, is a usual pairing.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 4 lb, cut into pieces
  • 1 onion, grated
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 fresh hot chile, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Mix onion, garlic, chile, salt, and oil. Rub the chicken and rest it 20 minutes in the refrigerator.
  2. Heat a charcoal grill for medium heat.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), not touching bone.
  4. Rest 5 minutes. Serve with alloco if you have fried it separately. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Thighs and drumsticks only, which are simpler to cook evenly.
  • A hotter chili paste if that is how the stand you know does it.

Pairings

  • Sliced onion
  • Tomato
