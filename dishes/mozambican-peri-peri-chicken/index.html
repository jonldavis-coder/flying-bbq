Mozambican Peri-Peri Chicken

Next stop: South African Boerewors · on Africa's fires by plane, by Plane

Pronounced PEE-ree PEE-ree

Charcoal chicken with garlic, lemon, and a very hot peri-peri chile.

30 min prep · 45 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 servings

History

Peri-peri chicken in Mozambique is a charcoal bird seasoned with the small hot chile that reached Africa from the Americas through Portuguese trade. On this side of that route the dish is Mozambican grill food, not a chain-restaurant recipe and not a British invention. Maputo grills and home fires both cook it. The bird is poultry and is cooked through. The Portuguese charcoal chicken is a related plate with its own page.

Cultural background

Mozambican cooks baste chicken with chile, garlic, and lemon over coals. The history of the chile is a trade history. The page does not hand the dish to a restaurant brand.

Why it tastes this way

The small chile is sharply hot, and chicken fat carries it better than a lean sauce does. Lemon and garlic keep the baste from tasting only of burn. Charcoal smoke is the other flavor. Telling the story from Mozambique matters: the chile's African life is not a footnote to a European menu or a chain.

How it is made

Marinate a flattened chicken in peri-peri, garlic, and lemon, and grill it until the thigh is cooked.

Authentic touch

Baste late so the sugar and chile do not carbonize. Check the thigh. Wash your hands after handling the chiles.

How it is used

Cut up, with rice and a salad. The sauce is hot.

Field notes

  • The peri-peri chile reached Africa from the Americas through Portuguese trade, and Mozambican grills made it a charcoal-chicken seasoning.
  • This plate is Mozambican grill food, not a chain-restaurant recipe.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 4 lb, backbone removed and flattened
  • 6 fresh hot red chiles, chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 lemon, juiced
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Blend or finely chop the chiles with garlic, lemon juice, oil, and salt. Rub the chicken, including under the skin. Refrigerate 30 minutes.
  2. Heat a charcoal grill for indirect heat.
  3. Grill, turning, until the thickest part of the thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), not touching bone.
  4. Rest 5 minutes and cut. Boil any leftover marinade before using it as a sauce. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Thighs only, easier to cook evenly.
  • A milder chile, named as milder, if the bird's-eye heat is too much.

Pairings

  • Rice
  • Tomato salad
  • Lime
