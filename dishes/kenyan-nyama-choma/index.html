Kenyan Nyama Choma

Next stop: Mozambican Peri-Peri Chicken · on Africa's fires by plane, by Plane

Pronounced NYAH-mah CHO-mah

Goat with salt, a charred edge, and a squeeze of lime or chili salt.

20 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Nyama choma is Swahili for grilled meat. In Kenya it is often goat, and beef is also served, at roadside stops and in Nairobi restaurants built around the fire. The meat is salted, roasted, and often chopped. It is social food. This recipe uses goat pieces as whole muscle. A kachumbari salad of tomato and onion is the usual fresh side.

Cultural background

Kenyan butchers and nyama choma houses cook this to order. It is grilled goat, not a sauced stew and not a minced kebab. People wait while it roasts.

Why it tastes this way

Goat over charcoal tastes of its own fat once the surface dries and browns. Salt is enough because the fire is the seasoning. Tomato and onion on the side add water and acid that the meat does not have. The social wait is part of the dish: the meat comes in batches, hot, and is eaten with the hands or with a knife at a shared table.

How it is made

Salt goat pieces and grill them over charcoal until they reach a safe temperature, rest them, and chop if you want the usual plate.

Authentic touch

Roast larger pieces than thin steaks so the goat stays juicy, then chop them after the rest. Do not hide the meat under a sweet sauce.

How it is used

With tomato-onion salad and ugali or potatoes. Ugali is a maize side related to other East African porridges.

Field notes

  • Nyama choma is Swahili for grilled meat.
  • In Kenya the meat is often goat, and beef is also served.

Ingredients

  • 3 lb bone-in goat, in large pieces
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped, for the salad
  • 1 onion, sliced, for the salad
  • 1 lime

Steps

  1. Rub the goat with oil, salt, and pepper. Heat a charcoal grill to medium-high.
  2. Grill, turning, until the meat is browned on the outside.
  3. Cook whole goat to at least 145°F (63°C), away from bone, and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Chop if you like, and serve with tomato, onion, and lime. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Beef steaks on the same fire, still a whole cut at the same temperature.
  • A chili-salt sprinkle at the table.

Pairings

  • Tomato and onion salad
  • Ugali
  • Chili salt
