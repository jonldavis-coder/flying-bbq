Yemeni Chicken Mandi

Next stop: Moroccan Mechoui · continuing the voyage

Pronounced MAN-dee

Chicken and rice with a hawaij-style spice of cumin, coriander, turmeric, and pepper.

30 min prep · 1 hr 15 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Mandi is a Yemeni dish of meat and rice cooked in a pit or a taboon, now also made in homes and restaurants far beyond Yemen. The pit is traditional, and a covered oven pot is the home version. This page uses chicken and a simple hawaij-style spice. It does not assign the dish to one community as an owner. Lamb is the other common meat. Chicken, as poultry, is cooked to 165°F.

Cultural background

Yemeni families and the cooks who learned the dish from them make mandi for gatherings. The home pot is labeled. It is not a quick grill and not a single household's secret.

Why it tastes this way

Rice cooked under the meat takes the drippings and the spice, which is why mandi is a pot and not only a roasted bird. Cumin, coriander, turmeric, and black pepper make a straightforward hawaij-style mix; real blends vary by shop and house. A pit adds smoke a home oven only suggests. The chicken stays the protein, and the rice is the rest of the plate.

How it is made

Rub chicken with a simple spice mix, brown it, and cook it with rice in a covered oven pot until the poultry is done.

Authentic touch

Brown the chicken first so the rice has some roasted drippings. Keep the pot covered. Do not claim a pit you did not dig.

How it is used

Chicken over the rice, with a salad. It feeds a group from one pot.

Field notes

  • The pit or taboon is the traditional mandi cook, and a covered oven pot is the home version.
  • This page does not assign mandi to one community.

Ingredients

  • 6 chicken thighs, bone in
  • 2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 2 teaspoons ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 2 cups long-grain rice, rinsed
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Mix cumin, coriander, turmeric, pepper, and salt. Rub half on the chicken. This is a simple hawaij-style mix, not a claim of one correct blend.
  2. Brown the chicken in oil in an oven-safe pot. Add onion, rice, the rest of the spice, and water. Set the chicken on the rice and cover the pot. A covered oven pot is the home version; the pit is traditional.
  3. Bake at 375°F (190°C) until the thickest thigh reaches 165°F (74°C), not touching bone, and the rice is tender.
  4. Rest 10 minutes covered. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lamb shoulder instead of chicken, cooked as a whole muscle to at least 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest, and longer if you want it tender.
  • A shop hawaij, checked for its own allergens, in place of the simple mix.

Pairings

  • Rice from the pot
  • Tomato salad
  • Lime
