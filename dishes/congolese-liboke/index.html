Congolese Liboke

Next stop: Malagasy Zebu Brochettes · continuing the voyage

Pronounced lee-BOH-keh

Fish steamed in a leaf with tomato, onion, and chili, finished over the grill.

25 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 packets

History

Liboke is fish cooked in a leaf packet in the Congo Basin, grilled or set in the coals so the parcel steams. The local leaf is part of the aroma. If that leaf is hard to buy, spinach or chard will wrap the fish, and the substitute should be named. Kinshasa tables know the dish from river fish. The fish is cooked until it flakes. This is not a fried fillet.

Cultural background

Cooks in the Democratic Republic of the Congo wrap fish with aromatics and cook the packet. Spinach or chard is a stand-in, not the forest leaf. The method is a packet, whether the heat is a grill or steam.

Why it tastes this way

The leaf holds moisture, so the fish steams in tomato and onion while the outside of the packet picks up smoke if it sits on a grill. Chili perfumes the juice. A substitute green will not taste identical to the local leaf, which is why it has to be named. The packet is the technique that makes liboke different from a bare grilled fish.

How it is made

Season fish, wrap it in leaves with tomato and onion, and cook the packets on a grill until the fish flakes.

Authentic touch

Wrap the packet tightly so the juice does not run into the fire. If you use chard or spinach, say so at the table.

How it is used

Open the packet at the table, with the juices, and eat the fish with a starch cooked separately.

Field notes

  • Liboke is fish cooked in a leaf packet in the Congo Basin.
  • Spinach or chard can wrap the fish when the local leaf is hard to buy, and that substitute should be named.

Ingredients

  • 4 firm fish fillets, about 6 oz each
  • 1 large bunch chard or spinach, as the stand-in leaf
  • 2 tomatoes, sliced
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 fresh chili, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Salt the fish. Oil the leaves. This packet uses chard or spinach because the local leaf may be hard to buy, and it should be called that.
  2. Wrap each fillet with tomato, onion, and chili in several leaves so the juice stays in. Tie with kitchen string.
  3. Grill the packets over medium coals, turning once, until the fish inside reaches 145°F (63°C) and flakes. Open one packet to check.
  4. Serve in the leaves. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Steamed on a rack over boiling water if you cannot grill the packets.
  • A whole small fish in each packet instead of a fillet.

Pairings

  • Rice
  • Plantain
  • Chili
