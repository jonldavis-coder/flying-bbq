Memphis Wet Pork Shoulder

Next stop: Kansas City Burnt Ends · continuing the voyage

Smoked pork shoulder finished with a thin, tangy tomato-vinegar sauce.

30 min prep · 10 hr cook · Serves 10 · about 10 servings

History

Memphis serves pork more than one way. Dry ribs wear a rub and no sauce. This shoulder is the wet plate: smoked pork dressed with a thin tomato-and-vinegar sauce, not a thick sweet glaze. It belongs to the same city as the dry rib and is not the same dish.

Cultural background

A sauced Memphis pork shoulder. Do not confuse it with Memphis dry ribs or with Lexington dip, which is a Piedmont North Carolina sauce.

Why it tastes this way

The shoulder is rich, and a thin tomato-vinegar sauce cuts it without burying the hickory smoke. Paprika in the rub seasons the bark before any sauce goes on. The sauce stays loose, closer to a mop than to Kansas City molasses.

How it is made

Smoke a rubbed pork shoulder and dress it with a thin tomato-vinegar sauce.

Authentic touch

Smoke the shoulder dry until the bark is set, then sauce it late or after it is pulled so the sugars do not burn.

How it is used

Pulled or chopped, with extra sauce. It can be a sandwich or a plate.

Field notes

  • Memphis wet shoulder uses sauce. Memphis dry ribs do not.
  • The sauce is kept thin, tomato and vinegar, rather than a thick molasses glaze.

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in pork shoulder, about 8 pounds
  • 2 tablespoons kosher salt
  • 1 tablespoon paprika
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 cup tomato sauce
  • 1/2 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons brown sugar
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper, for the sauce

Steps

  1. Rub the shoulder with salt, paprika, and pepper. Smoke indirectly over hickory at about 250°F (121°C).
  2. Simmer tomato sauce, vinegar, brown sugar, and pepper for 10 minutes until the sauce is thin and pourable.
  3. Cook the shoulder to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so it pulls. Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest; this cook continues past that for tenderness.
  4. Rest, pull, and toss with enough sauce to glisten. Serve the rest on the side.

Whole pork is safe at 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. For pulled or chopped pork, continue to about 195–205°F (90–96°C) so the collagen softens, then rest it.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chopped finer, the same shoulder becomes a sandwich filling.

Pairings

  • white bread
  • pickles
  • coleslaw
