Costa Rican Carne Asada

Next stop: Belizean Grilled Snapper · continuing the voyage

Grilled beef with a homemade onion and lime sauce.

20 min prep · 18 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 servings

History

Costa Rican carne asada is grilled beef, often cooked for a family lunch and eaten with rice, beans, and a simple relish. A commercial bottled sauce is common on Costa Rican tables, and this recipe does not reproduce it. The onion sauce here is homemade, it contains no tamarind, and it is not Salsa Lizano.

Cultural background

The written sauce is onion, sweet pepper, cilantro, and lime. It is a fresh relish for the steak. The beef is a whole cut, salted and grilled.

Why it tastes this way

Onion and lime stay raw and sharp, so they brighten beef that has picked up a light char. Without a bottled sauce, the plate tastes of the grill and the fresh chop, not of a sweet vegetable concentrate. Rice and beans make it a full Costa Rican lunch.

How it is made

Grill beef to a whole-muscle temperature and serve an onion and lime sauce that is not a commercial condiment.

Authentic touch

Rinse the chopped onion in cold water if you want it milder, then dry it before it meets the lime.

How it is used

Serve with rice and beans, fried plantains, and a small salad.

Field notes

  • The onion sauce in this recipe is homemade, contains no tamarind, and is not Salsa Lizano.
  • Costa Rican carne asada is a weekend grill, usually beside rice and beans.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds beef steak
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 white onion, finely chopped
  • 1/2 sweet red pepper, finely chopped
  • 1/3 cup chopped cilantro
  • 1 lime
  • 1 teaspoon salt, for the sauce

Steps

  1. Salt and oil the steak. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Mix the onion, sweet pepper, cilantro, lime juice, and a pinch of salt. This sauce contains no tamarind and it is not Salsa Lizano. Keep it cold. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill the steak until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested steak. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Spoon on the onion sauce. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few slices of tomato can go into the onion sauce. They do not turn it into a bottled product.
  • Chicken would be a different dish and would need 165°F (74°C).

Pairings

  • rice and beans
  • fried plantains
  • cabbage salad
