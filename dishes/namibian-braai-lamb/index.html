Namibian Braai Lamb

Next stop: Zimbabwean Braai Chicken · continuing the voyage

Pronounced braai lamb

Lamb chops with salt, pepper, and braai smoke.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

A Namibian braai draws on German settlement in what is now Namibia and on South African braai practice. Lamb chops are a straightforward grill in Windhoek and on farms. Game is also braaied in Namibia. This page does not invent an oryx recipe or any other game formula. The lamb is salted and cooked as a whole cut. The cookout is a shared fire, not a costume.

Cultural background

Namibian households braai lamb as ordinary meat. The German and South African influences are part of how the cookout arrived and changed. Game exists on other fires and is not faked here.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb fat, salt, and hardwood or charcoal smoke are the whole flavor. Dry air and a hot grid brown the fat edge quickly. The braai form, a social fire with several meats possible, is shared with South Africa and also sits in a country where German-speaking farmers left sausage and baking habits beside the grid. This plate stays with lamb so it does not pretend to be a game dish.

How it is made

Season lamb chops and grill them on a braai, then rest them.

Authentic touch

Salt the chops and grill them hot. If someone at the table talks about game, do not substitute an invented oryx method.

How it is used

With a salad and potatoes. It is a straightforward chop plate.

Field notes

  • Namibian braai practice has German and South African influences.
  • Game is also braaied in Namibia, and this page does not invent an oryx recipe.

Ingredients

  • 8 lamb chops or 2 lb lamb steaks
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Oil the lamb and season it with salt and pepper.
  2. Grill over a hot braai, turning once.
  3. Cook whole lamb to at least 145°F (63°C) and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with salad. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A lamb shoulder roasted longer, still checked with a thermometer.
  • A coil of sausage beside the chops only if the sausage reaches 160°F (71°C).

Pairings

  • Potatoes
  • Green salad
  • Tomato
