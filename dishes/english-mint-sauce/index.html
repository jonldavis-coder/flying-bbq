English Mint Sauce

Next stop: Welsh Salt-Marsh Lamb · continuing the voyage

Pronounced mint saws

Sharp vinegar, fresh mint, and a little sugar, meant to cut lamb fat.

15 min prep · 5 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 cup

History

Mint sauce is the English table sauce of vinegar, mint, and sugar served with lamb. It belongs next to a roast, not on a grill of its own. Garden mint and malt or white vinegar are the usual pantry pieces. The sauce is mixed and left to stand so the mint softens in the acid. It is not a jelly and not a pesto.

Cultural background

This page is only the sauce. The ingredients are mint, vinegar, and sugar. It is not a barbecue rub, and it is not cooked over a fire.

Why it tastes this way

Lamb fat is rich, and vinegar cuts it. Mint brings a cool, green note that the British kitchen has long put with spring lamb. A pinch of sugar keeps the vinegar from tasting only harsh. The combination is about balance, not sweetness.

How it is made

Stir chopped mint into vinegar with sugar and a pinch of salt. This is a bowl sauce, not a grill.

Authentic touch

Chop the mint fine and stir it into warm vinegar so the leaves soften. Let it stand before you serve it.

How it is used

Cold or cool, in a jug beside roast or grilled lamb. A spoonful on the chop is enough.

Field notes

  • English mint sauce is vinegar, mint, and sugar, and it is served with lamb.
  • The sauce is not a grill and not a mint jelly.

Ingredients

  • 1 packed cup fresh mint leaves
  • 4 tablespoons malt or white wine vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons boiling water

Steps

  1. Chop the mint finely. Stir the sugar and salt into the boiling water until they dissolve.
  2. Add the vinegar and the mint. This is a sauce, not a grill, so there is no animal-protein thermometer target.
  3. Let the sauce stand at least 20 minutes so the mint softens in the acid.
  4. Taste and add a few drops more vinegar or a pinch more sugar if you want it sharper or rounder. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. This is not a canning recipe.

These ingredients are vegetables, bread, or a sauce. Keep them clean, and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. This is not a canning recipe.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A little hot water to loosen a very sharp mix.
  • White wine vinegar in place of malt vinegar.

Pairings

  • Roast lamb
  • New potatoes
  • Peas
