Cornish Grilled Mackerel

Next stop: English Mint Sauce · continuing the voyage

Pronounced KOR-nish MAK-er-ul

Oily, smoky mackerel with lemon, salt, and a charred skin.

15 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 whole fish

History

Mackerel has long been a summer fish off Cornwall, landed in harbors such as Newlyn and eaten while the oils are still bright. Grilling whole fish over a hot fire is a coastal habit, not a restaurant invention. The skin protects the flesh, and the fish needs little more than salt. This page keeps the recipe to that grill.

Cultural background

Cornwall is the place, and the fish is Atlantic mackerel. It is a simple shore supper. It is not a smoked kipper and not a sauced fillet.

Why it tastes this way

Mackerel is rich in oil, so a hot grill browns the skin before the flesh dries. Salt and lemon cut that richness. A wood or charcoal fire adds a light smoke that suits an oily fish better than a thick sauce would.

How it is made

Score whole mackerel, salt them, and grill over a hot fire until the skin blisters and the center flakes.

Authentic touch

Grill the fish the day you buy it. Oil the skin and the grate, and turn only once, or the flesh will break.

How it is used

On the bone, with lemon, brown bread, and a green salad. One fish is a usual serving.

Field notes

  • Mackerel is an oily Atlantic fish that runs off Cornwall in the warmer months.
  • Whole fish hold together on a grill better than fillets of the same catch.

Ingredients

  • 4 whole mackerel, gutted, about 12 oz each
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 lemon, cut in wedges
  • Black pepper

Steps

  1. Heat a grill until it is hot. Pat the fish dry, oil the skin, and season with salt and pepper. Cut two slashes on each side.
  2. Oil the grate. Grill the fish over the coals, turning once, until the skin is blistered.
  3. Cook the fish to 145°F (63°C) in the thickest part, until it is opaque and flakes.
  4. Serve at once with lemon. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few fennel stalks in the cavity.
  • Cold leftover fish the next day with horseradish, if it was refrigerated promptly.

Pairings

  • Lemon
  • Brown bread
  • Watercress
  • New potatoes
