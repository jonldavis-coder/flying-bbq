Brazilian Picanha

Next stop: Argentine Asado · on Asado and churrasco by plane, by Plane

Pronounced pee-KAHN-yah

Rump cap grilled with its fat cap on, seasoned only with coarse salt.

15 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Picanha is the rump cap of the beef, grilled with the fat cap left on and with coarse salt as the seasoning. It is a defining cut of Brazilian churrasco, strongly associated with the south and now cooked across the country. It is not carne de sol, the salted beef of the Northeast. Some restaurants serve it pinker than the temperature on this page. The guidance here is 145°F (63°C) and a rest.

Cultural background

The fat cap is not trimmed off. It bastes the meat. A long spice paste is not what makes the cut picanha. Steaks or a C-shaped skewer are both used. This page uses a skewer and allows a hot grill.

Why it tastes this way

The fat cap melts over the lean cap of the rump, so each slice tastes richer than a lean steak from the same animal. Coarse salt stays on the surface and crackles. Southern Brazilian churrasco is a wood or charcoal fire, and that dry heat browns the fat instead of steaming it.

How it is made

Salt the fat-on rump cap and grill it on a skewer, fat toward the heat, to a whole-beef temperature.

Authentic touch

Score the fat cap in a crosshatch, without cutting into the meat, so the salt has somewhere to sit and the fat can render.

How it is used

Slice it against the grain and eat it with farofa, a vinaigrette relish, and rice. Those sides have their own pages.

Field notes

  • The cut is the rump cap, and the fat cap stays on.
  • Coarse salt is the seasoning. A long spice paste is not what makes it picanha.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 pounds beef picanha, rump cap, fat cap left on
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt
  • Long metal skewers, or a hot grill if you name it as the home version
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Steps

  1. Score the fat cap and salt the meat. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Fold the piece onto skewers with the fat outward, or lay steaks fat side down on a hot grill and say that is the home version. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested meat across the grain. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Thick steaks on a hot grill, fat cap down first, are a home version of the skewer. Say if you are not using a skewer.
  • Carne de sol is a different Brazilian beef and not a stand-in for this cut.

Pairings

  • farofa
  • molho a campanha
  • rice
