Finnish Loimulohi

Next stop: Icelandic Hot-smoked Lamb · continuing the voyage

Pronounced LOY-moo-LO-hee

Salmon with a salted crust, wood smoke, and a moist, flaky center.

20 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 servings

History

Loimulohi is salmon fixed to a wooden plank and stood beside an open fire. The name points at the flame. It is a Finnish way of cooking a side of fish at lakeside and holiday fires, including in the north. The plank shields one side while the fire colors the other. This is an outdoor method, not an oven fillet.

Cultural background

Finnish hosts cook this when there is a fire and a board. It is not gravlax, which is cured and not cooked, and it is not a soup.

Why it tastes this way

Salmon fat bastes the flesh as the plank heats. Standing the fish beside the fire, rather than over it, keeps the smoke gentle and the outside from drying before the center cooks. Salt is the main seasoning. Birch or other northern hardwood is a common fuel and leaves a light wood taste.

How it is made

Salt a salmon side, fasten it to a plank, and cook it upright beside an open fire until it flakes.

Authentic touch

Nail or tie the fish firmly to a clean hardwood plank. Angle the board so the heat hits the salmon without the flames touching it.

How it is used

In pieces off the plank, with new potatoes and dill.

Field notes

  • Loimulohi is salmon fixed to a plank and cooked beside an open fire.
  • The Finnish word loimu refers to a flame, which is the heat source for the plank.

Ingredients

  • 1 side of salmon, about 2 lb, skin on
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • A clean hardwood plank
  • Dill for serving

Steps

  1. Mix the salt and sugar and rub them over the flesh. Fasten the side, skin against the board, with clean nails or soaked wooden skewers.
  2. Build an open fire. Stand or angle the plank beside the flames, not in them, with the fish facing the heat.
  3. Cook until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the flesh is opaque and flakes. Move the board closer or farther to control the heat.
  4. Take it off the plank and serve. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A shorter piece of fillet if a whole side is too large for the fire.
  • Served without dairy, with oil and dill only.

Pairings

  • New potatoes
  • Dill
  • Cucumber
