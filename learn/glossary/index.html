Glossary

Asado
In Argentina, Uruguay, and neighboring countries, a grill cook of meat, often a long social meal. The asador tends the fire.
Barbacoa
A Spanish word, from a Taíno word for a wooden framework, used in Mexico for pit-cooked meat. It is also the root of the English word barbecue. It is not one recipe.
Bark
The dark, seasoned crust on smoked meat. It is not burnt beyond eating unless the fire was too hot.
Braai
Southern African word, from Afrikaans, for a wood or charcoal cookout. The food and the gathering are both the braai.
Burnt ends
Cubes from the fatty point of a smoked brisket, tender and often glazed. A Kansas City specialty.
Char siu
Cantonese fork-roasted pork, glossy with a sweet-savory glaze. The name describes the roasting method.
Churrasco
Grilled meat in Brazil and in parts of the Spanish-speaking world. In Brazil it often means the rodizio or the backyard skewer cook, especially picanha.
Earth oven
A pit or heap of heated stones used to steam and roast food. Names include imu, hāngi, lovo, umu, pib, and pachamanca.
Galbi
Korean grilled short ribs, often marinated. Pork and beef versions are different cuts.
Hāngi
A Māori earth oven. A backyard imitation is not a marae hāngi.
Jerk
Jamaican seasoning and cook, built on allspice, thyme, and hot chile, historically tied to Maroon cooking and pimento wood.
Kalua
Hawaiian pit-roasted pork from an imu. A covered-pot home version is a different cook.
Low and slow
Indirect cooking at roughly 225–275°F (107–135°C) for hours, used for brisket, pork shoulder, and ribs.
Mop
A thin basting liquid, often vinegar, used during a long cook. It is not the same as a thick table sauce.
Parrilla
The grill itself in Argentina and Uruguay, and the restaurant that cooks on one.
Pib
A Yucatecan and broader Maya pit oven. Cochinita pibil is pork cooked in that tradition.
Picanha
The Brazilian rump cap, grilled on a skewer with the fat cap intact.
Rub
A dry mix of salt, pepper, sugar, or spices pressed onto meat before cooking.
Sajji
Whole lamb or chicken from Balochistan, salted and cooked slowly beside coals.
Stall
The temperature plateau during a long smoke, caused by evaporative cooling.
Suya
Hausa beef skewers from northern Nigeria, coated in a spicy peanut seasoning called yaji.
Tandoor
A clay or metal oven fired very hot, used in Punjab and across South Asia and into Central Asia and the Caucasus for bread and meat.
Tare
A glaze for Japanese grilling, especially yakitori and eel, usually soy, mirin, sugar, and sometimes sake.
Tri-tip
A triangular beef roast from the bottom sirloin, the center of Santa Maria barbecue.
Yakitori
Japanese grilled chicken skewers, salted or glazed, cooked over charcoal.
Yaji
The peanut, chile, and spice coat on Nigerian suya.
